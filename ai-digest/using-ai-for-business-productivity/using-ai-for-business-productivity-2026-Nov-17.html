1. Copilot's accounting skills in Excel reach general availability, and Frontier enrolment is no longer required

This updates the 2 November item on Microsoft's October Copilot features. Message Center notice MC1536053 says the accounting skills in Excel and Cowork became generally available on 13 November 2026. From that date, anyone with a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence can use them without joining the Frontier early-access programme. The skills cover reconciliations, journal entries, variance analysis, reporting, budgeting and forecasting, and audit, tax and compliance work. Copilot only drafts the work; people review, approve and post it, and nothing is written to an ERP without a person signing off. An optional audit trail records what Copilot changed and why. It is live in Excel, while Microsoft lists Cowork support as planned. Microsoft says that during limited Insider testing, more than 8,000 people used the accounting skills over 44,000 times. For finance teams this matters because month-end close is repetitive, rules-heavy spreadsheet work. The rollout lands as Ignite 2026 opens on 17 November, where previews expect Microsoft to focus on Copilot pricing and agent governance.

Sources: Message Center MC1536053 (merill.net), CPA Practice Advisor, CFO Dive, M365 Admin (HANDS ON tek), The Motley Fool (Ignite preview)

2. Databricks turns Genie into an always-on coworker that keeps working after the chat ends

On 11 November Databricks released a new version of Genie that it calls an autonomous coworker rather than a chat assistant. Staff can give Genie a goal, and it keeps working in the background on its own compute. It can watch connected systems, respond to schedules or changes in the data, act through approved tools, and hand pieces of the work to subagents without a new prompt. Unity Gateway lets admins set permissions, budgets and spending caps for these background jobs, and it logs every action. Genie Ontology, the layer that tells Genie what a company's data means, is now generally available. Databricks names Zillow, Unilever, adidas, Atlassian and AstraZeneca as production users. It says that in a benchmark it built with Turing, Genie succeeded on 91% of tasks while using fewer than half the tokens of frontier-agent baselines, but those are vendor numbers. Genie One is free until 31 January 2027. Analysts quoted by TechTarget say Databricks also needs to control what agents do in production, not just which data they can see. That is the main question for any company planning to let agents run unattended.

Sources: Databricks blog, TechTarget, SD Times, AlphaSignal

3. Harvard–Jellyfish study: coding agents add code, but code review caps how much more gets shipped

A Harvard working paper by Fiona Chen and James Stratton uses engineering telemetry from Jellyfish covering more than 700 companies. It finds that adopting AI coding agents brings more code but not proportionally more finished work. Early coverage reported about 30% more lines of code, 20% more commits and 23% more pull requests after firms adopted agents. A revision dated 7 November, covering roughly 720,000 engineers at 655 companies, reportedly gives larger figures: lines of code up 44% and pull requests up about 36%. Resolved Jira issues rose only about 14%, a borderline result, and engineering headcount did not change in any measurable way. The bottleneck is code review. The time between opening and merging a pull request rose about 49%, the share of pull requests sent back for changes nearly doubled, and AI produced only a small share of review comments. The authors' advice for business leaders is to invest in review capacity alongside agents; otherwise the productivity gain stays in the volume metrics. Sources disagree on some exact figures, so check the paper before quoting a number.

Sources: Jellyfish, BERI, Let's Data Science, FourWeekMBA

4. ElevenLabs launches ElevenAI, a workplace app for meeting notes and dictation

At its New York Summit on 11 November, ElevenLabs released ElevenAI, a Mac app in free early access. ElevenAI records in-person and online meetings, labels each speaker, and afterwards writes a summary, action items and a draft follow-up. It also offers press-to-talk dictation in more than 90 languages that works in any text field and removes filler words. Both features run on ElevenLabs' Scribe v3 transcription model. Notes can be passed to other AI tools through MCP. During the beta, Google Meet is the main supported meeting app. ElevenLabs says it asks everyone's consent before taking notes, does not store audio, and keeps transcripts on the user's Mac. Its own guide warns that the built-in notices may not satisfy recording laws everywhere, so companies should check before rolling it out. At the same event ElevenLabs announced models fine-tuned on customer data, and a partner programme with more than 550 consultancies and integrators plus an alliance with McKinsey. Taken together, the voice-AI company is now selling a meeting and note-taking productivity app, which puts it in competition with existing meeting-notes tools and the assistants built into office suites.

Sources: The Next Web, ElevenLabs — NYC Summit, ElevenLabs — ElevenAI, ElevenAI usage guide

5. Salesforce's Agentforce Coworker passes 185,000 users and adds a $50-per-user option

Salesforce said on 4–5 November that use of Agentforce Coworker, its AI assistant for employees, grew 21-fold in three months to more than 185,000 users. Named customers include The Adecco Group, Fulton Bank and BACA Systems. Salesforce also added a seat priced at $50 per user per month, aimed at buyers who want a predictable per-person bill as usage grows. The seat includes Coworker's searches, its security and governance controls, and 30 MB of indexing per user. Tasks that Coworker hands to other agents are billed on those agents' own terms, so heavy use can still cost more than $50. Coworker can also be paid for through Flex Credits, and Salesforce did not say how many of the 185,000 users are on each model or give revenue figures. Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 connectors let Coworker pull email, calendar and files into tasks such as meeting briefs. Microsoft Teams integration, desktop and iOS apps and an MCP server are scheduled for general availability in November. This item is about two weeks old; it is included because pricing per seat versus pricing per action is now a key decision for buyers of workplace agents.

Sources: Salesforce News, Salesforce (APAC press release), FourWeekMBA, CTOL Digital
