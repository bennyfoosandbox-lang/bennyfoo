1. 44% of CEOs can't show AI returns — and 63% say the fix is redesigning the business

Gartner's 2027 CEO Survey (203 CEOs and senior executives, polled April–May 2026) finds 44% struggle to demonstrate financial returns from AI, even though 75% still plan to raise technology spending. The headline remedy isn't more tools: 63% name radically redesigning the operating model as the top move to unlock returns, 52% point to deploying AI agents as employees, and 47% to freeing capital through restructuring. Gartner's advice to CIOs is to measure outcomes such as unit economics and decision velocity rather than AI activity. For anyone running AI pilots, it's a clear signal that bolt-on productivity gains are no longer being accepted as proof of value.

Sources: TechEdge AI (Gartner release)

2. Manchester study: businesses are buying AI faster than it pays off

A report from The Productivity Institute at the University of Manchester, commissioned by Bytes Technology Group and published 29 October, finds AI makes individual tasks dramatically quicker but the gains shrink at each step up from task to job, team, organisation and economy. Cited studies show customer-support staff resolving 14% more queries an hour and professionals finishing writing and analysis work 40% faster. The authors judge that closing the UK's "AI ambition gap" could plausibly add 0.5 to 0.7 percentage points a year to productivity growth. Their conclusion is that value depends on redesigning whole workflows, not adding AI to individual activities.

Sources: The Productivity Institute, Business Wire via Yahoo Finance

3. Fragment launches with $56M to automate the exceptions enterprise software leaves behind

Fragment emerged from stealth on 29 October with $56 million in total funding, including a Series A led by 8VC and a seed co-led by General Catalyst and A*. Its Autonomous Context Engine investigates and resolves purchasing and payment exceptions such as invoice discrepancies and order changes across a customer's existing systems, without a data migration. Graybar and CNX are named customers. Notably, Fragment prices on work completed by its agents rather than per seat, a model that ties vendor revenue to the operating burden removed.

Sources: AiThority (GlobeNewswire)

4. China's AI-office race: huge usage, no proven business model

A 36Kr analysis published 29 October says AI office productivity has become a must-do for China's tech giants: Tencent's WorkBuddy logged about 20.97 million monthly PC visits three months after launch, Alibaba folded three agent products into "Qianwen Office", and ByteDance's Doubao Work drew more than 100,000 enterprise beta applications in a week. The author argues commercialisation looks weak because the person who benefits from faster work is the employee while the payer is the boss, so efficiency alone is hard to charge for. The proposed way out is pricing on new value for the enterprise, such as extra output or orders, rather than seats. It is an opinion piece, but the usage figures are a useful read on how crowded the desktop-agent market has become.

Sources: 36Kr, Yicai Global, Caixin Global

5. Celigo and SentinelOne ship controls for the agents already inside the business

Celigo launched MCP Management on 29 October, a gateway and registry that ties every Model Context Protocol call to a real user through the company's identity provider and lets administrators approve access one tool at a time, with an audit trail. It also supports composite tools so a finance agent can pull CRM, ERP and billing data in one call without full access to each system. The same day SentinelOne introduced Singularity Agentic Detection and Response to discover AI agents, govern their access and stop unintended actions. Together they show governance of assistants and agents becoming a product category of its own as adoption widens beyond specialist teams.

Sources: IT Brief Asia, IT Voice
