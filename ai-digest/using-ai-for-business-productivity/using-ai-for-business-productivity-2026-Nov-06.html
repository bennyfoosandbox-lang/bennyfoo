1. Miro survey: AI lifts individual output, but organizations aren't seeing it

Miro published research drawn from more than 9,500 leaders and workers showing a split between personal and organizational results. Most respondents say AI has made them individually more productive, while far fewer say the quality of what their teams produce has improved. The takeaway for operators is that tool adoption alone isn't translating into better company-level outcomes. Teams chasing productivity should measure output quality and shared workflows, not just usage.

Sources: BNN Bloomberg (press release)

2. AI users are working up to 90 minutes longer each day

A new report based on activity data from more than 9,000 employees found that AI users' workdays ran up to 90 minutes longer than non-users', with more application switching and shorter focus sessions. Gizmodo's coverage pairs it with an eight-month Berkeley study at a 200-person tech company that found AI made work more intense rather than freeing up time. Together they challenge the assumption that AI hours saved convert into slack. The report's own framing is that the goal can't simply be AI usage.

Sources: TechRadar, Gizmodo

3. TCS study: 84% of executives rank productivity as AI's top benefit, but only 10% let agents act unsupervised

Tata Consultancy Services released an enterprise AI study in which 84% of executives named productivity improvement the leading source of value from their AI investments. Yet only 10% allow AI agents to make decisions without human approval across multiple functions. The gap shows that most companies still treat agents as supervised assistants rather than autonomous workers, which caps how much productivity they can capture. Expect governance and approval design to remain the main bottleneck.

Sources: TCS newsroom, Irish Tech News, Technuter

4. McKinsey: 80% say AI boosts their productivity, yet only about 6% are true high performers

McKinsey's latest AI survey, covered on November 4, finds that about 37% of respondents attribute at least some EBIT impact to AI, roughly unchanged from last year. The share of high performers, those crediting AI with at least 5% of EBIT and calling its impact significant, stayed flat at about 6%. At the individual level the picture is far brighter: 80% say AI improved their productivity and half say it helps them decide better. The flat enterprise-level numbers reinforce the message that personal gains aren't yet compounding into profit.

Sources: diginomica

5. Atlassian shares drop 17% as slower subscription growth overshadows an earnings beat

Atlassian beat earnings expectations on November 5 but its stock fell about 17% after it forecast slower subscription growth. The CEO said model power alone no longer sets companies apart, a notable admission from a vendor betting heavily on AI agents in its collaboration products. For buyers, the reaction signals investor doubt that AI features are yet driving seat or subscription growth for work-management software. It adds to a broader debate over whether AI helps or erodes incumbent SaaS economics.

Sources: SiliconANGLE
