1. Amazon opens its Seller Assistant to outside AI tools, starting with Claude

Amazon announced on September 23 the next version of Seller Assistant, adding persistent memory and always-on workflows, plus a new Selling Partner plugin that pipes a seller's listings, inventory, sales analytics and performance data into outside AI assistants — launching with Amazon Quick and in beta with Anthropic's Claude. Sellers approve actions before they execute, and every interaction is logged with a full audit trail, mirroring the human-in-the-loop pattern other platforms have adopted for agentic commerce. Amazon justified the move by noting that 90% of its selling partners already use third-party AI tools to run their businesses, so the plugin formalizes access rather than creating a new category of behavior. Every primary selling account also gets a free 12-month Amazon Quick Plus subscription (with two extra seats) through December 31, 2026, as Amazon tries to make its own assistant the default even while opening the door to competitors — the plugin is US-only for now, with international rollout to follow.

Sources: GeekWire, About Amazon, SiliconANGLE, Unix Commerce

2. Google, OpenAI and Anthropic court Sriram Krishnan to run their new AI safety body

The industry self-regulation push this digest first flagged on September 19 (when Cohere's CEO called it a "cartel") now has a name and a leadership shortlist: Google, OpenAI and Anthropic are reportedly forming the "Frontier AI Standards Agency" (tentatively also referred to as SAFA, a Standards Authority for Frontier AI), modeled on Wall Street's FINRA, and have approached former White House AI policy advisor Sriram Krishnan to be its chief executive. The three labs have also reportedly approached former Biden administration tech official Arati Prabhakar, ex-diplomat Condoleezza Rice and venture capitalist David Friedberg for roles, and the body is expected to focus on third-party pre-deployment security assessments, incident-reporting requirements and auditor qualification standards. It would launch without government oversight — notable given Krishnan himself said on leaving the Trump administration in June that "there will not be an FDA for AI." For enterprise buyers, a credible voluntary standards body would give procurement and compliance teams a reference point they currently lack, but its industry-run structure (and the lack of any binding authority) means it settles little of the same regulatory uncertainty flagged in this digest's coverage of Altman and Amodei's UN Security Council appearance two days earlier.

Sources: BankInfoSecurity, AI Weekly, Phemex News, BigGo Finance

3. Alibaba Cloud unveils AgentCore, betting enterprises want a managed agent stack, not raw models

At its Apsara Conference this week, Alibaba Cloud laid out a full-stack "agentic cloud" roadmap built around three layers: AI Native Cloud for large-scale training and inference, Agent Native Cloud — centered on the new AgentCore platform and an Agent Security Center — for deploying and governing enterprise agents, and Agent Context, a context-engine service that folds corporate documents, business systems and chat history into real-time memory for agents. Alibaba says Agent Context alone can cut token usage by as much as 67%, a concrete efficiency claim aimed squarely at enterprises worried about the run-time cost of agentic workflows at scale. AgentCore covers the full agent lifecycle — build, run, manage — putting Alibaba in the same managed-platform category Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform and NVIDIA's open agent stack have staked out this year, rather than competing purely on model price as it has with Qwen. The move signals Alibaba shifting its enterprise AI pitch from "cheaper models" to "the infrastructure that makes agents production-ready," a positioning fight now playing out across every major cloud vendor.

Sources: Pandaily, TechAfrica News, Futu News, Pan African Visions

4. Ema raises $77M as "AI employees" start eating into enterprise software budgets

Ema, which builds teams of AI agents to automate HR, IT and finance workflows, closed a $77 million Series B on September 23 led by Creaegis, with existing backers Accel, S32 and Prosus all increasing their stakes — bringing total funding to $140 million at more than four times its prior valuation. The company says it now has more than 50 active enterprise deals and over 1 million active users, with customers including NTT DATA, Hitachi, ADP, PwC, Google, KPMG, Wipro and Microsoft, and claims revenue has grown 50-fold over two years with bookings past $150 million. The round lands squarely in a trend this digest has tracked all year — "digital worker" and "AI employee" framing displacing traditional per-seat SaaS pricing — and the funding will go toward scaling go-to-market rather than core R&D, with Ema having already hired multiple senior leaders. For enterprise buyers, Ema's traction numbers (if they hold up under scrutiny) are a data point that agent-based automation is starting to win budget away from conventional workflow software, not just pilot budgets.

Sources: TechCrunch, GlobeNewswire, PYMNTS

5. Paychex launches WISE Hire, betting agentic AI can hire small-business staff 4x faster

Paychex introduced WISE Hire on September 23, an AI-native recruiting product that combines agents for candidate sourcing, workflow automation and matching with access to hiring coaches, aimed at the small and mid-size businesses that make up most of Paychex's HCM customer base. Paychex says the tool helps employers find and hire qualified talent 4x faster on average by automating multi-channel sourcing — pulling from channels like Google for Jobs and Meta's local jobs marketplace — alongside screening, coordination and scheduling. Employers retain final say over every hiring decision, with Paychex emphasizing human-in-the-loop oversight throughout, a governance framing that has become standard for HR AI launches this year after earlier bias and compliance controversies in automated hiring tools. WISE Hire ships integrated across Paychex's HCM platforms but is also available standalone, making it one of the more concrete signs yet that agentic recruiting tools are moving from enterprise pilots into mainstream SMB payroll-and-HR software.

Sources: GlobeNewswire, Paychex Newsroom, StockTitan
