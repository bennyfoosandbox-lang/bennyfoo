1. Honeywell launches Forge Cognition, putting AI agents and digital twins in charge of facility operations

On October 14 Honeywell introduced Forge Cognition, built with NVIDIA and Dell Technologies, as an agentic AI and simulation platform for running buildings, campuses and industrial sites. AI agents classify and verify building-management alarms, predict equipment failures and surface energy savings, while a digital twin lets operators model scenarios before acting. Customers choose manual, semi-autonomous or autonomous operation and can override the agents at any time. Early figures come from pilots and are not independently validated: up to 90% fewer needless alerts and up to 10% energy and utility savings at NVIDIA's Voyager campus. It extends the agent trend from office work into physical operations, where the productivity case rests on fewer false alarms and less manual triage.

Sources: Honeywell press release, IT Brief Asia, RTTNews, Seeking Alpha

2. EY CEO Outlook: half of CEOs credit AI for productivity, but few can see what it costs

EY's October CEO Outlook finds that half of UK CEOs name AI among their three biggest productivity contributors over the past year, ahead of redesigning business processes (40%). Yet only 14% of UK CEOs report full real-time visibility over AI costs, and about a third say they cannot properly assess AI's return. Coverage of the global survey of 1,200 CEOs in 21 countries puts the equivalent figure at 16% and adds that 23% say AI gains were absorbed by complexity, regulation, risk and extra work. The takeaway is that leaders feel the productivity benefit but lack the cost and ROI instrumentation to prove it, which is a different gap from the governance one EY flagged in September. Figures are survey self-reports, and the UK 14% figure is reported by TechRadar rather than in EY's own UK release.

Sources: TechRadar, CFO Dive, Newsweek

3. DXC licenses LangSmith to scale governed IT-operations agents across 125 customer environments

DXC announced on October 13 a partnership with LangChain to expand governed agentic AI in mission-critical IT operations. DXC's OASIS orchestration platform already runs on LangChain's LangGraph runtime and is scaling toward 125 customer environments in regulated industries; the deal adds a LangSmith license for building, evaluating and monitoring OASIS agents. Agents react to operational events, investigate issues in parallel and bring in people when judgment or approval is needed. DXC reports a 95% reduction in resolution time and 92%+ diagnostic accuracy across more than 500 measured use cases, though these are company-reported with limited methodology disclosed. The significance for productivity programs is the emphasis on evaluation and observability tooling as the price of running agents in production.

Sources: DXC newsroom, Runtime Wire, Finviz

4. Meta-commissioned survey: 84% of APAC small businesses use AI and most report a productivity lift

A YouGov survey of nearly 4,000 small business owners across nine Asia-Pacific markets, commissioned by Meta, finds 84% already use AI in their business and 58% use it at least a few times a week. Among users, 76% report a positive effect on productivity and 71% on revenue or sales, versus 6% reporting a negative productivity impact; 80% say AI helps them compete with larger firms. The headline title notes that most owners still want more guidance on using it. Because Meta sells the AI products being discussed (it recently pushed its Muse agent toward small businesses) and the underlying YouGov report was not located, treat the percentages as sponsor-framed and self-reported.

Sources: Meta Newsroom, Asia Times

5. Update: California’s No Robo Bosses Act is signed, tightening human oversight of AI workplace decisions

This updates an earlier item that listed California's SB 947 as awaiting the governor: Gibson Dunn's inaugural Artificial Intelligence in Employment Newsletter (October 5) reports Governor Newsom signed the No Robo Bosses Act on September 30. The law restricts employers from relying solely on automated systems to discipline or fire workers and requires human oversight of those decisions. The newsletter groups it with automated-decision-tool rules already enacted in Colorado, Connecticut and New York City, which share notice and non-discrimination duties but differ in scope, obligations and enforcement. For employers rolling out AI to manage or evaluate staff, the practical point is that human-in-the-loop review is moving from best practice to legal requirement in major states. The newsletter is a law-firm summary, not the statute text, and this item is about ten days old.

Sources: Gibson Dunn newsletter, Gibson Dunn (PDF)
