1. Cloudflare's network crosses a milestone: more of its traffic is now AI agents than humans

Cloudflare's Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 6, showed revenue of $696.1 million, up 36% year-over-year and ahead of estimates, sending shares up as much as 17% and prompting the company to raise full-year revenue guidance to $2.864–$2.870 billion. The quarter's most striking disclosure wasn't a dollar figure: for the first time, more than half of the traffic crossing Cloudflare's global network was not generated by a human, as AI crawlers, autonomous agents and bot traffic now interact with the web at a scale that outpaces people. Customer growth accelerated alongside it, with more than 80,000 new paying customers added in the quarter (74% year-over-year growth) and large accounts spending $100,000+ annually up 27% to 4,698. For a company that sits in front of a meaningful share of the internet's traffic, the non-human-majority milestone is a concrete, infrastructure-level signal that agentic AI has moved from pilot projects into background infrastructure that runs continuously. It's also a reminder that "AI productivity" increasingly shows up not in headline chatbot-usage stats, but in the sheer volume of automated agent traffic now quietly flowing through enterprise networks around the clock.

Sources: Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, Quartz

2. SAP: AI agent sprawl has become a board-level risk, not just an IT problem

A SAP News Center analysis published this month argues that "AI agent sprawl" — agents created, connected and deployed faster than enterprises can inventory, secure or retire them — has crossed from an IT nuisance into a board-level governance concern, citing Gartner's forecast that the average Fortune 500 enterprise will be running more than 150,000 AI agents by 2028. The piece leans on a companion SAP LeanIX survey finding 98% of companies have already deployed AI agents or plan to, yet fewer than half have a working inventory of the agents already running inside their own systems — a governance gap reminiscent of the "shadow IT" problem of a decade ago, but now involving software that can take action, not just store data. SAP frames its own AI Agent Hub, opened to customers this year, as a direct response: a central registry meant to give enterprises ownership, permissions and lifecycle control over every agent before sprawl becomes unmanageable. The framing — risk ownership, regulatory exposure, auditability, accountability for autonomous decisions — echoes what rival vendors have shipped in recent weeks, from AWS's open-source Loom governance platform to Snowflake's Cortex AI Gateway, suggesting the enterprise-software industry now treats agent governance as this cycle's must-have feature rather than a nice-to-have. For CIOs, it's another sign that scaling AI agents responsibly is becoming as much an organizational-design problem as a technical one.

Sources: SAP News Center, Simply Wall St, Yahoo Finance

3. Genpact's bet on "Agentic Operations" pays off: $1B+ in AI contract value, five times last year's pace

Genpact's Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 6, showed revenue of $1.343 billion, up 7.1% year-over-year, with its Advanced Technology Solutions segment — which bundles data, AI and agentic offerings — surging 24.1% and now representing 27% of total revenue; management raised full-year segment growth guidance to at least 25%, up from 20%. The quarter's headline number was contractual rather than purely financial: Genpact says it is on track to book more than $1 billion in "agentic" total contract value in 2026, roughly five times what it booked in 2025, as the decades-old business-process-outsourcing firm repositions around AI agents that combine automation with its process expertise rather than pure headcount arbitrage. Adjusted earnings per share grew 13.6% to $1.00, beating estimates, though shares still fell more than 4% in after-hours trading — a sign investors remain cautious about the pace and margin profile of the shift even as agentic bookings accelerate. Set alongside similar results this week from EPAM and other IT-services peers, Genpact's numbers reinforce a pattern across the BPO sector: incumbents that once sold labor are now selling agentic contracts, and the market is still working out how to value that transition. It's a useful counterweight to surveys earlier this summer suggesting enterprise AI ROI is elusive — in outsourced operations specifically, agentic contract value is growing fast enough to reshape a company's entire growth guidance.

Sources: Genpact Newsroom, Investing.com, GuruFocus

4. Airbnb says AI is cutting its support costs — and its CEO wants to spend a lot more on it

Airbnb's Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 6, showed revenue up 17% to $3.61 billion, beating expectations and prompting the company to raise full-year guidance to at least mid-teens growth; shares jumped roughly 15% on the results. Airbnb disclosed a concrete productivity number rather than just an adoption stat: customer-support cost per booking fell about 16% year-over-year, driven by an AI assistant that now resolves nearly 45% of support issues without any human agent involved. CEO Brian Chesky told analysts AI is "the best thing to ever happen to Airbnb," pointing to a second, less-obvious productivity effect — the number of product features Airbnb shipped in the first half of 2026 rose almost 80% versus a year earlier, which he credited to AI-assisted engineering as much as to the support-cost savings. Chesky said the company plans to spend meaningfully more on AI going forward rather than treat this quarter's gains as a ceiling, framing AI investment as compounding rather than a one-time efficiency unlock. The results echo Expedia's own AI push this quarter — including its acquisition of AI trip-planning startup Layla and native integrations inside ChatGPT and Claude — suggesting measurable, bottom-line AI payoff, not just usage growth, is starting to show up consistently across the travel sector's Q2 earnings.

Sources: CNBC, CX Dive, BigGo Finance

5. EPAM's AI-native revenue keeps compounding as it rebuilds delivery around "agentic harnesses"

EPAM Systems' Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 6, showed revenue of $1.415 billion, up 4.5% year-over-year, with AI-native revenue exceeding $160 million — now more than 11% of total business — and posting its sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit sequential growth; management reaffirmed a full-year AI-native revenue target of roughly $600 million. Rather than selling AI as an add-on, EPAM describes taking clients "beyond traditional SDLC into fully integrated agentic enterprise harnesses," using proprietary tools including AI/Run, DIAL and MF Lens that combine token-cost engineering, real-time data modernization and cyber resilience, delivered by engineers trained specifically on agentic workflows. The company was also named Databricks' 2026 Consulting and Systems Integrator AI Partner of the Year, external validation for a strategy built on operationalizing AI for enterprise clients rather than just building demos. Investors were unconvinced in the short term — shares fell roughly 22% on the report despite the beat, reflecting concern about slowing North American growth even as AI-native work accelerates. Set against Genpact's results the same day, EPAM's numbers reinforce a broader theme in this earnings season: traditional IT-services and consulting firms are re-basing their growth stories almost entirely around agentic AI delivery, even where the market isn't yet ready to reward the transition.

Sources: The Motley Fool, Yahoo Finance UK, StockStory
