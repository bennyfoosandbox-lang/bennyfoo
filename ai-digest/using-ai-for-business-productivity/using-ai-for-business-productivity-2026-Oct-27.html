1. Oracle's Fusion Companion lets employees run ERP and HR work from ChatGPT

At Oracle AI World on 26 October, Oracle announced Fusion Companion and Fusion Agentic Home, a layer that takes a business objective and picks the transactions, approvals, workflows and AI agents needed to finish it. Staff can reach it from supported AI tools such as ChatGPT or from Fusion Agentic Home inside the apps, so they no longer need to know which screen or agent to use. Oracle says execution stays inside the user's existing permissions and approval controls, with exceptions escalated to a human. Coverage notes that 25 agentic applications are now available across Fusion and that Oracle published no pricing, so the productivity case is still a promise rather than a measured result.

Sources: Oracle, Forkast, Small Business Trends, IT Brief Asia

2. Oracle's Deep Trust architecture enforces AI agent permissions inside the database

Also on 26 October, Oracle introduced Deep Trust AI Architecture, a blueprint that pushes security and business rules down into the database instead of trusting each agent or AI-built app to get them right. Deep Data Security checks the user's identity on every SQL request, SQL Assertions block invalid actions such as charges over an employee's spending limit, and Deep Trust Data APIs expose only the business objects a task needs. Deep NL2SQL adds business terminology and a correction-verification loop to improve natural-language answers, and APEX generates only application definitions, not executable code. The pitch is that agents can be scaled without scaling risk, a direct answer to the governance gaps that keep many pilots from reaching production.

Sources: Oracle, Oracle Blogs, IT Brief Asia

3. SAP says 45,000 cloud customers now use its AI, with Novartis targeting 35% productivity uplift

SAP's Q3 2026 AI release highlights, published 26 October, say 45,000 cloud customers now use its AI solutions and describe dozens of new specialised agents grouped under Joule Assistants. Joule Work, the new AI front end, lets people state a goal and then routes it to the right assistant, which orchestrates agents while respecting authorisations and keeping audit trails. PwC is using the Billing Assistant to spot issues earlier, and Novartis is piloting a Sourcing Assistant, with its head of procurement expecting 25% faster cycle times and a 35% uplift in FTE productivity. Those Novartis figures are an expectation from a pilot, not a measured outcome.

Sources: SAP News

4. Singapore launches 'Champions of AI' for 24 firms and agentic AI workshops for SMEs

At SWITCH 2026 on 27 October, Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong announced three AI initiatives led largely by Enterprise Singapore. The flagship Champions of AI programme starts with 24 companies, including DBS, OCBC, UOB, Singapore Airlines and PSA International, and offers tailored transformation roadmaps, expert access, workforce redesign and upskilling. Agentic AI workshops for SMEs follow a pilot that supported more than 280 small businesses in marketing, sales and customer service, with more sessions from next month. The agency says many firms experiment with AI but lack the in-house skills to integrate it, and will look at last-mile deployment support. The third initiative, MARVEL-AI, is a S$150 million materials-discovery effort.

Sources: The Straits Times

5. Twilio argues agents fail without a conversation layer, and ships four pieces to build it

At its SIGNAL event in Sydney, Twilio said the next phase of enterprise AI depends on communications, data and orchestration plumbing rather than smarter models alone, and named the resulting 'conversation gap'. It announced Conversation Orchestrator, which keeps one conversation going across channels and participants, Conversation Memory for carrying customer context between sessions, humans and agents, Conversation Intelligence for real-time signals and sentiment, and an open-source Agent Connect toolkit for plugging in any model or framework. Atlassian's customer-service product lead said that while AI makes it easy to vibe-code a website, a reliable, compliant global communications network cannot be vibe-coded. Twilio's stance is bring-your-own model, data and stack, positioning itself as infrastructure rather than a model vendor.

Sources: eCommerceNews Australia
