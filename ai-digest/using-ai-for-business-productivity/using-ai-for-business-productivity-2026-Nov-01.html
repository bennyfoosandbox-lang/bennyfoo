1. Amazon's Q3: AWS growth hits 39% as Bedrock and agent tools become the enterprise on-ramp

Amazon reported third-quarter results on 29 October, with AWS revenue up 39.3% to about $46 billion, its fastest growth in nearly five years, and a backlog of $578 billion. For business AI, the details matter more than the headline: Fortune 500 spending on Bedrock is up nearly 7x year over year, token volume is up nearly 17x, and tens of thousands of customers use AgentCore, deploying twice as many agents in Q3 as in the previous three quarters combined. Amazon Quick, its AI work companion, now extends into Outlook, Word, PowerPoint and Excel, with Mondelez, 3M, Southwest Airlines, EchoStar and DXC named as customers. The cost side is just as notable: about $77 billion of quarterly capex, roughly $220 billion for the year, and a trailing free-cash-flow outflow, so the productivity bet is being funded by very large infrastructure spend. Jassy also argued Bedrock could grow as large as EC2 because enterprises want several models without lock-in.

Sources: Amazon (Jassy Q3 letter), CRN, Data Studios, Amazon Q3 earnings release

2. GitHub makes Copilot's cloud agent generally available, and starts billing it like CI

At GitHub Universe on 28 October, GitHub made its connected Copilot cloud experience and the cloud sandboxes behind it generally available on paid plans. Sessions can start from github.com, mobile, the CLI, VS Code, Slack or Teams and be picked up from any other surface, because the agent keeps working in a GitHub-managed sandbox. The commercial change is the one for budget owners: sandbox usage is billed from November at published per-second compute and memory rates, drawn from a single dollar-based pool that is shared with Actions runners. Organization owners must switch on the cloud-sandbox policy, and Copilot AI-credit budgets do not cap sandbox compute, so teams need a separate budget. Agent work is becoming a metered line item next to CI.

Sources: Data Studios

3. Collective raises $100 million on an AI back office that cut staffing needs per client

Collective, which handles company formation, bookkeeping, payroll and tax filings for solo business owners, announced $100 million on 29 October: a $40 million Series B led by General Catalyst, QED Investors and Gradient Ventures, plus $60 million from a General Catalyst customer-acquisition fund. CEO Hooman Radfar says AI plus people-in-the-loop review has raised monthly bookkeeping closes per bookkeeper from the roughly 40 typical with accounting software to 300 this summer, heading toward 600, so growth no longer requires proportional hiring. The company has served more than 16,000 businesses and plans to expand into banking and insurance. The pitch is that one person managing agents can run a much larger business without venture capital, though Radfar concedes system-of-record software vendors keep real advantages. The closes figure is the company's own claim.

Sources: PYMNTS

4. Customers Bancorp says AI closes some commercial loans in seven days, not 30 to 60

Customers Bancorp said on 22 October, with its Q3 results, that it is scaling a multi-agent underwriting process that closed select commercial loans in seven days against a typical 30 to 60. It reports an estimated 76,000 cumulative hours saved by employees, up nearly 65% from Q2, more than $15 million in annualized capacity from positions not backfilled, and a doubling of deployed AI agents in the quarter. The bank pairs engineers, including OpenAI staff, with the bankers who own each workflow, builds on its own data, and measures results before an agent enters daily operations. The hours figure comes from an employee survey and the bank's own estimates, and a PYMNTS Intelligence survey cited alongside says only 5% to 10% of large firms report a full return on AI. This is about nine days old.

Sources: PYMNTS, Customers Bancorp Q3 press release (SEC)

5. PwC puts Amazon Quick agents to work on IT investment reviews and sales admin

On 21 October Amazon described two PwC deployments of Amazon Quick, its AI assistant, research and automation product. At a US financial services firm, PwC built a tool that reviews thousands of documents and survey responses to help the CIO prioritise technology investment, which Amazon says shrank the assessment from weeks or months to days. At a large technology company, sellers spent over 60% of their day on administration across at least 16 systems, and PwC is using Quick to cut that load, though no savings were quantified. The cases show the pattern vendors are selling: agents wired into company data with rules-based steps and human review. The evidence is a vendor press release with unnamed clients. This is about 11 days old.

Sources: Amazon press release, PYMNTS
