1. Menlo Ventures sizes enterprise AI at $177 billion as agents overtake humans

Menlo Ventures' 2026 State of Enterprise AI report, published 3 November, puts global enterprise AI spending at about $177 billion, up 5.4x in a year, based on a survey of nearly 400 US enterprise decision-makers overseeing more than $4 billion of AI budgets. Its headline claim is that agents now drive most usage: on OpenRouter they generate roughly 80% of all tokens, and agent consumption has grown 37x since overtaking human traffic in February versus 5x for human-driven use. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google together take 69% of deployment spend (up from 50%), while 55% of incremental AI money came from new budgets and only 22% from existing software lines, which suggests AI is expanding rather than replacing the software stack. For anyone planning AI productivity spend, the signal is that the money is following agentic workloads and concentrating in a few frontier labs.

Sources: The National Law Review (Menlo press release), Menlo Ventures

2. Survey: managing AI output takes as much effort as training a new hire

A Founder Reports survey of 2,052 US workers who use AI weekly found that 60% of those who have trained an employee say training, checking and fixing AI's work takes at least as much effort as onboarding a person, and one in three say it takes more. The burden is not limited to novices: 56% of multiple-times-daily users agree, and 40.7% of managers and above spend at least three hours a week overseeing AI versus 25% of individual contributors. Overall, 31.6% spend three or more hours weekly on this "botsitting", and 8.4% spend more than ten. The takeaway for productivity planning is that headline time savings can be offset by hidden review and rework, and that managers carry a disproportionate share of that cost.

Sources: Silicon Valley (Stacker, Founder Reports), WFTV

3. Larridin's CEO says AI is splitting the workforce: a 10% surge and a 25% drag

Speaking in a BigGo Finance podcast, Larridin co-founder and CEO Russ Fradin argued that AI is producing a sharply uneven productivity split inside large companies. In his telling, roughly 10% of employees gain dramatically, becoming 50x or even 100x more productive, while about 25% of workers at the bottom actually become less productive when they use AI. These are a vendor executive's claims rather than peer-reviewed data, and Larridin sells AI-adoption measurement, so they should be read as a hypothesis. They do echo the wider finding that individual gains do not automatically translate into firm-level returns, and strengthen the case for measuring who benefits before scaling licences. This is a new angle on Larridin, separate from its funding round covered earlier.

Sources: BigGo Finance (podcast summary)

4. Bespin Global launches an agent that operates ERP and groupware directly

South Korea's Bespin Global has launched HelpNow Computer, an execution-capable enterprise AI agent that turns natural-language requests into step-by-step plans and then carries them out in internal ERP, groupware, email, CRM and work portals through MCP and APIs. It packs nine execution tools covering document creation in PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Hangul, data analysis via OCR, vision-language models and NL2SQL, and application and submission work. The move shifts enterprise AI from answering questions to doing the work, a trend visible across vendors this autumn. Bespin has said productivity gains will be quantified through customer-specific proofs of concept, so whether it publishes hard metrics will be the test of buyer traction.

Sources: BigGo Finance

5. Mastech claims 67% productivity gain from running itself AI-first

IT services firm Mastech says its Autonomous Enterprise initiative, in which it treats itself as "customer zero", has delivered 67% productivity gains from AI-first operations by rethinking internal processes around AI, automation and agentic workflows. Analytics India Magazine's piece argues that building an AI-first company takes more than deploying agents: it requires changing how people work, how processes run and how the business creates value. The figure is company-reported and the measurement method was not detailed, so treat it as a marketing claim. It is still a useful counterpoint to the oversight-burden survey above, pointing to process redesign as the lever that separates large gains from small ones.

Sources: Analytics India Magazine
