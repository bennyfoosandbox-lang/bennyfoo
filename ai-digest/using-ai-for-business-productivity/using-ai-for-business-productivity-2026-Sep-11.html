1. OpenAI aims ChatGPT squarely at Wall Street's junior bankers with a financial-services build

OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services, a version of ChatGPT Work powered by GPT-6 Astra and developed with Morgan Stanley and Evercore as design partners, aimed at the research, modeling and client-materials work that occupies investment-banking and equity-research teams. The product bundles licensed data feeds from Daloopa, PitchBook, LSEG News and Crunchbase directly into the model's context, indexing and hosting the data itself so it can attach granular citations that let bankers trace a number or claim back to its original source. It can generate PowerPoint decks styled to a bank's own template and pull and verify figures against earnings-call transcripts and original filings, tasks that currently consume large amounts of junior-analyst time. It's a sharper, vertical-specific bet than OpenAI's general ChatGPT Work push, and a concrete example of a frontier lab building directly for one white-collar function rather than leaving that integration work to systems integrators.

Sources: OpenAI, CNBC, Fortune, Unite.AI

2. Microsoft ships an AI tool built to pull Salesforce customers onto Dynamics 365

Microsoft introduced Dynamics 365 Activate, an AI-powered migration tool in public preview that profiles a company's existing Salesforce (or other CRM/ERP) data, entities and customizations, then produces a blueprint of what should move as-is, what should change, and what should be redesigned before a switch to Dynamics 365. Microsoft says early use of the tool has already processed more than six billion data records, with Comcast and pet-food company Ziwi Pets among the organizations testing it ahead of a migration. The company plans to extend the same AI-assisted approach to additional CRM and ERP migration scenarios later this year, explicitly using AI to lower the switching costs that have historically locked enterprises into an incumbent platform. It's a pointed example of AI productivity tooling being aimed not just at internal workflows but at competitive displacement — automating the tedious data-mapping work that has made large enterprise-software migrations slow and risky.

Sources: The Register, CIO, Techzine, IT Daily

3. OpenAI opens the Codex harness to every developer via a new Agents API

OpenAI moved its Agents API into public beta, exposing the same session management, orchestration, context compaction and error-recovery machinery that powers Codex behind a single API call, so developers only need to supply tools and pick where the agent's compute runs. Built-in capabilities include context compaction, tool search, programmatic tool calling and subagents, with agents able to operate in a sandbox to execute code, edit files, connect to MCP servers and produce artifacts; sandbox partners include Cloudflare, DigitalOcean, Modal, Oracle and Vercel, and there's no additional fee beyond token and tool usage. By packaging what used to be a bespoke, hard-to-build orchestration layer into a managed service, OpenAI is lowering the engineering cost of standing up business-facing agentic tools — the same infrastructure gap that has slowed many companies' move from AI pilots to production agents according to recent enterprise-readiness surveys.

Sources: OpenAI, MarkTechPost, AlphaSignal, Blockchain.News

4. Adobe's Q3 shows AI assistant usage doubling even as investors want more

Adobe reported record fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $6.76 billion, up 13% year over year, with non-GAAP EPS of $6.13 and ending ARR of $27.5 billion, and raised its full-year targets on the back of the results. The more telling productivity signal sat inside the numbers: combined Acrobat and Express monthly active users topped 900 million, up more than 25% year over year, while Acrobat's AI Assistant saw its own monthly active users double quarter over quarter — a sign document-centric AI features are moving from novelty to habitual daily use inside businesses. Firefly's ending ARR across its apps and credit packs grew 40% quarter over quarter, reinforcing Adobe's pitch that "agentic" software, not just generative image tools, is now the growth engine. Even so, the stock fell on the report, with investors reportedly wanting faster proof that this usage growth converts into durable revenue acceleration — the same skepticism that has hit other software vendors' AI-tied earnings this quarter.

Sources: StockTitan, Investing News, Investing.com, Grafa

5. Visa, Mastercard and Ant International agree on how card networks will recognize AI shoppers

Ant International, Mastercard and Visa announced a collaboration on a Know-Your-Agent (KYA) interoperability framework that connects their three previously separate agent-identity protocols — Ant's Agentic Mobile Protocol, Mastercard's Verifiable Intent and Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol — through infrastructure from BuildFin.ai. The framework is meant to let card networks, digital wallets, agent platforms and marketplaces share certification requirements and cross-network traceability so an AI agent doesn't need separate identity verification with every payment network it transacts on, cutting integration cost and time-to-market for agentic payment services. The companies frame the stakes in scale terms: they project AI agents could orchestrate $3 trillion to $5 trillion of global consumer commerce by 2030, up from agents today mostly just making recommendations rather than completing purchases. It's an early, concrete step toward the "trusted agent" plumbing that has to exist before businesses can safely let AI agents buy on their behalf at any real volume — a governance and interoperability problem sitting alongside the access-control and oversight gaps enterprises are still working through internally.

Sources: PYMNTS, Electronic Payments International, Forkast, Investing News
