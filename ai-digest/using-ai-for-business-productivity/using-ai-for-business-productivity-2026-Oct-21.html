1. Notion rebuilds its agents to run 24 hours on one task, at a fraction of the cost

Notion's sixth-generation agent harness lets a single agent task run for more than 24 hours, up from five minutes, using durable execution on a queue-backed worker loop so work survives restarts. An upgraded "Auto" router sends each task to the cheapest model that still does the job, and Notion says it matched Opus 5.5-class results at about 90% lower average cost across 20+ use cases; Custom Agent runs cost roughly 30% less in its testing. The release also adds a ChatGPT-subscription connection, an Enterprise bring-your-own-key alpha, an AI Command Center with admin spending limits and agent governance, and a two-tier credit system (1,500 Standard and 500 Select credits per seat per month) that moves existing customers over on 2 November. For teams already running work in Notion, it is a concrete example of the shift from AI as a feature to agents as metered, governed infrastructure. Cost figures are Notion's own.

Sources: Notion blog, AI Agent Store, AI Weekly

2. Anthropic's own workforce data: Claude does six hours of work per hour, but "bot-sitting" is the new job

Anthropic's 16 October report on how employment is changing inside the company, summarized by Inc., describes Claude producing about six hours of output for every hour of employee work. The share of junior staff in its technical departments fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and junior technical hires dropped from 27% to 9%. Employees with at least a year of tenure were 50% more likely to move departments each month in 2026 than in 2025, and whole teams have been reallocated after handing work to agents. Inc.'s takeaway is that automation does not remove the work of oversight; it turns people into supervisors of agents, a cost most productivity business cases leave out.

Sources: Inc., Anthropic Research

3. Five months on, Google's "coming soon" enterprise agent Gemini Spark still has no business release date

At Google I/O in May, Google Cloud told enterprise customers that Gemini Spark, a 24/7 agent for Gemini Enterprise, was "rolling out to customers soon", yet Datafloq's review of Google's release notes found no Spark entry as of 16 October. What did ship first was the control layer: Agent Gateway and Agent Registry reached general availability on 18 June, Model Armor guardrails followed on 24 June, and context-aware access controls arrived in September. Other I/O promises also slipped into narrow rollouts, such as Gmail Live reaching paid Workspace plans on 15 October for US English mobile users only, and Gemini 4 Argon is off by default in the enterprise app until its toggle flips on 27 October. For buyers, the lesson is to contract around governance and model-retirement terms rather than announced roadmap dates.

Sources: Datafloq

4. Smarsh and FTI: 55% of enterprises are deploying AI, only 26% say governance keeps pace

New research from Smarsh and FTI Consulting, as summarized in a 20 October Bonfy analysis, finds 55% of surveyed enterprises actively deploying AI while just 26% believe their governance frameworks are fully aligned with that pace. Only 30% report comprehensive ability to detect and manage unsanctioned "shadow AI", meaning roughly seven in ten cannot see much of what employees are doing with AI tools. FTI's Jim Condon frames readiness as a data-governance problem rather than a technology one, and the write-up argues that blocking apps tends to push use out of sight rather than stop it. The figures matter for productivity programs because the gains employees report are being generated in a part of the business that compliance teams often cannot audit. Note that the source is a security vendor's blog relaying the study, so check the original survey for methodology.

Sources: Bonfy blog

5. eDiscovery vendor Reveal makes employees' Claude conversations collectable as legal evidence

Reveal added Claude to the AI sources its Onna platform can collect and search, using Anthropic's Compliance API for Claude Enterprise customers, so legal and IT teams can pull conversations, files and shared artifacts into review with one chain of custody. Claude joins ChatGPT and Gemini as supported sources, and Reveal says preservation for AI sources is coming to its Reveal Hold product. The company cites its 2026 Collaboration Data Risk and Readiness Report, in which the average legal team found new data sources partway through collection in 39% of matters, forcing restarts. As prompts and agent output become business records, every AI tool rolled out for productivity also adds retention and discovery obligations; collection is in early access.

Sources: Reveal press release
