1. Conference Board: only 11% of firms have written AI into their workforce plans, and just 5% expect it to add jobs

The Conference Board surveyed 775 leaders around the world and found that only 11% have built AI's expected effects into their enterprise-wide workforce and financial plans. Another 35% have done so for some functions, business units or regions. A third are still discussing it, and 13% haven't started assessing what AI means for headcount. The direction leaders expect is clear even where the plans aren't: 47% think they will have fewer full-time staff in two years, and just 5% expect more. For anyone running AI programmes, this is the gap behind many stalled ROI stories. Time saved only becomes a business result when hiring, budgets and role design change to match, and most companies haven't done that work yet.

Sources: The Conference Board, HRD (HC Magazine), Canadian HR Reporter

2. SnapLogic: more than half of enterprises have abandoned agentic AI projects because the business value was unclear

SnapLogic's 2026 Agentic Enterprise Readiness Index, fielded by 3GEM over the summer and published on 17 November, finds that 54% of enterprises have dropped agentic AI projects because the business value wasn't clear. Another 48% say they struggle, or expect to struggle, to measure returns. Spending is also hard to control: 89% report some agent overspend, and only 42% say they can accurately forecast their AI and token costs. Companies scored themselves 88 out of 100 on governance and trust readiness but only 48 on turning agents into value, the lowest of the five areas measured. SnapLogic sells integration software, so its argument that weak data foundations cause the failures is self-serving. Even so, the numbers match a theme this digest keeps hitting: agents are easy to start and hard to prove.

Sources: GlobeNewswire (SnapLogic), TechRadar, CDO Magazine

3. Slingshot: AI has become the first instinct for half of employees, and 68% of managers now require its use

The first part of Slingshot's 2026 Digital Work Trends Report surveyed 300 US employees and 200 managers through Dynata. It finds that AI use has become a habit: 51% of employees and 66% of managers rely on it without much thought, and 52% of employees reach for it before trying anything else or after only a quick try on their own. AI is also becoming a requirement rather than a suggestion. 68% of managers now say their staff must use AI, up from 59% in 2025, and the share who only strongly encourage it fell from 24% to 14%. The two groups see dependence very differently: 51% of managers think their teams would struggle or slow down without AI, but only 26% of employees agree. The sample is small and the report is vendor-sponsored. Its practical point still holds: once AI is mandatory, leaders need to check that it builds judgement and doesn't replace it.

Sources: GlobeNewswire (Slingshot), VMblog, Unite.AI

4. LangChain opens LangSmith Tool Gateway, one control point for the credentials and tool calls of every agent

LangChain put LangSmith Tool Gateway into public beta on 19 November. It is a managed layer between AI agents and the tools and APIs they call. Instead of each agent holding its own secrets, admins set up shared credential profiles that background agents reuse, and the gateway checks identity and access on every call. Each call is traced with its tool, server, identity and outcome, but not the data passed. Enterprise plans also get an audit log of tool calls and of changes to servers, toolkits, profiles and policies. The beta is on Plus and Enterprise plans and is capped at 100 requests per second, with bursts up to 1,000. For teams moving agents from pilots into real business systems, this kind of central control point is what security and audit reviewers are increasingly asking for before they sign off.

Sources: LangChain blog, LangSmith docs, Unite.AI

5. Staffbase–Forrester: information gaps hurt the productivity of nearly half of frontline workers, who want AI answers to fix it

Most workplace AI research looks at desk workers, so this study is useful for looking at the frontline instead. In a Forrester Opportunity Snapshot commissioned by Staffbase, 48% of frontline workers and managers in retail, healthcare, manufacturing, and transport and logistics said information gaps cut their productivity or operational efficiency. More than a third (35%) run into conflicting, outdated or unclear information a few times a week or more. When asked what a unified frontline experience should offer, 47% put AI-powered answers and guidance in their top three. The lesson for operations leaders is that frontline AI depends on its source material: an assistant built on scattered or stale procedures will repeat the same confusion faster. Staffbase sells employee-communication software, so read the framing with that in mind.

Sources: GlobeNewswire (Staffbase), Chain Store Age
