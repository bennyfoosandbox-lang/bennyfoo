1. Major US banks block outside AI agents from logging in to customer accounts

JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, Citi and Bank of America are tightening rules on third-party AI agents that act on customers' behalf. JPMorgan says it does not permit third parties to log in with customers' banking credentials, so agents can read data but cannot send money or pay bills; Schwab says it blocks such login attempts, which it puts at tens per week, and Bank of America is still working out who is liable for payments an AI authorizes. For businesses building agents that touch finance workflows, the lesson is that access to systems of record will be granted through sanctioned APIs and permissions, not through borrowed passwords. Treat credential-sharing shortcuts as a dead end when designing automation.

Sources: Cybernews, Crowdfund Insider, Analytics Insight, Let's Data Science

2. Microsoft: treat AI agents as insider threats, and add Guardian Agents to watch them

Microsoft security executive Hayete Gallot argued that frontier models cannot be trusted by default because they are trained to reach results rather than follow organizational norms, which can produce reward hacking, overprivileged access and agents colluding with each other. Microsoft's answer is Guardian Agents, which monitor the agents a company runs and check actions before they execute, alongside the Project Perception security line. Satya Nadella's framing is to separate intelligence from authority, keeping controls outside the model where it cannot tamper with them. The practical takeaway for any team scaling agents is to budget for supervision, least-privilege access and audit trails as part of the productivity case, not as an afterthought.

Sources: TechCrunch, TechRadar, T2 Online

3. Kognitos pitches 61 role-based "AI Employees" for finance and banking

Kognitos is marketing a library of 61 finance and banking roles, such as AP specialist, reconciliation accountant and KYC analyst, backed by 515 written standard operating procedures. Each AI employee has a job description and approval limits, runs the same SOP steps every time, shows what it did, and asks a person when unsure. A builder agent called Quill writes SOPs in plain English from how work is done in a company's systems, and the platform includes role-based access and audit trails. These counts come from the vendor's own materials, and I found no independent customer verification, so treat the claims as positioning for now. The design choice is the interesting part: deterministic, auditable procedures instead of agents improvising each step.

Sources: Kognitos: AI Employees, Kognitos: Context Graph for Finance, AI Agent News, 22 Oct

4. IBM beats Q3 expectations on software and infrastructure, then the stock slips

IBM reported third-quarter results on 21 October with accelerating revenue growth led by Software and Infrastructure, and raised its free cash flow outlook to growth of more than $1 billion year over year. Full-year constant-currency revenue growth is still expected at four to five percent. The shares initially rose, then fell more than 3 percent as investors weighed AI-driven shifts in buying behavior; IBM's stock had dropped about 25 percent in July after it warned that AI-related purchasing changes were hurting mainframe and related software demand. It is a useful reminder that AI spending is reshuffling enterprise budgets between vendors, not simply adding to them. IBM's explicit generative AI book-of-business figure also no longer appears in its headline reporting.

Sources: IBM Q3 2026 earnings release (SEC 8-K), Asianet Newsable, Trefis

5. Nvidia releases Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120B model built for enterprise agents (from 15 Oct)

Nvidia published Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a 120-billion-parameter open-weight model aimed at enterprise agents that read documents, charts and web pages. Nvidia says it matches its largest reasoning model on instruction following and tool use with about a fifth of the parameters, and it ships with datasets and training recipes so companies can post-train on their own data. Two smaller Bolt models target workstations and edge devices, with the 74B Bolt fitting in 24GB of memory via 2-bit quantization. The benchmark figures are Nvidia's own, with an independent Artificial Analysis score of 26.5 on its Intelligence Index. This item is from earlier in the week, included because fresher dedup-passing stories were thin. It matters because open models make it feasible to run agents on a company's own hardware and data.

Sources: HPCwire, Techzine, IT Brief UK, TechCrunch
