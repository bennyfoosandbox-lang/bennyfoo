1. KPMG puts AI agents into US audits and runs a full FTSE 250 audit with more than 100 of them

KPMG rolled out its Clara intelligence (KCi) agent platform across US audit practice on 17 November, after deploying it to more than 125 US engagement teams and about 500 teams worldwide this year. It wants KCi in every private-company audit in 2027, with public-company audits phased in after that. A day later the Financial Times reported "Project Exponential", a UK pilot where more than 100 agents worked through an entire audit of an unnamed FTSE 250 company alongside a normal human audit. KPMG says the agents asked sharper questions than its auditors. A human still had to step in when one agent failed to match the regulations to the company's documents, and the firm plans up to 20 larger trial audits before offering it to clients. This is one of the clearest cases yet of agents taking over a whole regulated professional workflow, not just helping with parts of it.

Sources: Inside Public Accounting, Unite.AI, ITPro, International Accounting Bulletin, European Business Magazine

2. OpenAI launches ChatGPT for Accounting, with plugins for QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite and BILL

OpenAI released ChatGPT for Accounting on 18–19 November. It is built around an Accounting plugin for ChatGPT Work and Business plans. It drafts journal entries from invoices and receipts, suggests account categories, collects bank statements and payment records for reconciliations, and helps with month-end reports and audit prep. About eight finance apps connect at launch, including Intuit QuickBooks, Xero, BILL, FreshBooks, Oracle NetSuite, Rillet and Campfire; reports differ slightly on the exact list. OpenAI's guidance keeps a person in charge of approvals: users check the supporting records and calculations before anything is posted. Xero's expanded integration is US-only for now. This moves the business-AI race from general assistants into bookkeeping, a high-volume task where small firms feel the time savings straight away.

Sources: Dataconomy, ANI News, Business Upturn, TechBooky

3. Adecco's survey of 40,500 workers: AI saves the most time where it is built into the workflow, but training is falling

The seventh Global Workforce of the Future report from The Adecco Group came out on 19 November. It surveyed 40,500 workers in 36 countries in June and July. Workers whose AI is well integrated into their workflows report saving about 20% more time per day than those with patchier set-ups. They are also more likely to put that time into higher-value work, and seven times less likely to feel overwhelmed by AI. Training is going the wrong way: participation fell from 77% in 2025 to 67%. Among AI users, 93% of those trained say it made them more productive, against 47% of those who were not. 96% of workers involved in redesigning their own jobs say they are ready to adapt, against 29% of those left out. The figures are self-reported, but they back the idea that productivity comes from redesigning workflows rather than from handing out licences.

Sources: GlobeNewswire (Adecco Group), EuropaWire, Newswire, The Manila Times

4. Meta says agents now do more than 60% of the work of spreading improvements across its ads models

An engineering post Meta published on 18 November says that since March 2026, AI agents have handled more than 60% of "technique propagation": taking a modelling improvement that worked in one ads-ranking model and adapting, training and validating it across the rest of the fleet. Meta is making agent-driven propagation the default way this work gets done. Coverage of the post says each engineer can now handle about three times as many adaptations, and that Meta expects more than $45 million in compute savings by year-end on selected technique and model pairs. Those last figures come from secondary reports and Meta's own estimates, so treat them as directional. It is a rare inside look at agents taking over a skilled, repetitive engineering job at scale, with the gains measured in output per engineer rather than headcount.

Sources: Engineering at Meta, Superpower Daily, TechBytes

5. Replit launches Missions: a team of agents that works toward a business goal within a set budget

Replit released Missions on 17 November, so this one is from earlier this week. Instead of prompting one agent, a user sets an objective, a spending cap and approval rules, and a coordinated group of agents keeps working until an AI reviewer judges that the goal has been met. Replit's examples go beyond code: replacing a legacy system, or winning a set number of enterprise customers, with approval gates before agents contact prospects or make product commitments. In internal tests Replit says Missions built a monitoring app in two days on a $3,000 budget, and ran up to 19 times faster with 32 agents on benchmark tasks. These are vendor figures, and pricing per mission isn't published yet. Together with similar multi-agent launches from Cognition and Perplexity, it points to a new way of buying AI: paying for an outcome within a budget instead of per seat.

Sources: Technobezz, RuntimeWire, byteiota, FourWeekMBA
