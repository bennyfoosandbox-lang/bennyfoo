1. New research: layoffs done "in the name of AI" undercut the very productivity gains they're meant to deliver

A study by University of Pittsburgh business professor Mark Ma, published this week via The Conversation and picked up widely across outlets including Fast Company and Tech Xplore, argues that AI-driven layoffs are self-defeating: the job insecurity they create damages employee sentiment toward AI, which the research identifies as one of the strongest predictors of whether AI actually lifts productivity at a firm. Workers who fear their own jobs are next tend to under-adopt or quietly resist the very tools management is counting on to generate returns, offsetting the efficiency gains the layoffs were supposed to fund. The study also found that stock-market reactions to AI-linked layoff announcements were close to zero on average — investors aren't rewarding the cuts the way executives may expect. It lands as roughly 90% of executives say AI hasn't yet visibly boosted their company's productivity, and as thousands of companies continue citing AI as the rationale for headcount reductions — a pattern this digest has tracked most weeks since July.

Sources: The Conversation, Tech Xplore, Fast Company, Naked Capitalism, Digital Information World

2. Cisco's AI order book hits $9.3B for the year — up 4.5x — as record Q4 caps its strongest quarter yet

Cisco reported record fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of $17.3 billion, up 18% year-over-year, with non-GAAP EPS of $1.22 (up 23%), in results presented August 12. The headline number for enterprise AI watchers: Cisco booked $4 billion in hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders in the quarter alone, pushing the full fiscal-year hyperscaler AI order total to $9.3 billion — 4.5 times what it booked the prior year — while orders from neocloud, sovereign-cloud and enterprise customers topped $1 billion annually for the first time. Networking orders grew 40% for an eighth straight quarter of double-digit growth, and Splunk contributed sizable on-premise security deals, with firewall orders up more than 30% for a second consecutive quarter. Cisco's numbers are a useful upstream indicator for this topic: before a company can put agentic AI to work internally, it has to buy the networking and security capacity to run it, and Cisco's order book shows that build-out phase is still accelerating sharply.

Sources: TIKR, Investing.com, Futurum Group, Yahoo Finance

3. Applied Materials guides 30%+ chip-equipment growth as AI-driven fab demand keeps accelerating

Applied Materials posted record Q3 2026 revenue of $9.12 billion, up 24.8% year-over-year, with record non-GAAP EPS of $3.50 and record operating income of $3.10 billion (34.0% margin), in results announced August 13. Management guided Q4 revenue to $10.25 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and reiterated that its semiconductor-equipment business is on track for more than 30% growth in 2026 and over 50% growth in packaging revenue, driven by fabs racing to build capacity for cutting-edge AI chips. The results place Applied Materials alongside Cisco this week as a supply-side bellwether: two infrastructure companies with no direct AI product of their own are both posting record quarters purely because their customers are spending aggressively to build the compute and networking capacity enterprise AI adoption requires. Shares had pulled back roughly 27% from a recent peak heading into the print despite the strong guide, reflecting investor uncertainty about how durable AI-driven capex cycles will prove even as the underlying order numbers keep climbing.

Sources: Applied Materials IR, Investing.com, TradingKey, 24/7 Wall St.

4. A10 Networks ships an AI Gateway to put cost controls and governance over every model an enterprise uses

A10 Networks announced general availability of A10 AI Gateway on August 13 at Black Hat USA, positioning it as a single control plane that sits over every AI application, agent and large language model an organization runs, whether on-premises, in private cloud, or air-gapped. The product applies access policies synced to directory systems, tracks real-time per-request cost in dollars, enforces per-model and per-team token budgets with hard limits and soft-limit alerts, and routes simpler requests to cheaper models while sending complex tasks to more capable (and expensive) ones. The launch is part of a broader pattern this digest keeps surfacing: as enterprises move AI from pilots to production, cost and governance tooling — not the underlying models themselves — is becoming the next big product category, echoing similar plays from HubSpot's Agent Hub and Snowflake's Cortex AI Gateway covered in earlier runs. For finance and IT leaders, tools like this are a direct answer to the "token cost rationing" and agent-sprawl problems that have shown up repeatedly in this digest's knowledge base over the past month.

Sources: Help Net Security, AiThority, Converge Digest, StockTitan

5. PubMatic trims headcount with AI and automation while its agentic ad platform drives double-digit growth

PubMatic's Q2 2026 results, reported August 13, showed revenue up 11% year-over-year — a return to double-digit growth ahead of schedule — with adjusted EBITDA up 38% to $19.6 million (a 25% margin) and free cash flow up 47%. Management said AI and automation increased efficiency across engineering, marketing, customer success and finance during the quarter, allowing total headcount to decline year-over-year even as the company grew go-to-market headcount and investment by 12% in its highest-priority sales areas. At the same time, the company's "Agentic OS" advertising platform was credited as a key driver of the quarter's growth, with total operating-expense growth held to 4% — well below revenue growth — showing the efficiency gains flowing straight to margin. It's a small but concrete example of a pattern this digest has tracked across far larger companies (Datadog, Shopify, Uber): AI absorbing internal workflow overhead while a separate, AI-native product line does the actual revenue growing.

Sources: The Motley Fool, Investing.com, StockStory, AOL
