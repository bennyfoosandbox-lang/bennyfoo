1. Anthropic quietly overtakes OpenAI on revenue as the two labs' Q2s diverge sharply

Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate hit $65 billion by the end of July — more than sevenfold its pace a year ago — as the company confidentially filed for an IPO it could complete as early as this fall, working with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan. The company generated more than $11.5 billion in preliminary Q2 revenue, over 14 times what it made in the same quarter last year, and posted a small operating profit. OpenAI's Q2, by contrast, landed at $6.7 billion (up 18% from Q1's $5.7 billion) while its operating loss, including stock-based compensation, widened to $12.3 billion from $9.3 billion the prior quarter. For businesses betting budgets and integrations on either lab, the divergence — Anthropic scaling into profitability while OpenAI scales into deeper losses — is now a live variable in vendor risk, not just a headline number.

Sources: Bloomberg, Fortune, Axios, Investing.com (WSJ), KuCoin

2. Sierra doubles to $200M ARR in two quarters as agentic customer service scales up market

Sierra, the enterprise AI startup founded by former Salesforce co-CEO Bret Taylor and ex-Google executive Clay Bavor, reached $200 million in annualized recurring revenue in May — double the $100 million it hit just six months earlier — and now says it serves 40% of the Fortune 50. The company closed a $950 million round led by Tiger Global and GV at a $15.8 billion valuation on that same $200 million ARR figure. Sierra sells outcome-priced AI agents that handle customer service end-to-end rather than deflect to a human, and its growth curve — roughly $100M ARR added per quarter — puts it among the fastest-scaling enterprise software companies in memory, a concrete data point for how fast agentic AI is being bought, not just piloted, inside large businesses.

Sources: Crypto Briefing, TechCrunch, Sacra, TechBuzz

3. Google pays $10M for Spirit Airlines' internal data to train AI on how a real company actually runs

Alphabet is buying a de-identified slice of bankrupt Spirit Airlines' internal business data for $10 million — reportedly around 100 million emails and 500 million Teams/collaboration messages, plus spreadsheets, calendars and operational documents spanning marketing, scheduling and logistics. The appeal isn't customer data (excluded, along with any PII) but authentic examples of how a mid-sized company coordinates work across employees, pricing and operations — exactly the kind of messy, real-world business context that generic web text doesn't capture, and that Google says it wants to use to improve AI models and workplace productivity tools. A flight attendant union has already criticized the deal, and it's a preview of a coming category: bankrupt or distressed companies' internal comms as a distinct, monetizable AI training asset.

Sources: CNN Business, Pulse2, Forbes, Investing.com

4. Alteryx survey: 53% of enterprises still can't get business context into their AI systems

Alteryx's 2026 IT Leader Research, surveying 1,400 IT leaders globally, finds AI adoption itself is no longer the bottleneck — 80% expect spending to keep rising and 69% already report moderate-to-significant ROI — but 53% say their organization struggles to translate real business context into AI workflows, even though 77% agree that context is what makes AI output accurate and useful. The report traces this to an ownership gap: strategy (37%) and delivery (38%) still sit mostly with IT, while business teams are stuck defining requirements (30%) rather than building, and two-thirds say AI performs best when managed inside the line of business rather than centrally. It's a concrete, well-sourced counterpoint to ROI-boosterism: spend and usage are both up, but the "translate this to how we actually work" step is where most enterprise AI programs are still stalling.

Sources: BigDATAwire, Morningstar (PR Newswire), IT Pro, Trade Arabia

5. Snowflake's Cortex AI Gateway learns to shop around for cheaper models, cutting AI spend up to 3x

Snowflake added dynamic model routing to its Cortex AI Gateway, letting it automatically pick the model with the best quality-for-cost tradeoff for each task instead of routing everything to a single frontier model. Under what Snowflake calls an "advisor pattern," a smaller, cheaper model attempts the task first and only calls a larger model as a fallback tool if it can't finish — Snowflake's internal testing found agents building a dbt pipeline this way used up to three times fewer tokens while holding quality steady. The routing is built into Snowflake's own CoCo and CoWork AI products and is available to third-party agents through Cortex AI Gateway, alongside newly added access to DeepSeek-V4-Flash and GLM-5.3. It's a direct answer to the "AI token cost rationing" and FinOps-waste stories that have run through this digest all month: infrastructure vendors are now competing on cost-efficiency, not just capability, as enterprises get more disciplined about what they're actually paying for.

Sources: VentureBeat, SD Times, CIO Dive, IT Pro
