1. McKinsey and Infosys both find enterprise AI still isn't moving the earnings needle

A new McKinsey survey of 1,719 executives finds that despite record AI spending, only 6% of organizations qualify as "AI high performers" attributing at least 5% of EBIT to AI, while 63% report no measurable enterprise earnings impact at all. The gap is between individual and enterprise-level gains: horizontal tools like chatbots and copilots help individual workers, but those time savings stay at the worker's desk rather than flowing to the income statement — realizing value requires redesigning workflows around AI, not bolting a tool on top of them. A separate Infosys report, surveying more than 1,000 senior executives at large U.S. companies, arrives at nearly the same conclusion: 72% of respondents have scaled less than a quarter of their AI pilots, and two-thirds say they struggle to measure the ROI they're being asked to prove. More than a quarter of enterprises now spend over 10% of their total IT budget on AI, so the pressure to show returns is intensifying even as the returns themselves stay elusive — this digest has tracked this exact pattern for weeks, but two of the industry's largest advisory shops landing on nearly identical numbers within a day of each other shows how entrenched it has become.

Sources: IT Pro, The Register, Tech Times, CIO Dive

2. Salesforce's Agentforce revenue triples to a $1.5B run rate as Claudeforce debuts

Salesforce's fiscal Q2 2027 results, released August 26, show its Agentforce AI product line crossing a $1.5 billion annualized revenue run rate, up 240% year over year, with bookings growing at triple-digit rates as more customers move from pilots into production and consumption-based billing via "Flex Credits." Total revenue hit $11.35 billion (+11% YoY) and net income jumped 87% to $3.53 billion, boosted by a $2.6 billion gain on Salesforce's equity stake in Anthropic. The company also unveiled Claudeforce, a plugin that lets Anthropic's Claude compose sales emails, pull account context, and update CRM records directly through chat, deepening the Salesforce-Anthropic relationship beyond the investment tie. Shares jumped 14% in after-hours trading and Salesforce raised its full-year revenue guidance to $46.1-46.4 billion, a sign that Wall Street is starting to treat Agentforce's commercial traction, not just Salesforce's core CRM business, as the story that matters.

Sources: Salesforce, CNBC, MarTech Notes

3. CrowdStrike's record quarter shows securing AI adoption is now a business of its own

CrowdStrike's fiscal Q2 2027 earnings, also released August 26, beat expectations with revenue up 26% year over year to $1.47 billion and net-new annual recurring revenue up 51% to a record $332.8 million, driven substantially by enterprises buying security specifically to govern their AI rollouts. CEO George Kurtz called it a "Mythos moment" — referencing a recent high-profile AI-security incident — that has translated into "mass-market acceptance that AI adoption needs security," arguing "every enterprise will run on AI, and securing it is the largest market opportunity in our history." ARR from accounts on CrowdStrike's Falcon Flex platform passed $2.29 billion, up 101% year over year, and the company raised its full-year net-new-ARR growth guidance. The results are a useful counterpoint to the McKinsey/Infosys item above: even where AI's productivity payoff is unproven, the compliance and security spending it forces onto enterprises is proving very real and very measurable.

Sources: CNBC, Quartz, StockTitan, 24/7 Wall St.

4. Mistral and Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN strike a sovereign-AI deal worth hundreds of millions of euros

French AI lab Mistral and Saudi Arabia's state-backed HUMAIN announced a strategic collaboration on August 24 spanning compute infrastructure, frontier model development and go-to-market deployment across the Gulf region, in a deal reportedly worth hundreds of millions of euros. The partnership pairs Mistral's open-weights model expertise with HUMAIN's data-center buildout under Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, with an initial focus on cybersecurity and voice applications for regulated industries. It's explicitly framed around "sovereign AI" — letting enterprise and government customers keep data, compute and model ownership within their own borders rather than depending on U.S. hyperscalers — and includes plans to build frontier models with stronger Arabic-language performance for regional businesses. For companies weighing where to run AI workloads, it's a sign that data-sovereignty and compliance requirements, not just raw model capability, are becoming a first-order factor in enterprise AI infrastructure decisions.

Sources: Mistral AI, Unite.AI, TechAfrica News, PR Newswire

5. Nvidia's enterprise and industrial AI sales jump 138% as Q2 revenue nearly doubles to $96 billion

Nvidia's fiscal Q2 2027 results, reported August 26, showed revenue nearly doubling to $96.2 billion (+106% YoY), crushing analyst estimates of $92.2 billion, with data-center revenue alone reaching $89 billion. The standout line for business-productivity purposes: sales to Nvidia's "AI Clouds, industrial and enterprise" (ACIE) customer segment — distinct from pure hyperscalers — hit $40.3 billion, up 138% year over year, indicating a growing share of AI compute demand is now coming directly from enterprises building and running their own AI applications rather than renting capacity through cloud AI services. CEO Jensen Huang said demand is "accelerating," and Nvidia guided to $108 billion in sales for the current quarter, above the $104.2 billion Wall Street expected. The scale of enterprise-side demand growth is worth watching alongside today's ROI-gap headlines above: infrastructure spending on AI compute keeps climbing steeply even as most companies can't yet prove a financial return on it.

Sources: Fortune, CNBC, MarketBeat
