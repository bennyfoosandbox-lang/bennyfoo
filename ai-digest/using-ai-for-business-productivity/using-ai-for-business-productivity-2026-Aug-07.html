1. Shopify's AI agents triple traffic and orders, driving 34% revenue growth

Shopify's Q2 2026 earnings, reported this week, showed revenue up 34% year-over-year to $3.58 billion and gross merchandise volume up 32% to $116 billion, with the company crediting AI tools as a primary driver of the beat rather than a side feature. Its Sidekick AI assistant saw daily active merchants grow 3.6x year-over-year and daily sessions grow 4.8x, handling roughly 34 million conversations and helping merchants spin up about 36,000 custom apps in the quarter. Agentic AI traffic and orders to Shopify-powered storefronts each tripled year-over-year, and catalog-powered AI search converted at roughly twice the rate of search built on scraped data, pointing to a real commercial payoff from structured, agent-readable product data. The results moved several Wall Street analysts to raise price targets, treating Shopify's AI integration as a genuine growth driver rather than a narrative. For an SMB-heavy commerce platform, the numbers are an early, concrete data point that AI agents can move revenue for the businesses using them, not just cut internal costs.

Sources: Retail TouchPoints, The AI Insider, Yahoo Finance

2. Uber says AI coding tools have essentially doubled engineer output

In its Q2 2026 earnings call this week, Uber said engineer adoption of AI-based coding tools is now near 100%, and that code output per engineer has roughly doubled as a result, helping the company moderate headcount growth even as gross bookings jumped 22% year-over-year to more than $58 billion — a fourth straight quarter above 20% growth. Executives described the productivity gains as broad rather than singular, framing AI's benefit as "thousands of small hits and improvements" spread across the business rather than one breakthrough feature. Beyond engineering, Uber pointed to AI-driven tools like Cart Builder, which it says is increasing average order sizes by up to 2x in its delivery business, and the quarter included targeted, AI-linked headcount cuts of 10-20% in a couple of organizational units after effectiveness reviews. The results land in the same stretch in which Uber has drawn scrutiny for capping employee spend on AI coding tools like Claude Code after exhausting its 2026 budget months early, a reminder that productivity gains and cost control are now being managed as two sides of the same rollout. Together, the two data points make Uber one of the clearest large-company case studies yet of AI productivity gains showing up directly in reported financials.

Sources: GuruFocus, BigGo Finance, Yahoo Finance

3. HappyRobot raises $150M, joins the unicorn club building AI agents for supply-chain ops

HappyRobot, which builds AI agents that execute tasks inside enterprise operations rather than just chat, raised a $150 million Series C led by Prysm Capital and co-led by Eurazeo, valuing the company at $1.2 billion. The round brings HappyRobot's total funding to roughly $200 million and was joined by existing backers a16z, Base10 and Y Combinator alongside strategics including Koch Disruptive Technologies, Orange, Deutsche Telekom's T.Capital and — notably — Uber, one of its own enterprise customers. The company says it now works with more than 150 enterprise customers, including DHL, Kuehne + Nagel and Repsol, deploying agents that reason and act inside logistics, freight and supply-chain systems, and that it has grown 5x since its Series B roughly a year ago. With the new capital, HappyRobot plans to expand beyond supply chain into insurance, energy and utilities, telecom and airlines. The raise is one of the clearest signals yet that investors see durable enterprise demand for agents that plug directly into existing operational workflows, not just generate content.

Sources: Tech.eu, Business Wire, Fortune

4. Zenity raises $125M as AI agent security becomes its own category

Zenity, which monitors and governs AI agents as they gain access to enterprise systems and data, closed a $125 million Series C led by Norwest Venture Partners, with new investors Qumra Capital, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Hitachi Ventures and LG Technology Ventures joining existing backers Vertex Ventures, Third Point Ventures, DTCP and Intel Capital. The round brings Zenity's total funding to about $185 million and comes as the company says it has tripled revenue in each of the past two years, with a third straight tripling on track for 2026. Norwest framed the investment around the idea that "the agent is the new perimeter" — as autonomous AI agents get standing access to sensitive business systems, traditional network and identity security models no longer cover the risk they introduce. The raise lands the same week as a separate $30 million round for rival Hush Security (covered in this digest's August 5 edition), underscoring how quickly a distinct AI-agent-governance market is forming around the broader rush to deploy agents in production. For enterprises racing to adopt agentic AI for productivity gains, Zenity's growth is a signal that governance and security are increasingly treated as a prerequisite for scaling, not an afterthought.

Sources: Business Wire, AIwire (HPCwire), Norwest Venture Partners

5. Google's Gemini Enterprise push collides with an AI talent exodus

Even as Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai touts 90% of Fortune 100 companies now using Gemini Enterprise and Google Cloud posts 82% quarterly growth, reporting this week describes a deepening talent exodus among the researchers who built Google's AI stack. Departures cited include Gemini co-lead Noam Shazeer, who left for OpenAI, and Nobel Chemistry laureate John Jumper, who moved to Anthropic, alongside broader unease following Jeff Dean's shift out of day-to-day leadership and Demis Hassabis stepping back from daily DeepMind management. Researchers quoted in the coverage point to frustration that Google Cloud sells scarce TPU compute to rivals like Anthropic even as internal teams compete for the same capacity, plus multiple layers of internal approval that make shipping research into products slower than at leaner competitors. The tension matters for enterprise buyers because Gemini Enterprise's product velocity and reliability depend on the research bench Google is now struggling to retain, even as the commercial product wins large customers. It's a reminder that enterprise AI platform competition is being fought as much over researcher retention as over quarterly adoption numbers.

Sources: CNBC, eWeek, Breitbart
