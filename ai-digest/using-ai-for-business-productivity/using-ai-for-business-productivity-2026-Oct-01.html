1. ChatGPT Space and GPT-6.1 Sol: OpenAI's DevDay push into shared team workspaces and cheaper reasoning

Beyond the always-on Dots agents already covered, OpenAI's DevDay also introduced ChatGPT Space, a shared project environment where teammates, ChatGPT and agents work from common knowledge and standing instructions. It also launched GPT-6.1 Sol, positioned close to GPT-6 Astra in capability at roughly one-fifth of Astra's standard token prices, plus an Ultrafast tier for latency-sensitive work. For businesses, the pairing matters because it shifts AI from individual chat sessions toward team-level context while cutting the unit cost of running agents at scale.

Sources: Axios, The Neuron, Analytics India Magazine, BGR

2. PwC survey of ~50,000 workers: AI is splitting the workforce, and the best AI users are flight risks

PwC's 2026 Global Workforce Hopes and Fears Survey, covering nearly 50,000 workers in 48 countries and regions, finds AI use up 10 points while access to learning and development is down 8. It sorts workers into four groups: front-runners (14%), AI insurgents (18%), indispensables (11%) and an "engine room" (56%) that is not far along the AI learning curve. Notably, 29% of front-runners say they are very or extremely likely to change employer within a year. The takeaway for leaders is that uneven AI enablement is becoming a retention problem as well as a productivity gap.

Sources: PwC, PR Newswire, Manila Times

3. Korn Ferry: India leads on AI adoption, but productivity gains arrive with heavier workloads

Korn Ferry's Workforce 2026 Insights Report finds Indian employees well ahead of global peers: 76% feel adequately trained on AI versus 52% globally, and 79% say they are more productive than a year ago. The cost is visible, though: 78% report significantly higher workloads over two years (62% globally), 73% say AI has increased the number of tasks expected of them, and 72% say they now cover more than one role. With 70% saying AI fluency is already part of performance reviews, the study is a caution that productivity gains can simply be absorbed as expanded scope.

Sources: ANI, The Tribune, ThePrint

4. ServiceTitan: trades contractors move from "whether" to "how" on AI, but trust lags

ServiceTitan's second annual State of AI in the Trades report surveyed 1,017 US contractors (fielded May 26 to June 15, 2026). Active AI engagement rose from 46% in December 2025 to 52%, and 66% of AI users save at least three hours a week, with 47% reporting better profitability and 43% higher revenue. Yet only 11% place high or complete trust in their AI tools, while 44% cite not knowing how to use AI in their business as a barrier. It is a useful read on how AI productivity is landing outside the knowledge-work office.

Sources: Manila Times (GlobeNewswire), ServiceTitan, Contractor Magazine, ACHR News

5. Palma.ai raises $1.8M for a governance layer between AI agents and corporate systems

Palma.ai closed a $1.8M pre-seed round led by D11Z, with Plug and Play Ventures, Deel and Scale Now Ventures participating. Its platform sits between agents and corporate tools, letting administrators set policies and approved MCP tools across Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot, enforce them at runtime and log each transaction to a tamper-evident audit trail. The raise is small, but it reflects continued demand for permissioning and audit as employees get agents that can act inside business systems.

Sources: Unite.AI, FinTech Global, Dealroom, Ventureburn
