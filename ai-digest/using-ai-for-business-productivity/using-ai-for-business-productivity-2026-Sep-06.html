1. Cisco gives all 90,000 employees a personal AI agent — with real cost and governance controls

Cisco rolled out MyAgent, a personalized AI agent, to its entire roughly 90,000-person global workforce, built on Circuit, its internally governed, multi-model AI platform. Each employee's agent draws on more than 800 backend subagents and permissioned enterprise connectors across Outlook, Webex, Jira and SharePoint to run supervised autonomous workflows, while a policy server blocks destructive actions and stops company data from training third-party models. For cost control, Cisco routes 50-60% of requests to open-weight models and another 20-30% to plain software automation, reserving frontier-model calls for the minority of tasks that actually need them, and external actions still require explicit human sign-off. It's one of the largest enterprise-wide agent deployments disclosed to date, and the architecture — cheap models by default, frontier models by exception, hard governance guardrails throughout — reads as a template other large enterprises are likely to copy as they try to get real productivity out of agents without runaway inference bills or security incidents.

Sources: Cisco Blogs, PYMNTS, YourStory, Karmactive

2. CrowdStrike and OpenAI team up to police the coding agents companies are unleashing on their own codebases

CrowdStrike and OpenAI expanded their partnership on September 2, extending CrowdStrike's Falcon Guardian AI Detection and Response product to monitor OpenAI's Codex coding agents at runtime, and bringing OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Cyber model into the Falcon platform starting with its Frontier AI Readiness and Resilience service. Falcon Guardian is built to catch unauthorized or compromised agent behavior — an agent touching data or systems outside its assigned scope — before it spreads across endpoints, cloud environments, SaaS apps and browsers, addressing a blind spot most security teams currently have limited visibility into. The deal matters for productivity because coding agents are one of the fastest-growing agent categories inside enterprises (McKinsey puts the share of firms now building software in-house with agentic coding tools instead of buying it at roughly a third), and the time savings only stick if security teams trust those agents aren't quietly creating new attack surface. It also signals OpenAI positioning itself as a cybersecurity distribution partner rather than just a model vendor, embedding cyber-specific models directly into an established security platform's runtime defenses.

Sources: CrowdStrike Investor Relations, AIwire (HPCwire), StockTitan, Investing News

3. Adobe buys a one-year-old Indian startup to put AI agents inside marketing teams' actual workflows

Adobe acquired Rilo, a Peak XV-backed Indian AI automation startup founded in 2025, to bolster its agentic marketing capabilities. Rilo's platform lets marketing and go-to-market teams spin up AI agents from natural-language instructions to handle tasks like competitive intelligence, prospecting, LinkedIn outreach, sales-call analysis and campaign research — work that traditionally required stitching together several point tools by hand. Terms weren't disclosed, but the deal is Adobe's second acquisition of an Indian AI startup, and it comes as Adobe competes with Canva, Anthropic and OpenAI for a share of marketing-team budgets that are increasingly shifting toward agentic tools. For business productivity specifically, it's a concrete example of an established enterprise vendor buying rather than building agent capability for a function — marketing operations — where manual research and outreach grind has been an obvious automation target.

Sources: Business Standard, Yahoo Finance, Storyboard18, American Bazaar Online

4. Proofpoint puts OpenAI's cyber models to work inside the security operations center

Proofpoint introduced the Proofpoint SOC Analyst Agent on September 3, bringing OpenAI's specialized Daybreak models into its investigation workflows so security analysts can ask natural-language questions and get back structured, traceable findings and recommended next steps instead of digging through raw alerts and logs themselves. The agent is entering private preview with a growing group of beta customers, with general availability targeted for the end of Q3 2026, and Proofpoint has kept consequential containment decisions in human hands rather than letting the agent act autonomously. It's a specific, function-level example of a pattern showing up across enterprise software this quarter: rather than a general chatbot, vendors are wiring frontier models into the exact investigation steps that eat the most analyst time, turning hours of manual log correlation into a natural-language query. The move also extends OpenAI's push into enterprise security tooling beyond its own products, following its cyber-model partnership with CrowdStrike announced the same week.

Sources: Proofpoint, GlobeNewswire, Yahoo Finance Singapore, CIO Influence

5. South Korea's KT wins a contract to rebuild Woori Bank's chatbot around AI agents that remember the conversation

Telecom operator KT was selected in late August to rebuild Woori Bank's AI chatbot and consultation-bot infrastructure, applying its new "Agent Connector" solution to link the bank's chatbot, consultation bot, "AI Banker" service and AI agents into one system that carries customer context across channels. The goal is to let a customer who starts in the chatbot continue seamlessly with a human agent or a different channel without repeating themselves, while improving how accurately the system identifies customer intent and expanding AI-assisted consultation into outbound calls and branch interactions. It's a smaller, single-institution deal next to the platform-level announcements dominating this week's coverage, but it's a concrete illustration of where agentic AI is actually landing in a regulated, customer-facing function like retail banking — replacing disconnected chatbots with a context-aware layer rather than a flashier generative assistant. Woori Bank has separately been expanding AI agents into corporate finance and sales this year, making this chatbot rebuild the customer-service half of a broader bank-wide agent push.

Sources: Seoul Economic Daily, Digital Today, The Elec
