1. ServiceNow's AI numbers beat, but weak guidance sends the stock down 18%

ServiceNow reported third-quarter results on 28 October with subscription revenue of $3,988 million, up 21% year over year, and said it beat the top end of its guidance on every topline and profitability metric. The AI story was the headline: EmployeeWorks, its employee-facing agent front door, reached about $100 million in annual contract value in under three quarters, and press coverage reports the full-year target for AI net new ACV was lifted to $1.7 billion from $1 billion (one outlet's AI-generated summary conflicts on this, so treat the exact figure as reported rather than confirmed). The market focused elsewhere, though: a fourth-quarter revenue outlook of $4.25–4.26 billion trailed the roughly $4.36 billion analysts expected, and shares fell about 18%. For buyers, the takeaway is that agent governance (Control Tower) is turning into real revenue even as investors question how fast it converts.

2. Anthropic ships tools to show what enterprise Claude spend is actually buying

On 28 October Anthropic made three reporting features generally available for Claude Enterprise: smart reports, in-app surveys and pooled group budgets. Administrators can see team activity, results and cost over set periods, get flagged workflow bottlenecks with suggestions, and cap token spending per team through shared budget pools; Anthropic says report data is not used for model training by default. TechCrunch framed it as an answer to buyers' growing demand to prove return on AI spend. Secondary coverage notes the announcement gave no pricing, customer count or measured-savings example, and that Team-plan and API-only users appear not to get the same attribution and alerts. It is a sign that cost governance, not just capability, is now a selling point in enterprise AI.

Sources (2026-10-28 (5 days old)): TechCrunch, FourWeekMBA, Digital Today

3. Anthropic's IPO filing puts hard numbers on enterprise AI demand

Anthropic filed for an initial public offering on 26 October, and coverage of the filing gives an unusually clear read on who pays for enterprise AI. As of 30 September the company counted roughly 250 customers above $10 million in trailing-12-month revenue, more than 1,900 above $1 million and more than 11,900 above $100,000, while its five largest customers made up about 25% of revenue over the first nine months of 2026. Reports point to a listing as early as mid-November, and outlets differ on the filing mechanics (an earlier confidential draft dates to 1 June), so the details are worth checking against the S-1 itself. For anyone budgeting AI tools, it shows spend concentrating in large deployments, with meaningful dependence on a handful of buyers.

Sources (2026-10-26 (7 days old)): Built In, Verdict, Futurum, Advisor Perspectives

4. Meta's Q3: capex outruns cash while headcount falls 15% and business agents head to SMBs

Meta's third-quarter results, released 21 October, show what funding an AI buildout does to the cost base: revenue was $64.6 billion, up 26%, but quarterly capital spending hit $34.9 billion (versus $19.4 billion a year earlier), producing roughly negative $2.1 billion of free cash flow, the first sub-zero quarter in over a decade. Meta narrowed 2026 capex guidance to $135–145 billion, and one tracker reports year-end headcount of 66,775, down 15% from a year earlier, consistent with the 8,000-person cut announced in the spring (the AI link is that site's interpretation, not Meta's statement). On the product side, analysts describe Meta pitching model APIs and business agents to its base of hundreds of millions of small businesses, with subscription and usage-based revenue expected over several years. The numbers are mostly from secondary write-ups, so verify against the earnings release before relying on them.

Sources (2026-10-21 (12 days old)): Let's Data Science, FourWeekMBA (headcount), FourWeekMBA (cash flow), Crypto Briefing

5. Microsoft's October Copilot roundup: Excel cleanup tools, per-file rules and Office files inside Workspaces

Roundups of Microsoft's October 2026 Copilot updates describe a push from chat toward finished work products. Excel gains a clean-data tool that flags duplicates, inconsistent formatting and wrong data types, plus accounting skills; Frontier-preview items include Workbook Rules, which let you set Copilot preferences per file, and interactive HTML dashboards. Copilot Workspaces can reportedly now create and edit Word, Excel and PowerPoint files directly, and PowerPoint gets a Record a Skill preview. Microsoft itself says published timelines are tentative, several features arrive in November or later, and some (such as Clean Data and Formulas by Example) appear tied to the paid Copilot Premium tier, so availability will vary by licence. The details here come from third-party roundups rather than Microsoft's release notes.
