1. Larridin raises $30 million to measure what enterprise AI is actually delivering

Larridin closed a $30 million Series A on 2 November, led by Pelion Ventures with Andreessen Horowitz and Gradient Ventures returning, bringing total funding to $47 million. The platform ties AI adoption, spend and output data to the work of both people and agents, and the company says customers see a 47% average gain in measurable team output and a median $2.4 million of AI spend optimized in year one. Its data points at the uneven reality inside firms: at one customer a single engineer drove about 65% of a team's weekly AI spend, two tools doing comparable work differed 66x in cost per shipped change, and AI-assisted pull requests rose from 35% to 62% between April and September while waits for the first human code review grew 50%. These are vendor-reported figures, but the raise shows investors betting that the next bottleneck is proving AI's value, not buying it.

Sources (2 Nov 2026): Yahoo Finance (press release), Ventureburn

2. OpenAI and ServiceNow deepen their tie-up, putting ChatGPT inside IT workflows

On 28 October OpenAI and ServiceNow expanded their partnership so that workflow actions, such as finding similar incidents, assessing change risk and updating records, can be done from within ChatGPT, governed by ServiceNow's permissions and business rules. OpenAI models will also power ServiceNow's Build Agent and Autonomous Engineer, Codex is planned for its AI Workflow Factory, and the two are developing a joint security offering plus a voice experience in the Control Tower for logging AI use cases. ServiceNow says it will give all employees ChatGPT Work and Codex, while OpenAI uses ServiceNow workflows internally. The deal shows the system-of-record vendors choosing to meet employees inside the assistant they already use rather than compete with it. It is dated a few days before this run but was not previously covered.

Sources (28 Oct 2026 (6 days old)): OpenAI, UC Today

3. Tencent says its WorkBuddy agent has handled more than 9 billion tasks since March

Tencent used its Middle East cloud push on 2 November to update WorkBuddy, its AI productivity agent, which it says has processed over 9 billion task requests since launching in March and is used across more than 50 industries and by half of China's top 500 enterprises. The ecosystem now counts more than 50,000 developers and partners and over 200,000 skills for automating business processes. Tencent also previewed its Hy4 and cheaper Hy4 Flash models for long-running agent tasks and named new regional partners including Alsaif Gallery and TruKKer. The adoption figures are company-reported, but they put a scale on China's workplace-agent race that earlier coverage lacked.

Sources (2 Nov 2026): Arab News, ITP.net

4. Newsweek opinion: redesign the process before you automate it

In a 2 November Newsweek opinion piece, Brad DeMent argues that companies are restructuring headcount around projected AI gains before they have put the operating model and decision rights in place to earn them. He cites SSON Research & Analytics findings that fewer than one in five organizations have automated most of their processes, while 90% of those with a Global Process Owner report better process performance. His order-to-cash example shows sales, supply chain and finance each hitting targets while the customer still sees delays at the handoffs. In one case, a technology company's process owner used operational data to fix purchasing first, leaving roughly 70% of purchase orders needing no buyer intervention. It is an opinion piece rather than new research, but it frames a theme running through the week's measurement stories.

Sources (2 Nov 2026): Newsweek

5. Google Workspace's October drops add cell-level citations to Gemini in Sheets

Google's October Workspace roundup, published 30 October, is aimed at making AI output easier to check. Fill with Gemini in Sheets can attach exact web-source citations to each generated cell, handles up to 3,000 cells per request, and Google says it is up to 55 times faster than manual entry on a 500-cell task, based on an internal study of 95 participants. Gemini can also edit several slides or a whole Slides deck from one prompt, and the release adds native Markdown editing in Docs, live voice drafting and real-time speech translation in Meet. For finance and operations teams doing research-heavy spreadsheet work, per-cell sources make audit and review easier, though the speed claim is Google's own. This is from four days before the run and was not previously covered.

Sources (30 Oct 2026 (4 days old)): Google Workspace Blog, UC Today
