1. At Dreamforce, Salesforce unveils AIforce and widens its AWS, Google Cloud and Siemens agent alliances

At Dreamforce 2026 in San Francisco (Sept. 15–17), Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff — joined on stage by OpenAI's Sam Altman — used the keynote to unveil AIforce, a new "live interface layer" sitting above Agentforce, Data 360 and Customer 360 that lets any AI agent reason over Salesforce data and workflows, with the company reiterating a zero-data-retention pledge. Alongside it, Salesforce deepened its cross-cloud bets: with AWS, Salesforce business context now flows natively into Amazon Quick via MCP and AWS's own agents (a DevOps Agent live now, Security, FinOps and Partner Central agents due this fall) are landing inside Slack; with Google Cloud, Salesforce is running workloads on Google's infrastructure so Agentforce and Gemini Enterprise agents can act on shared data without custom integration; and with Siemens, Agentforce is being wired into Teamcenter to push agentic workflows into industrial sales and service. The combined push is Salesforce's clearest bet yet that enterprise buyers want one governed layer spanning multiple vendors' agents rather than a single-vendor stack, with the AWS pieces live now and the rest phasing in through early 2027.

Sources: Salesforce Newsroom, SiliconANGLE, Unite.AI, Salesforce Ben, Salesforce Newsroom

2. Google Mandiant's new AI risk report: a runaway agent racked up a $50,000 cloud bill in under an hour

Google's Mandiant published its 2026 AI Risk and Resilience report on Sept. 16, drawn from Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group incident-response work, and leads with a cautionary case study: a financial-services firm gave an agent read/write access to its billing database to reconcile ledger anomalies, and when a corrupted null value broke its formatting tool, the agent didn't fail safely — it entered an unconstrained recursive reasoning loop, fired more than 15,000 high-cost API calls in under an hour, ran up roughly $50,000 in charges, and locked the database badly enough to halt live business transactions. The report frames this as a governance failure rather than a security breach in the traditional sense: no attacker was involved, just an autonomous system with too much access and no circuit breaker. It lands in a year where separate surveys already put AI-agent security incidents at a majority of enterprises running agents (88% report a confirmed or suspected incident) while only 6% of security budgets are earmarked for agent security — reinforcing that cost and operational runaway, not just data leakage, is now a board-level AI risk.

Sources: Help Net Security, Google Cloud, Google Cloud Blog

3. AIUC raises $40M to sell insurable proof that an enterprise's AI agents are actually safe

AIUC (Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company) raised a $40 million Series A led by Ribbit Capital, with First Harmonic participating, taking total funding to $55 million, the San Francisco startup said Sept. 16. AIUC built AIUC-1, an evaluation standard that audits enterprise AI agents against jailbreaks, hallucinations, prompt injection, anomalous behavior and data leaks, then backs a passing certification with real insurance underwritten through Lloyd's of London; ElevenLabs holds the first AIUC-1-backed policy, covering up to $50 million against hallucination-driven loss, data leakage and faulty tool actions. The raise is a direct market answer to the governance gap Mandiant just quantified: AIUC is betting enterprises will pay to turn "we think our agents are safe" into an auditable, insurable claim before letting agents near production systems, and plans to use the new capital to extend audits and coverage to frontier models themselves.

Sources: SecurityWeek, Forkast, The SaaS News, Crypto Briefing

4. Manufacturing-AI platform CADDi raises $114M Series D at a $1.2B valuation

Chicago- and Tokyo-based CADDi entered definitive agreements for a $114 million Series D at a $1.2 billion valuation, the company said Sept. 15–16, taking total funding to $234 million with Salesforce Ventures joining new backers Moore Strategic Ventures, Coreline Ventures, HR Tech Fund and Woven Capital alongside existing investors Atomico and Globis Capital Partners. CADDi's platform ingests manufacturers' scattered drawings, specs and part data and structures it so procurement, sourcing and engineering teams can search and reuse it instead of re-quoting parts from scratch — the unglamorous back-office data cleanup that any later factory-floor AI agent work depends on. Fresh off launching a next-generation version of the platform in Japan in August, the company says the new funds go toward global expansion centered on North America, deeper AI/data technology, and talent — one of the larger recent bets that industrial back-office productivity, not just white-collar copilots, is where AI ROI is showing up first.

Sources: Fortune, Investing.com, 01net, The SaaS News

5. Nationwide survey: small businesses are adopting AI faster than they're managing its risks

A Nationwide survey of small and mid-market business owners, fielded by Edelman Intelligence and released Sept. 15, found six in 10 say employees already use public AI chatbots or writing tools for work, yet only 36% have a written AI-use policy and just 37% offer training on responsible use. The specifics get more pointed: only 27% have rules on what company or customer data can be typed into an AI tool, just 25% have any process for verifying AI-generated output before it's used in a business decision, 35% believe employees are already using unauthorized AI tools without their knowledge, and nearly a third of respondents say their business has already been targeted by a generative-AI scam in the past year. It's a smaller-business counterpart to the enterprise governance story playing out at Dreamforce and in Mandiant's report this same week — the tools are being adopted faster than the guardrails around them are being built, at every size of company.

Sources: PR Newswire, Complete AI Training, CPA Practice Advisor
