1. Anthropic moves into the office suite with Claude Sheets, Whiteboards, Meetings and Data

On 10 November Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta on paid plans, made Claude Data (formerly Claude Dashboards) generally available, and introduced Claude Meetings, which joins Google Meet calls to take notes and update the team's working documents as the conversation happens. Sheets lets Claude set up trackers and budgets that teams then keep editing together, and Whiteboards turns a planning session into a structured board that Claude can convert into a doc or slide deck. Coverage is split on Meetings: TechCrunch reports a beta for Team and Enterprise customers, while Anthropic's own page calls it coming soon. Admins have to approve before Claude can join calls with external guests, and on Enterprise plans Sheets and Whiteboards start switched off. The launch came at the start of Anthropic's IPO roadshow, and most coverage reads it as a direct challenge to Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace for everyday knowledge work.

Sources: TechCrunch, Claude (Anthropic), Yahoo Finance, iClarified, MLQ.ai, Crypto Briefing

2. IBM and Microsoft launch Imagine Enterprise, a platform of industry agents with engineers attached

IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise on 10 November, a joint platform for building, running and governing AI agents for specific industries. It starts with more than 6,000 agents and assets from IBM Consulting Advantage, running on Microsoft Foundry, Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot, Fabric, Microsoft 365, Entra, Purview and Defender. PYMNTS points out that the 6,000 figure counts other assets as well as agents. The bigger selling point is the people: engineers from both companies will work directly with qualifying customers to connect agents to existing systems and move them from pilot into production, with controls, observability and compliance tooling built in. The companies plan to add manufacturing, energy, the public sector and mobility later. No platform pricing was announced, and early analysis notes that connecting prebuilt agents to a company's own systems is still where most of the work is.

Sources: IBM Newsroom, PYMNTS, RuntimeWire, Crypto Briefing, IntelligentHQ

3. OpenAI gives businesses verified ChatGPT profiles and agents, and puts ChatGPT Enterprise on AWS Marketplace

OpenAI made two business-facing moves within about 24 hours. On 9 November ChatGPT Enterprise, along with ChatGPT Work, Codex and the healthcare and financial-services editions, went on sale in AWS Marketplace, so eligible companies can pay through AWS billing and count the spend toward existing EDP or PPA cloud commitments. That takes away a common procurement hurdle, though OpenAI notes that buying this way doesn't mean customer data stays on AWS. On 10 November it launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta: through a Business Hub, companies verify their identity through Persona, edit their profile and product feeds, and see how often ChatGPT mentions them and how many users click through. Qualifying US e-commerce and online-service firms can also run customer-facing Business Agents, which can serve as the landing point for ChatGPT Ads. Both are free at launch, and OpenAI says paying doesn't change how often a business is recommended.

Sources: OpenAI, AWS Marketplace, PPC Land, The Next Web, Search Engine Watch, WebProNews, Mi3

4. Payscale: AI is saving time at work, but pay hasn't caught up

Payscale published its full AI Workforce Impact Report on 10 November, following a September preview, and its main finding is that pay systems are behind. Of the employees surveyed, 56% think workers who build AI skills should earn more, but only 16% say upskilling has raised their pay and 70% report no change. The time savings are smaller than vendor claims suggest: 61% say AI saves them less than 10 hours a week and 15% say it saves no measurable time. On the employer side, 74% expect AI to affect headcount or hiring within 12 months. The September data showed 61% rewriting job descriptions and 49% saying their salary structures haven't kept pace. Payscale's chief compensation strategist Ruth Thomas argues that companies should treat workforce planning, productivity and pay as one continuous cycle. The survey covers organizations with 100 or more employees.

Sources: GlobeNewswire (Payscale), FinancialContent, Payscale (preview), HR Tech Edge

5. Bay raises $50M to check every AI agent action before it runs

Israeli startup Bay came out of stealth on 10 November with $50 million: a $37 million Series A led by Battery Ventures, with Microsoft's M12, Micron Ventures and Wiz CEO Assaf Rappaport taking part, plus a previously undisclosed $13 million seed led by YL Ventures and Striker. Its platform checks each action an agent tries to take against the task it was given and its earlier behavior, then allows it, blocks it, or sends it to a human for approval, all in real time before the action runs. Bay says it has dozens of enterprise customers, including Micron, Peloton, ClickUp and Wix, and that agents in some customer environments now run autonomously three times as much as before. That's a company claim, but it matches a theme across recent digests: governance is what decides how much work companies let agents do. The 35-person company is mostly based in Israel.

Sources: VentureBeat, GovInfoSecurity, Globes, The Next Web, FourWeekMBA
