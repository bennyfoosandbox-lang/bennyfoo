1. ServiceNow's AI Workflow Factory turns process mining, building and governance into one loop

ServiceNow announced AI Workflow Factory and Autonomous Engineer on 6 October at its Mumbai World Forum. The Factory chains process mining (to find what to change), Build Agent and Autonomous Engineer (to build it), App Engine (to run it) and AI Control Tower (to govern it), with Action Fabric extending the loop to third-party agents. Autonomous Engineer plans and builds implementation work unattended after a human approves the plan. The Factory is globally available, Autonomous Engineer is early access, and Accenture and Infosys are adopting it. Analysts caution that buyers should measure business outcomes against recurring platform costs. (From six days ago, not covered here before.)

Sources: ServiceNow Newsroom, InfoWorld, CIO, Futurum

2. Datarails survey: finance teams spend 26% of their time checking AI output

A Datarails survey of 270 CFOs and finance leaders at large US companies, released 6 October, found finance teams spend an average of 26% of their work time verifying or correcting AI-generated outputs. Every respondent uses AI in finance, 96% spend at least 10% of their time on checks, and 8% spend more than half. Auditability was the top worry at 75%, ahead of accuracy and hallucinations at 71%. Some 32% overshot AI budgets by at least 10%, yet 53% plan to add licences. The burden is heaviest where systems are disconnected. The survey is vendor-sponsored, so treat the exact figures with care.

Sources: CFO Dive, ITPro, PR Newswire, CPA Practice Advisor

3. Claude Haiku 5.5 cuts small-model costs by about 75% for high-volume work

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on 7 October, calling it its cheapest, fastest and most capable small model. Pricing is $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens for prompts under 100,000 tokens, and $0.50 and $2.50 above that, which Anthropic puts at about 75% below Haiku 4.5 on average. It scored 72.4% on the offline subset of OSWorld 2.1, against 15.7% for Haiku 4.5, and adds an adjustable effort setting. Targeted uses are summaries, classification, support and computer-use sub-agents, which makes high-volume automation cheaper to run. It is on Anthropic's platform plus AWS, Google Cloud and Azure; independent evaluations are still pending.

Sources: YourStory, Clubic, Digital Today, gori.me

4. Nous Research raises $90M to take open-source Hermes agent into business

Nous Research confirmed a $90 million Series B on 7 October, reportedly at a $1.5 billion valuation (a figure from the Wall Street Journal, not the company). The money funds Hermes for Businesses, adding single sign-on, audit trails of what an agent did and when, and granular workspace permissions while the code stays open and customers choose their own models. The WSJ put annualised revenue near $36 million in mid-September, with a goal of over $100 million by year-end; usage claims are the company's own. Reported investors include Robot Ventures, NVIDIA and Microsoft's M12, though outlets differ on the list. It shows open-source agents competing for enterprise budgets on governance features.

Sources: RuntimeWire, Digital Today, Mezha, AI Weekly

5. Manus raises over $500M, its first round since Meta's acquisition was unwound

Manus, the general-purpose AI agent developer, raised more than $500 million on 8 October, its first round since Beijing forced Meta to unwind a roughly $2 billion acquisition. Boyu Capital and IDG Capital led, with Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund participating. Bloomberg reported a prospective $4 billion valuation, which the company has not confirmed. Manus resumed independent operations in August, launched Manus 2.0 soon after, and is reportedly weighing a Hong Kong listing. For business users it signals strong investor appetite for agents that do work end to end, and a reminder that geopolitics can reshape which agent vendors are available.

Sources: TechCrunch, The Next Web, VKTR, Dealroom
