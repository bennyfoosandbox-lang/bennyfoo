1. Zoom's AI shows up in enterprise upsell, not headline growth: enterprise revenue up 8.6% and paid AI in all 10 of its largest CX deals

Zoom reported fiscal Q3 2027 results after the close on 23 November: revenue of $1.29 billion, up 4.9% year on year and slightly ahead of forecasts, with non-GAAP EPS of $1.59. Enterprise revenue grew much faster than the business as a whole, up 8.6% to $805.4 million, and management credited wider use of its platform and AI features among existing enterprise customers, adding that paid AI was part of all ten of its largest contact-centre (CX) deals in the quarter. Zoom raised its full-year revenue guide to $5.100–5.105 billion, declared its first-ever quarterly dividend ($0.25 a share), and pitched its Unified Context Layer, which links meetings, company knowledge and agents. Q4 guidance came in a touch under consensus, though, and Zoom still doesn't break out AI revenue. Read it as AI helping Zoom keep and upsell big accounts rather than sparking a growth spurt, which is a fair measure of what workplace AI is earning vendors right now.

Sources: StockTitan (Zoom results), MLQ.ai, Startup Fortune, Investing.com (call transcript), MarketBeat

2. Accounting firms' AI bills have jumped as vendors switch to usage pricing, and one user ran up $600 in a single day

Accounting Today has run a multi-part “Tokenomics” series this month on what AI now costs accounting firms. The finding is consistent: firm AI spending is up sharply on last year, mostly because model and software vendors have moved from flat subscriptions to consumption billing, even as the price per token has fallen. Inside Public Accounting data cited in the series shows 70% of firms raised their AI budgets over the past year. One CIO caught a single user running up $600 in AI costs in one day by repeatedly reworking the same deliverable, so firms are adding token tracking, usage rules and training, while warning that clamping down too hard could stall staff adoption. The final instalment, on vendors, says accounting-software makers are absorbing much higher R&D compute costs without cutting back and don't plan to pass them on yet. For any business budgeting for AI, the lesson is that per-seat assumptions break once usage is metered, and that cost governance now has to sit next to productivity tracking.

Sources: Accounting Today — Firms see surging AI costs, Accounting Today — Controlling AI spend, Accounting Today — Small firms, Accounting Today — Helping clients, Accounting Today — Vendors

3. Sumitomo Life puts a 203% estimated ROI on ChatGPT Enterprise, with more than 80% of staff using it every week

OpenAI has published a case study on Sumitomo Life, the Japanese life insurer, which moved from a 100–150-person pilot in 2025 to ChatGPT Enterprise for thousands of employees in June 2026. Planning teams use it to research overseas insurers and draft proposals, and investment teams use it to write analysis code. More than 80% of users are active each week. The company puts its overall estimated ROI at 203%, rising to 524% for heavy users who run more than 100 prompts a month. Executives drove adoption from the top, and the insurer plans to widen access in the second half of fiscal 2026 by mapping which tasks in each role AI can take on. Both ROI numbers rest on employees' own estimates of time saved, and Korean outlet TokenPost points out that the case study doesn't explain how they were calculated. Even so, the gap between average and heavy users is the useful part: returns depend on how deeply people use the tool, not just on having a licence.

Sources: OpenAI customer story, TokenPost, BigGo Finance, Blockchain.News

4. Levenza launches personal admin agents worldwide inside Teams, Slack and Outlook, and buys a voice-AI lab

On 23 November Levenza made its personal AI agents generally available to enterprises worldwide and announced it has acquired the voice-AI lab Fluid Inference. The agents work inside Microsoft Teams, Slack and Outlook and take on routine admin without being asked: scheduling, email triage, meeting-room booking and timesheets, with expenses and travel booking due in 2027. Buying Fluid Inference points to voice as the next way people will hand off these tasks. Most enterprise agent launches target departmental workflows. Levenza is going after the small, scattered admin chores that eat into each employee's week, a category Microsoft and Google are also chasing with their built-in assistants.

Sources: GlobeNewswire, Customer Service Manager, The Agile Brand Guide

5. Update: Anthropic opens Enterprise Frontier Safeguards to eligible businesses, so regulated firms can keep zero data retention and still get misuse checks

Anthropic put zero data retention with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS) into public beta for eligible organisations on 23 November. We first covered the feature at its September debut alongside Claude Fable 5.1, when access was limited. With EFS, encrypted copies of a customer's Messages API traffic go to the customer's own cloud storage, where automated checks run about every 15 minutes, spot misuse that spans several requests, and flag serious risks for the customer's security team, without Anthropic keeping the data. Anthropic says it built EFS with more than 100 customers, reportedly doesn't charge for it, and offers it on the Claude Platform, AWS (including Bedrock) and Google Cloud, with Microsoft Foundry still to come. Accenture, Deloitte, KPMG and PwC are named as deployment partners. For banks, law firms and healthcare companies, this removes one of the main blockers to running Anthropic's most capable, retention-restricted models on sensitive work.

Sources: Claude (Anthropic), PYMNTS, Interesting Engineering, AlphaSignal, FourWeekMBA
