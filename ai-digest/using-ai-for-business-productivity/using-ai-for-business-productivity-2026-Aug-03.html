1. Fed survey: nearly half of small employer firms now use AI — but almost none have gone past "experimenting"

A San Francisco Fed research brief published in July, drawing on the 2025 Small Business Credit Survey of 6,525 firms nationwide, finds that 46% of small employer firms now report using AI somewhere in their business, with another 15% planning to start within 12 months. But depth of adoption lags the headline number badly: about half of AI-using firms describe themselves as still "experimenting," 44% say they've only partially integrated it into business processes, and just 7% call their AI use fully integrated. A third of small firms have no plans to adopt AI at all. It's one of the more rigorous, nationally representative looks yet at how far AI adoption actually runs below the largest enterprises — and the gap between "using AI" and "AI running the business," which dominates headlines about companies like UnitedHealth or ADP, is enormous once you leave the Fortune 500.

Sources: San Francisco Fed, Fed in Print, Fed Communities

2. Qualcomm completes its Modular acquisition, betting AI software portability wins the enterprise compute fight

Qualcomm closed its acquisition of AI-infrastructure startup Modular on July 29, folding Modular's Mojo/MAX/Modular Cloud stack into its compute business and naming founder Chris Lattner Executive Vice President of Advanced AI Software and Platforms. The deal targets a specific pain point enterprise buyers keep citing: deploying generative and agentic AI workloads across mixed hardware — edge devices, data centers, industrial systems — without getting locked into a single vendor's chip-and-software stack. It lands the same week Qualcomm reported Q3 earnings that beat on revenue but flagged soft smartphone demand, underlining why the company is racing to diversify into enterprise AI compute; Modular's existing open-source products continue under their own brand rather than being absorbed. For enterprises evaluating where to run agentic workloads, it's a signal that "AI compute portability" is becoming a competitive battleground in its own right, not just a Nvidia-CUDA-versus-everyone-else story.

Sources: PR Newswire, AIwire, Pulse2

3. HubSpot opens Agent Hub and Agent Builder to fight "AI agent sprawl" inside revenue teams

HubSpot moved Agent Hub and Agent Builder into public beta for all Professional and Enterprise customers on July 23, giving go-to-market teams one console to view, activate, and manage every AI agent touching a customer account, plus a no-code canvas for assembling custom agents from natural-language prompts grounded in Smart CRM context. The launch names the exact failure mode other 2026 research keeps flagging: a sales agent and a service agent can independently contact the same account in the same week with no shared awareness of each other, because agents built ad hoc across a stack have no common customer context. Both tools ship included with existing Professional/Enterprise plans, with custom-agent actions metered on HubSpot Credits — a pricing pattern more vendors are adopting to avoid the "runaway agent bill" complaints that have dogged early agentic rollouts elsewhere. It's a bet that unifying and governing agents, not shipping more standalone ones, is what wins the next phase of agentic CRM.

Sources: HubSpot, CMSWire, CX Today

4. New survey: UK, US and DACH firms have largely exited the AI pilot phase — but strategy now splits them

A global whitepaper from software-development firm Vention, covering 450+ companies and 480+ tech leaders across the US, UK and DACH region and published in the past week, finds only 5% of UK respondents are still running AI pilots, versus 53% using AI in specific business areas and 41% calling it central to their product or overall business. Software development is one of the most AI-saturated functions measured: 79% of UK firms use AI somewhere in their dev process, most often for idea generation, architecture design, and coding assistance, with 54% having already invested in team AI upskilling. The report frames 2026 as a genuine phase shift from "should we try AI" to "how do we scale it well," while finding the three regions diverge sharply on strategy, talent sourcing, and how much unsupervised work they're willing to hand agentic systems. It adds a concrete data point to a now-familiar pattern this digest keeps surfacing: the pilot stage is largely over, but the maturity gap between regions, functions, and company sizes is widening rather than closing.

Sources: Vention, IT Brief UK
