1. Zendesk puts AI agents on the internal-admin queue, priced per resolution

Zendesk began rolling out its employee service AI agents on 3 November, with the rollout running through 1 December for Employee Service Suite Growth plans and above. The agents answer in the help center, Slack and Microsoft Teams, read more than 20 knowledge sources (SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence, Notion, Box) while respecting existing permissions, and can run multi-step action flows or hand off to a person. The pricing is the notable part: enterprise plans include about 15 automated resolutions per agent each month, then $0.99 per extra resolution. That ties spend to outcomes and pushes buyers to decide which IT, HR and finance requests are worth automating, and it puts Zendesk in direct competition with ServiceNow for internal IT work.

Sources: UC Today, Futurum Group, Zendesk Support, Enjo

2. Box Architect: describe a process in plain language and get a governed agent workflow

At BoxWorks on 5 November, Box unveiled Box Architect, a tool that designs, tests and deploys agentic workflows from a plain-language description of a process, built on content already stored in Box. Each step stays under existing permissions, classifications and retention policies, with MCP guardrails, threshold alerts and human review at key decision points. Box's examples include a law-firm contract review that learns an approved playbook and routes high-risk clauses to attorneys, and a government permitting workflow. Architect starts rolling out in beta to Enterprise Advanced customers in Q4. CEO Aaron Levie frames the goal as rethinking whole business processes rather than just speeding up individuals.

Sources: Box Investor Relations (press release), Reworked, Techzine, UC Today

3. Cisco research: trust, not technology, is now the speed limit on enterprise AI

A Cisco-commissioned report, "Permission to Scale: The Critical Role of Trust in Successful AI Adoption," argues that AI can already do far more than most organizations allow it to. It draws on 25 Australian organizations and cites University of Melbourne and KPMG data showing 50% of Australians use AI regularly but only 36% trust it. Cisco's separate Relentless Defense report found just 36% of Australian organizations apply consistent controls to AI agents and non-human identities. The recommended path is an adoption flywheel in which evidence builds trust and trust permits wider use, aiming for calibrated trust rather than blind faith or demands for perfection. This is a small-sample, vendor-sponsored study.

Sources: SiliconANGLE, Cisco (PR Newswire)

4. ANZ builds an "enterprise AI control plane" and says it will stop pursuing AI everywhere

Announcing a $7.59 billion cash profit for the year to 30 September (up 31%), ANZ CEO Nuno Matos said the bank is building an enterprise AI control plane on top of its existing AI gateways and governance council. It will give visibility and technical control across users, models, agents, tools, costs and platforms, with traceability of decisions and defined limits that halt agent activity outside bounds. Matos said that as agents proliferate, humans must remain in control, and that ANZ will prioritise AI opportunities that unquestionably deliver value with right-sized investment rather than pursuing AI everywhere. It is a concrete large-bank example of governance and cost discipline coming before scale.

Sources: iTnews

5. China's agent-portal cull: ByteDance and Tencent retire products as Alibaba bets on QwenWork

AI Magazine reports that ByteDance has wound down Coze Enterprise Edition after just over a year, and Tencent has shut its Yuanqi agent platform after two years, part of 38 AI products reportedly shut down or merged across Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent, Baidu, Moonshot AI and MiniMax, per 36Kr. Alibaba is positioning QwenWork, its all-in-one workplace agent platform merging Qoderwork, Mulerun and Wukong, as the survivor. It plugs into DingTalk, which serves over 20 million organisations, and offers four model tiers including the flagship Qwen3.8. The takeaway for buyers is that standalone agent portals are consolidating into agents embedded where work already happens.

Sources: AI Magazine
