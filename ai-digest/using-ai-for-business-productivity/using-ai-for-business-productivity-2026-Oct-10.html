1. Google launches 'Gemini agent', a universal agent for work, in private preview

At its Gemini at Work event on 8 October, Google Cloud introduced a single cloud-based agent that takes objectives rather than step-by-step instructions, spins up sub-agents and works across Workspace, Slack and Microsoft 365. It keeps one memory across devices and channels, can be reached from a command line or an API as a headless agent, and picks between Gemini and Anthropic Claude models depending on the task. Each agent gets its own identity, scoped access and activity log, and IT sets company-wide rules and spending caps once. It is in private preview, with wider availability promised for select Business and Enterprise Workspace plans; the push puts Google squarely against Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic for the 'AI coworker' budget.

Sources: 9to5Google, MarkTechPost, Quartz, Android Authority, Reworked

2. OpenAI starts rolling out GPT-6 with an 'Intelligent UI' that answers with interactive charts and forms

Reports say OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 alongside an Intelligent UI in ChatGPT on 7 October, with Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise first and Free and Go following from 8 October. Instead of plain text, replies can embed interactive charts, forms, buttons and calculators, which matters for workers who use ChatGPT to build quick analyses rather than just read answers. Enterprise availability can depend on admin settings, and users can dial down the amount of visual output. Coverage conflicts on which model tier powers which plan, and I found no primary OpenAI announcement, so admins should check their workspace model picker for what is actually enabled.

Sources: Let's Data Science, Scalevise

3. Stuut raises $52.5M Series B to run order-to-cash with AI agents

New York startup Stuut announced a $52.5 million Series B on 7 October, led by Insight Partners with Andreessen Horowitz and Microsoft's M12, bringing total funding to $93 million just ten months after its Series A. Its agents sit on top of existing ERP, CRM and payment systems and handle invoicing, collections, cash application and disputes. The company reports matching over 95% of incoming payments to orders automatically and cutting customers' days sales outstanding by 47%, and says it runs across 150+ enterprises processing more than $3 billion in payments; those figures are company-reported and customer lists differ between outlets. It is a clear example of finance back-office work becoming one of the first places agents earn their keep.

Sources: The Next Web, AI Weekly, HackerNoon

4. Cognizant launches 'Activate' to sell enterprise-grade AI transformation to $1B–$5B companies

Cognizant announced Activate on 5 October, a business unit for 'emerging enterprises' with $1 billion to $5 billion in annual revenue that sit between off-the-shelf software and consultancies built for the largest firms. It packages pre-configured kits for data and AI, cybersecurity, enterprise applications, cloud modernization and managed services, with a single accountable engagement lead from first conversation to delivery. It starts in financial services, healthcare, manufacturing and retail/CPG. No pricing, named customers or measured outcomes were disclosed, and the speed and single-lead claims come from Cognizant's own release, so treat it as a signal of where mid-market AI services are heading rather than proof of results.

Sources: Let's Data Science, HPCwire BigDATAwire, Finviz (press release)

5. Infor's 2026.10 release pushes industry AI agents from advising to governed execution

Infor's October release expands its Industry AI Agents across ERP, finance and supply chain, framing the line that matters as whether AI is built to execute rather than just advise. Alongside the agents it extends AI governance inside its GRC product so firms can manage AI initiatives next to other business risk, and it argues trusted data and governance have to advance together. Analyst notes from Infor's 2026 summit say buyers now ask whether agents can be governed, measured and scaled across end-to-end processes, and describe a roadmap from 'ERP with AI features' toward packaged, governed execution via Velocity Suite and an Agentic Orchestrator. Detail on the specific 2026.10 agent capabilities is thin and the main source is vendor marketing.

Sources: Infor blog, TechFinitive, Software Reviews
