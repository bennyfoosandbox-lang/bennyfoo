1. Anthropic's leaked IPO prospectus shows a $518B spending plan behind its enterprise boom

A draft of Anthropic's S-1 leaked on 28 September, giving the first detailed look at the books of the company many businesses now rely on for agents and coding tools. Preliminary Q2 revenue topped $11.5 billion, up from $787 million a year earlier, yet the filing also lays out roughly $518 billion of compute and infrastructure obligations against about $4.6 billion of 2025 revenue. It also shows customer concentration, with around a quarter of revenue coming from just two clients. For companies standardising on Claude, the practical takeaway is vendor-risk: the supplier is growing at extraordinary speed but is financing that growth with very large long-term commitments. The prospectus is still a leaked draft, not a public filing, and the figures may change before listing.

Sources: Fortune, Quartz, PYMNTS, The Next Web (Q2 revenue)

2. VentureBeat Pulse: OpenAI turns 69% of agent-platform installs into the primary platform; Claude manages 38%

A new VentureBeat Pulse read on enterprise agent platforms separates companies that merely try a platform from those that build on it. OpenAI converts 69 of every 100 installs into the primary orchestration platform, versus 38 for Anthropic's Claude Platform. Among primary platforms, OpenAI leads with 53 of 162 enterprises (33%), followed by Google's Enterprise Agent Platform at 24% and Anthropic at 11%. The finding matters because spend-based indices, such as Ramp's and OpenRouter's, cannot tell a trial from a production commitment, and so can overstate who is actually winning enterprise agent work. This is from the survey's August wave, reported in September, so treat it as a recent snapshot rather than breaking news.

Sources: VentureBeat, Ecosistema Startup

3. White House convenes AI CEOs on industrial strategy and safety rules for frontier models

President Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson met chief executives from the leading AI companies at the White House on 29 September. Reported attendees included Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Palantir's Alex Karp and OpenAI's Greg Brockman. The stated agenda covered industrial strategy, global competitiveness and safety rules for the most advanced models, and it followed OpenAI cancelling the release of its newest model over safety concerns. For businesses building on these vendors, the signal is that frontier-model rules are now being negotiated at the top of government, which could affect model availability, release timing and compliance obligations. No specific rules were announced in the coverage found.

Sources: MeriTalk, Pasquale Pillitteri
