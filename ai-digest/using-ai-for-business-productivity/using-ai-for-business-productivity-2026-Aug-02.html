1. Amazon's AWS accelerates for a 5th straight quarter as Bedrock and AI demand surge

Amazon's Q2 2026 results, reported July 30, showed AWS revenue growth accelerating to 36.7% year over year — its fifth consecutive quarter of acceleration — with an annualized run rate of $169 billion and a backlog of $496 billion growing at a triple-digit pace. Amazon Bedrock, its managed AI-model service, grew fast enough that customers spent more on it in the quarter than in all prior quarters combined, while AWS operating margin expanded roughly 650 basis points to 39% on efficiency and capacity gains. Amazon also raised its 2026 cash capex outlook to roughly $220 billion, citing rising memory costs, and said it still won't have enough capacity to meet demand in 2026 or 2027. For enterprise buyers building AI-driven productivity tools, the takeaway is that the compute crunch underneath agentic software isn't easing anytime soon — demand keeps outrunning even hyperscaler build-outs.

Sources: CNBC, GuruFocus, Investing.com

2. UnitedHealth: "AI now runs every function of our business" (from earlier this month, newly covered here)

On its Q2 2026 earnings call, UnitedHealth Group's chairman and CEO said the insurer now routes virtually every claim, prior authorization, and patient interaction through AI as part of what he called "the operating infrastructure of the future." AI-powered prior authorization is hitting a 96% first-pass approval rate, and the company committed to eliminating 30% of prior-authorization volume by year-end, including nearly two-thirds of prior-auth requirements for pediatric care. The payoff showed up directly in the numbers: adjusted EPS of $6.38 versus $4.08 a year earlier, operating earnings up 55% year over year, and a raised full-year outlook, on top of roughly $1.5 billion invested in AI to streamline operations. It's one of the clearest examples yet of AI-driven productivity gains landing directly on a large regulated company's bottom line rather than staying stuck at the pilot stage. Note on timing: this earnings call was held July 15 — before this digest series began tracking the topic — and hasn't been covered in a prior run, so it's included here as a genuinely new-to-this-KB finding even though it isn't from the last 24 hours.

Sources: PYMNTS, Business Wire, Tech Times

3. ExlService's AI-led services now drive 61% of revenue, and growth is accelerating

ExlService Holdings' Q2 2026 results, released July 28, showed data- and AI-led services now making up 61% of the business-process-services provider's revenue, with that segment's growth accelerating to 30% year over year. Total company revenue rose 16% to $595 million and adjusted EPS climbed 22%, with insurance revenue up 15% and healthcare up 22%; the company raised full-year revenue guidance to $2.39-$2.415 billion. EXL's recent acquisition of iMerit is aimed at deepening its AI data-labeling and model-training capabilities to expand further into high-growth AI segments, even as legacy "digital operations" revenue dipped slightly as AI gets embedded directly into client workflows. It's a concrete data point for the thesis that outsourced back-office work is increasingly becoming AI-run work — and that the vendors leaning hardest into that shift are the ones growing fastest.

Sources: GuruFocus, Investing.com, Simply Wall St

4. Elastic and OpenAI team up to ground AI agents in companies' messy, unstructured data

Elastic announced an expanded collaboration with OpenAI on July 30 targeting the unglamorous problem behind most stalled AI agent projects: most enterprise data — documents, tickets, logs, traces, security alerts — is unstructured, permission-gated, and constantly changing, and agents perform badly without reliable access to it. The partnership pairs Elasticsearch's retrieval and governance layer with OpenAI's reasoning models across three areas: permission-aware knowledge retrieval for context-aware agents, agentic observability for correlating telemetry and root-cause investigation, and agentic security operations for alert triage. Elastic also plans to integrate OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Cyber models into its security workflows through OpenAI's Daybreak Cyber Partner Program. It's a direct pitch at the data-readiness gap that keeps showing up across enterprise AI surveys as the real blocker to scaling agents.

Sources: BigDATAwire, AIwire, MarTech Series
