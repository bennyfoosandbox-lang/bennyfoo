1. Microsoft's Azure crosses $100B as Copilot hits 30 million paid seats

Microsoft's fiscal Q4 2026 earnings, reported July 29, showed Azure cloud revenue surpassing $100 billion annually for the first time, with FY26 revenue of $331 billion and quarterly growth accelerating to 18%. CEO Satya Nadella highlighted Microsoft 365 Copilot crossing 30 million paid seats — up from roughly 20 million in April — while GitHub Copilot's coding assistant reached 50 million users, direct evidence that AI-assisted productivity tools are now embedded at scale inside large organizations. The company spent $41 billion on capex in the quarter alone, adding 31 new datacenters, and its stakes in OpenAI and Anthropic contributed roughly $8 billion combined in investment gains to the bottom line. The results suggest enterprise AI spending is still compounding even as other companies (Cognizant, SAP, IBM) warn of a productivity plateau elsewhere.

Sources: CNBC, Fortune, Microsoft Source

2. New global survey: 73% of enterprises use AI regularly, but only 10% call it core to how they operate

Publicis Sapient's 2026 Global Enterprise AI Report, released at VivaTech Paris, surveyed 1,550 AI decision-makers at companies with at least 500 employees and $100M+ revenue across six countries. It found 73% of respondents say AI is used regularly or across most business processes, yet only 10% describe it as core to how their business actually operates, and 42% say their organization isn't structured to capture its value. CEO Nigel Vaz frames the gap as an operating-model problem rather than a technology one, arguing "the enterprise was not designed for the speed, scale and autonomy that AI makes possible." The findings extend a running 2026 theme in this digest — echoed by IBM, SAP and Dun & Bradstreet in recent editions — that deployment breadth has badly outrun organizational redesign.

Sources: Publicis Sapient, MarketScale, Information & Data Manager

3. Cognizant: a quarter of enterprises have paused AI deployments over unproven ROI — even as its own numbers improve

On its Q2 2026 earnings call, Cognizant said roughly one in four enterprise clients have paused AI deployments and that two-thirds of the Global 2000 have yet to see measurable AI productivity gains — a "value gap" it says it's now building services around, including context engineering and AI-infused rate cards, to close. Cognizant's own metrics tell a more optimistic story: over 40% of its software development is now AI-assisted, adjusted operating income per associate rose 7.1%, and revenue per associate climbed 4.6%. Revenue grew 4.1% in constant currency to $5.5 billion, at the top of guidance, though the company trimmed its full-year outlook citing macro caution. The split suggests internal, well-governed AI use is paying off faster than the AI Cognizant sells to clients.

Sources: Yahoo Finance, GuruFocus, BigGo Finance

4. China's 360 enters the crowded office-AI-agent race with a free-token push for small business

Qihoo 360 founder Zhou Hongyi launched "Nano Work" on July 28, a multi-agent enterprise work platform built after more than 1,000 internal test scenarios and 166 version iterations, aimed at letting non-technical staff orchestrate AI agents across everyday office tasks. 360 is giving early adopters 100 million tokens and has committed to helping 1,000 small businesses implement the platform, undercutting incumbents like Tencent in a Chinese office-AI-agent market that reached roughly 80.4 billion yuan (about $11.9B) in 2025. Zhou pitched "native security by default" as the platform's differentiator, warning that "an agent making mistakes is doing the wrong thing" — a materially higher-stakes failure mode than a chatbot giving a bad answer. The launch is a reminder that agent-based office productivity tooling is now a direct competitive battleground well beyond the US ecosystem.

Sources: BigGo Finance, All Weather Finance

5. Zuckerberg: Meta's enterprise AI opportunity goes well beyond agents

On Meta's Q2 2026 earnings call, Mark Zuckerberg said the company's AI business opportunity extends past coding and task agents toward "personal superintelligence" — always-on AI agents working 24/7 on a person's goals, health, relationships and finances — which he called the foundation for Meta's "next wave of products and revenue lines." He noted Meta already runs business agents for more than a million businesses on WhatsApp and Messenger, autonomously handling customer inquiries and completing sales without a person in the loop, and argued that selling intelligence carries a "significantly higher margin" than selling raw compute. The remarks came alongside Meta's Q2 results — covered in this digest on Jul-30 — showing AI capex up 83% year-over-year to $31.1B and free cash flow cratering, underscoring that Zuckerberg is doubling down on agent-driven monetization even as the near-term cash cost mounts. It's a bet that business productivity tooling (agents doing real transactional work, not just chat) becomes Meta's next major revenue line.

Sources: TechCrunch, TechCrunch, The AI Insider
