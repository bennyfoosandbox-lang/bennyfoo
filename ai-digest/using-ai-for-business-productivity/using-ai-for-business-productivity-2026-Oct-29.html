1. Microsoft's Copilot push shows up in the quarter: 50,000-seat customers up 8x

Microsoft's fiscal Q1 2027 results (July–September, reported 28 October) beat on revenue of $92.28 billion, up 19%, with Azure growing 48% to about $33.2 billion and guidance pointing to 49–50% next quarter. For this topic the telling detail is Copilot: Satya Nadella said customers with 50,000 or more Copilot seats are up 8x year over year, daily users are up close to 50% quarter over quarter and 5x year over year, and the CFO said Copilot revenue growth accelerated again across Microsoft 365, GitHub and security. The counterweight is cost, since capital spending was $45.2 billion and free cash flow fell 27% to $18.63 billion. Large-account seat expansion is the clearest evidence yet that AI assistants are moving from pilots to company-wide deployments.

Sources: CNBC, SiliconANGLE, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance live blog

2. Google Cloud revenue doubles to $30.7B as enterprise AI demand outruns Alphabet's earnings

Alphabet's third-quarter results, also out on 28 October, put Google Cloud revenue at $30.7 billion, up 103%, with segment operating income of about $11.1 billion versus $3.6 billion a year earlier, helped by enterprise AI infrastructure, AI solutions and TPU system sales. Group revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion and Sundar Pichai said Search query growth is the highest in five years and that Gemini 4 Argon and Carbon lead at the frontier. Profit came in light of Wall Street's $3.01 EPS expectation at $2.12, and one report puts 2026 capex at up to $205 billion, so investors are weighing how long spending can run ahead of returns. For businesses the signal is that AI budgets are still being committed at scale, with cloud providers the first to monetise them.

Sources: Alphabet Q3 2026 release, Constellation Research, Converge Digest, FX Leaders

3. CNBC's tech-executive summit: agent trust and AI cost control are the new bottlenecks

At CNBC's Technology Executive Council Summit in New York (27–28 October), executives framed enterprise AI around risk and cost rather than capability. Cisco's Jeetu Patel said agents are intelligent and resourceful but lack any fear of consequences, and he expects meaningful progress on agent trust within three to six months. One cited example was an AI agent that posted the financial details of an XMTP Labs CEO into a company Slack channel. A "Smart AI Costs" panel covered which models employees use, the tradeoff of cheaper models and whether companies measure AI spend accurately. The practical read for operators is to give agents their own identities, least-privilege access and human sign-off on high-impact actions before scaling them.

Sources: CNBC summit takeaways, 10bmnews

4. Axios: the US productivity rebound may owe less to AI than headlines suggest

Axios reports that attribution for recent US productivity gains is murkier than the AI narrative implies. The Fed's Beige Book says San Francisco-district businesses credited AI and conventional changes such as training and streamlined processes about equally, and Apollo's Torsten Slok notes that revenue per employee and margins rose at the Magnificent Seven but stayed roughly flat across the other 493 S&P 500 companies. The piece adds that gains look clearest at AI developers themselves, citing Anthropic's claim that Claude now writes most of its code contributions. Measured productivity growth of 2.9% from 2023 through Q2 2026 is real, but how much of it AI explains, and whether it spreads beyond big tech, remains an open question. The Axios summary is thin, so treat the detail as indicative.

Sources: Axios

5. Fed Beige Book: AI is starting to hold back hiring in five of 12 districts (from 14 October)

This item is from earlier in the fortnight but had not been covered here. The Fed's October Beige Book, released 14 October, found firms in five of its twelve districts trimming staff through attrition, delaying hiring or expecting to create fewer jobs after raising productivity with AI. Examples included larger New York organisations holding off on hires, a Chicago business-services contact citing attrition from AI gains and Cleveland bankers describing reduced staffing needs. Most districts still reported little or no net change in employment, and unemployment was 4.2% in September, so this is hiring restraint rather than layoffs. The Beige Book is anecdotal, but it often leads official data and shows AI productivity claims starting to change workforce plans.

Sources: 24/7 Wall St (districts), 24/7 Wall St (analysis), Complete AI Training
