1. Crusoe raises $3.9B Series F at a $30.9B valuation to build vertically integrated "AI factories"

AI-infrastructure company Crusoe closed a $3.9 billion Series F on Sept. 17, pushing its valuation to $30.9 billion, in a round co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, GIC, Nvidia, Qatar Investment Authority and TPG also participating. Crusoe — which pivoted from a crypto-mining operation running on flared natural gas into a builder of vertically integrated AI data centers — says it now has more than 6 gigawatts of gross contracted capacity, with over 1 GW delivered and operational, serving customers including Meta, Microsoft and Oracle. The new capital funds both large-scale campuses and modular "Crusoe Spark" units, part of a broader pattern this year of AI-infrastructure financing rounds outpacing application-layer funding as enterprises scramble to secure the compute their AI agents and productivity tools depend on. It's a reminder that the business-productivity story is downstream of a capital-intensive infrastructure buildout most workers never see.

Sources: TechCrunch, Crusoe Newsroom, GlobeNewswire, TheNextWeb

2. Salesforce follows AIforce with its own reasoning model, Koa, and an Anthropic-built "Claudeforce" skill pack

Two days after unveiling AIforce at Dreamforce, Salesforce disclosed further details on two more products built on top of it: Koa, a CRM-specific reasoning model built on Nvidia's open Nemotron 3 Super and expected to reach US general availability in winter 2026, and Claudeforce, an expanded Anthropic partnership that places a prebuilt MCP server carrying 37 ready-made sales skills directly inside Claude. Salesforce claims Koa matches or beats leading frontier models on CRM-specific actions with three times fewer errors, and says 1-800Accountant, Formula 1 and UChicago Medicine are already piloting it; Claudeforce, meanwhile, lets organizations work Salesforce data — prospecting, pipeline management, account research — from inside Claude itself while data stays inside Salesforce's system of record. Together with AIforce, the announcements show Salesforce hedging across two bets simultaneously: build a proprietary, task-tuned model for accuracy-sensitive CRM actions, while also making peace with the fact that many knowledge workers will keep doing their actual work inside a general-purpose chat interface like Claude, not a CRM tab.

Sources: TechCrunch, Anthropic, Tech Times, The Rundown AI

3. Notion 3.7 lets teams package their own AI know-how into shareable, portable "skills"

Notion shipped version 3.7 on Sept. 15, headlined by "Skills" — reusable instructions that teach Notion's AI agents how a specific team works, such as generating a monthly business review in a preferred format or critiquing a document against a manager's standards. Teams can now store skills in a shared Notion database so everyone in a workspace stays on the same, current version rather than re-explaining conventions to the AI every time, and skills can be exported as SKILL.md files to run inside Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini or Grok. The release also adds nested/sub-agent calls, so a custom agent can delegate specific steps to other custom agents each carrying their own instructions and permissions, plus an embedded-agent option inside documents and a new Agents iOS app. It's a concrete example of the "governed agent" trend spreading beyond big enterprise software into everyday productivity tools: less "chat with AI," more "codify how our team works so the AI does it the same way every time."

Sources: Notion Releases, AlternativeTo, Notion Help Center

4. Comp AI raises $34M Series A to push "agentic compliance" into continuous cybersecurity

Miami-based Comp AI announced a $34 million Series A on Sept. 17, led by Roo Capital and Grand Ventures, bringing its total funding to $37.5 million since its January 2025 founding. The company sells AI agents that write security policies, gather audit evidence and keep SOC 2 and similar certifications continuously up to date instead of treating compliance as an annual scramble — the kind of labor-intensive paperwork that otherwise blocks enterprise software deals from closing. Comp AI says it has grown revenue 15x year-over-year and now serves more than 1,000 customers, and plans to use the new capital to expand from compliance automation into continuous cybersecurity: real-time monitoring, control validation and security testing across applications and infrastructure. It's another data point for a theme running through this week's digests — back-office and compliance automation, not flashy chat interfaces, is where a lot of near-term enterprise AI ROI is showing up first.

Sources: TechCrunch, SecurityWeek, Comp AI, TheNextWeb

5. Anthropic says Claude sped up 30+ open-source biology models roughly 4x, hinting at AI-accelerated R&D at scale

Anthropic published research on Sept. 17 reporting that Claude optimized more than 30 open-source biomolecular models — spanning structure prediction, protein design, inverse folding and genomics — in under four weeks, speeding them up by a factor of roughly 4x on average and building a low-memory mode that lets a single Nvidia GPU node handle biomolecular systems larger than 10,000 tokens. Anthropic open-sourced the optimized code, launched a protein-design competition with Adaptyv Bio backed by up to $1 million in Claude credits and wet-lab validation for over 5,000 designs, and introduced a gated-access Life Sciences Verification Program for researchers. For a business-productivity lens, the result is less about biology specifically than about a template: a general-purpose model doing weeks of specialized optimization engineering that would otherwise consume scarce PhD-level researcher time, a pattern enterprises across finance, law and engineering are watching closely as a proof point for where "AI does the unglamorous technical grind" claims actually hold up under scrutiny.

Sources: Anthropic, Unite.AI, Endpoints News
