1. Skan AI raises $63M to build an "operating map" of how enterprise work actually happens

Skan AI, a Menlo Park startup, closed a $63 million Series C on August 12 co-led by Cathay Innovation and Dell Technologies Capital, with Citi Ventures, Bloomberg Beta, State Farm Ventures and Wipro Ventures also participating — pushing total funding to roughly $120 million. The round lands alongside general availability of two new products, Skan AI Blueprint and Skan AI Agents, built on the pitch that most enterprise AI deployments fail because they're built on assumptions about workflows rather than observed reality. Skan's software watches how employees actually move work across CRM, email and even mainframe sessions, then distills those observations into a continuously updated model of how a business actually runs — context the company argues is the missing layer for reliable agentic AI. Cathay Innovation led Skan's $14 million Series A and Dell Technologies Capital led its $40 million Series B; both firms co-leading the C signals conviction that "process intelligence" is becoming its own category alongside agent orchestration and governance.

Sources: VentureBeat, PR Newswire, Dell Technologies Capital, Dealroom

2. Oracle plans a fresh round of layoffs to help fund its AI data-center borrowing spree

Oracle is preparing another round of job cuts in August 2026, according to an internal document Business Insider reported viewing on August 11, with managers asked to identify affected employees ahead of the September 1 start of its second fiscal quarter. The move follows a fiscal year in which Oracle's headcount already shrank by 21,000 people (13%) to roughly 141,000; multiple reports suggest the new round could affect as many as 30,000 more roles, though Oracle hasn't confirmed a figure. The cuts trace directly to Oracle's AI infrastructure bet: capital expenditure hit $55.7 billion in fiscal 2026, up from $21.2 billion the year before, funded by $43 billion in new debt and $5 billion in stock sales, against a commitment to roughly $156 billion in AI buildout spending. It is one of the starkest examples yet of a company treating headcount reduction as a direct lever to free up cash — an estimated $8-10 billion — for AI capacity, rather than citing AI-driven productivity gains in the roles being cut.

Sources: Yahoo Finance, Quartz, TheNextWeb, TheStreet

3. Microsoft starts merging Copilot into one app — the first concrete step toward its "super app"

Microsoft began rolling out the long-planned merger of its consumer Copilot app and Microsoft 365 Copilot into a single application this week, with some Windows Insiders receiving the update starting August 10 and a worldwide mobile-and-web rollout beginning mid-August; Windows and Mac apps follow in mid-September. The unified app keeps the Microsoft Copilot name and a single icon, and lets users sign in with a personal account, a work/school account, or both, switching between them in the same shell — folding consumer chat and image generation into the same interface as the enterprise work experience. Our August 11 digest covered Microsoft's plan, unveiled on its July 29 earnings call, to merge the apps and add a paid, always-on Autopilot/Scout agent tier; this week's news is that the merge itself has actually started shipping, with the Podcasts feature and consumer Deep Research both being fully retired on August 18 as part of the transition. For enterprises already juggling separate Copilot licenses and identities, the rollout is an early look at how Microsoft intends to blur the line between personal AI assistant and paid workplace agent, with the always-on tier expected to arrive as a distinct, separately billed layer on top.

Sources: Fortune, GeekWire, Windows Central, Slashdot

4. Appian says customer AI usage is up 20x, cementing its pitch as an AI "control plane"

Appian's Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 13, showed cloud subscription revenue up 23% year-over-year to $131.7 million and total revenue up 19% to $203.3 million, with adjusted EBITDA doubling to $16.2 million — and the company credited AI for the acceleration. Customer usage of Appian's AI features rose 20-fold year-over-year, and 85% of new customer logos in the quarter purchased AI-enabled products, with deployments concentrated in healthcare, finance, banking and government. Management framed Appian's opportunity as being the "control plane" or orchestration layer that makes probabilistic AI reliable enough for mission-critical, regulated workflows — deterministic process logic wrapped around AI agents rather than AI acting unsupervised. On the strength of the quarter, Appian raised its full-year 2026 cloud subscription guidance to $525-529 million (about 20% growth) and total revenue guidance to $845-853 million, with shares jumping more than 12% on the report.

Sources: The Motley Fool, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance, Seeking Alpha

5. AI now drives over a third of ISG's revenue, and it's growing 64% a quarter

Information Services Group, the tech research and advisory firm, reported Q2 2026 AI-related revenue growth of 64% to $26 million, spread across AI advisory, research and governance work; for the first half of 2026, AI-related revenue reached $47 million — 37% of ISG's total firm-wide revenue. Overall Q2 revenue came in at $65.5 million, up 6.4% year-over-year, with adjusted EBITDA up 12.9% to $9.4 million and margin rising more than 80 basis points to 14.3%, while recurring revenue hit a record $30 million. The board approved a new $30 million share buyback, the largest in company history, reflecting confidence in the trajectory; ISG guided to $63.5-64.5 million in Q3 revenue. ISG's numbers are a useful proxy for a second-order effect of enterprise AI adoption: as companies scale agentic AI, spending on the advisory and governance layer that helps them do it safely is compounding faster than the underlying software spend itself.

Sources: GuruFocus, StockTitan, Yahoo Finance, Investing.com
