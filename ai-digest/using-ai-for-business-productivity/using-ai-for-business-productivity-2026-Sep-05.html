1. Anthropic pairs a cheaper frontier model with a fix for enterprises' biggest AI objection: data control

On September 1, Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 — two safeguard tiers of the same underlying model, with Fable 5.1 generally available for coding and knowledge work at a lower price and fewer restrictions, and Mythos 5.1 gated to vetted cybersecurity and life-sciences partners through new verification programs run with the US government. Alongside the release, Anthropic introduced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards (EFS), which combines zero data retention with automated misuse detection by storing activity logs inside infrastructure the customer controls rather than Anthropic's own cloud. EFS was built with more than 100 customers across financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, telecom, law, retail and the public sector, plus AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure as cloud partners, and begins rolling out in phases this fall. For business productivity, the pairing matters more than either piece alone: it ships materially better economics for everyday coding and knowledge work while removing the data-governance objection that has kept regulated industries from putting frontier models on sensitive workflows.

Sources: Anthropic, TechCrunch, CNBC, Unite.AI

2. Docusign opens its "Agreement Layer" to every AI agent, not just ChatGPT

Docusign announced an "Agreement Layer for the Agentic Enterprise," opening its Model Context Protocol (MCP) Server so any compatible AI agent — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot and Slack included — can natively call its Iris AI engine to create, analyze and act on agreements without a person switching apps. The company had already brought its Intelligent Agreement Management platform into ChatGPT and Codex through MCP; global availability for the wider agent ecosystem lands September 30, giving agents governed access to past negotiations, accepted contract terms and company policy rather than a blank slate. Agreements touch nearly every department — sales, procurement, HR, legal — so automating their creation and review directly inside the tools employees already use is a concrete productivity lever rather than a chatbot demo. It's also a bet that agreement infrastructure, not just conversational interfaces, becomes one of the layers agents plug into across the enterprise stack.

Sources: Docusign, Docusign Investor Relations, StockTitan

3. HiddenLayer raises $100M to secure AI agents once they're running in production

AI security startup HiddenLayer closed a $100 million Series B led by Delta-v Capital, with Booz Allen Ventures, Microsoft's M12, Morgan Stanley and Ten Eleven Ventures participating — pushing its total raised past $155 million. The Austin-based company secures the full lifecycle of agentic, generative and predictive AI systems, and will use the funds to deepen its Agentic Runtime Security module and launch Agent Harness Security, a new product aimed specifically at protecting autonomous coding agents once they're operating inside a company's stack. The round lands as enterprises increasingly grant agents write and deploy access to production code and data, widening the attack surface faster than most security teams' visibility into what those agents are actually doing. Microsoft's own venture arm backing the round underscores how central "trustworthy AI" tooling has become to the productivity pitch for agentic deployments — the promised time savings only materialize if enterprises trust agents aren't quietly introducing risk alongside the speed.

Sources: SecurityWeek, Unite.AI, PR Newswire, Fintech Global

4. EY: agentic AI could take over 80% of routine treasury work

A new EY analysis estimates agentic AI could automate roughly 80% of routine treasury coordination and processing — pulling data from disparate systems, manual validation, report preparation — freeing treasury staff to focus on risk management and strategic funding decisions that need human judgment. The report notes 60-70% of mid-level treasury bandwidth currently goes to exactly this kind of low-value work, with more than half of companies still relying on manual batch reconciliation; applied to cash forecasting, agentic AI could push 30-, 60- and 90-day liquidity forecast accuracy to around 90%, letting firms shrink excess liquidity buffers and free up capital. EY's caveat is the one running through most enterprise-AI reporting this year: fragmented ERP, treasury-platform and banking data has to be unified into a working data layer before agents can be trusted with the numbers, and the firm recommends starting with lower-risk use cases like reconciliation and KYC/AML exception handling. It's a rare function-specific estimate of where agent automation actually lands, in a year dominated by broad "is AI paying off" surveys.

Sources: ANI News, Asianet Newsable, Daily Prabhat

5. CauzzyAI wires AI agents straight into Unilever Prestige's ERP systems

CauzzyAI and Unilever Prestige announced a partnership bringing CauzzyAI's enterprise agent platform across Unilever's prestige beauty brands, connecting AI agents directly into ERP systems (including Oracle NetSuite) and enterprise data warehouses to automate workflows and surface real-time business insights. Unlike single-prompt assistants, CauzzyAI's agents are pitched as holding business context and orchestrating processes across multiple systems continuously, rather than responding one prompt at a time. Unilever Prestige frames the deal as a step toward becoming an "AI-native enterprise," simplifying complex cross-brand processes so teams spend less time on manual handoffs between systems and more on higher-value decisions. It's a concrete data point on where agentic enterprise software is actually landing this quarter — wired into the specific ERP and data infrastructure a consumer-goods company already runs on, not layered on top as a generic chat widget.

Sources: Business Wire, 01net.it, The AI Journal
