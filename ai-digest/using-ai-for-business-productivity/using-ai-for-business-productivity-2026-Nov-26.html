1. Fitch says US productivity is running at 1.9% a year, but it's too early to credit AI, and AI hasn't cut overall employment

Fitch Ratings published an economics report on 24 November finding that US labour productivity has grown about 1.9% a year over the past four years, well above the 2010–2019 pace. Business-sector productivity averaged 2.5%, against a 2.1% average since 1960. Fitch attributes the speed-up to faster total factor productivity and more capital per worker. It says it is too early to call AI the main driver and points to a possible "J-curve", where firms invest heavily before new technology pays off. On jobs, Fitch finds no fall in aggregate US employment and no surge in layoffs. Secondary coverage cites its estimate that AI's net effect on employment this year is close to zero, a reduction of under 0.4% of the workforce. The strain is falling on new entrants: hiring rates are down and youth unemployment is up 1.2 percentage points since late 2022. That matches the entry-level squeeze in the Burning Glass report we covered yesterday. For business leaders, a major ratings agency is now saying the macro productivity gains are real, but that AI is not yet clearly what's producing them.

Sources: Anadolu Agency, Hellenic Shipping News (Fitch text), IndexBox, Crypto Briefing

2. Atlassian's first AI SDLC Index: AI now writes 70% of merged code, and the bottleneck has moved to human review

Atlassian published the first edition of its AI SDLC Index on 18 November. It draws on telemetry from more than 340,000 engineers at over 500 organisations, plus developer surveys from 567 companies. Atlassian says AI-generated code rose from 49% of merged code in April 2026 to 70% in October, and AI spend per unit of output fell about 27%. Cheaper code hasn't cleared the pipeline, though. Human pull-request reviews per reviewer rose from 12.8 a month in January to 22.3 in October. Median time to first human review roughly triples, from 31 minutes to 98, when more than 80% of a PR is AI-written. CI and review waits are the largest sources of friction, at about 86 hours a year each. Developers' rating of incident handling also slipped, and analysis published this week by diginomica and SD Times put that dip at the centre of the debate. This is the largest telemetry-based evidence yet for the review bottleneck behind products like Anthropic's Claude Ship. The productivity gain now depends on review, CI and planning, not on writing code faster. Atlassian plans to update the index quarterly.

Sources: Atlassian (Inside Atlassian), SD Times, Techzine Global, diginomica

3. Anthropic lets MCP servers ship skills to Claude and makes long-running Tasks and mid-task Elicitation generally available

On 24 November Anthropic put Skills over MCP into public beta. MCP servers can now publish skills, the packaged instructions for how to use their tools, and Claude loads them from the server without a separate install, so they update in place. Two features also reached general availability. Tasks let Claude hand a long-running job to an MCP server, keep working, and collect the result later. Elicitation lets a server pause and ask the user for input through a form or link, then pick up where it left off. Skills over MCP is on by default for Pro, Max and Ultra users, needs an admin to switch it on for Team, and is "coming soon" for Enterprise. Tasks and Elicitation are rolling out to Team and Enterprise. Because these are part of the open MCP spec, a server that supports them should work in other compatible clients too, though the MCP project still lists Tasks as beta and host support varies. For businesses, this means the vendors behind internal tools can now ship the know-how for using them along with the connector. That makes agents easier to roll out, but it also gives IT a new supply chain to vet, and coverage notes Anthropic hasn't said how server-shipped skills get reviewed or approved.

Sources: Claude (Anthropic), Tech Times, SmartScope, AlphaSignal, Sulat AI & Tech News

4. Automation Anywhere closes its Boost.ai deal, connecting voice and chat agents to the back-office automations that finish the work

Automation Anywhere said on 24 November that it had completed its purchase of Boost.ai, the Norwegian conversational and voice AI vendor, which it agreed to buy from Nordic Capital on 7 October. Terms were not disclosed. Boost.ai sells mainly to banks, insurers and telecoms. It reports more than 650 deployments, works in over 36 languages, and says its agents resolve more than 90% of requests in production. Automation Anywhere plans to put Boost.ai in front of its own process-automation platform, which it says runs nearly half a billion agent and automation executions a year, so that a customer's call or chat triggers the back-end steps to resolve it rather than only answering questions. The deal follows its 2025 purchase of Aisera and moves the former RPA vendor into the customer-service agent market where Sierra, Salesforce and Zendesk compete. A customer-service bot is only useful if it can update the policy, issue the refund or change the account, and this deal brings the conversation layer and the automation behind it under one vendor. Customers should still check which integrations actually ship, since coverage describes much of the combined product as a roadmap.

Sources: The Next Web, Unite.AI, Pulse 2.0, CXOToday, Automation Anywhere (Oct deal release)

5. Adobe: AI-referred visits to US retail sites are up 127% this holiday season, and they now convert better than other traffic

Adobe Analytics data released on 25 November shows US shoppers spent $82.5 billion online from 1 to 22 November, up 9.1% on last year and well ahead of Adobe's 6.7% full-season forecast. Visits to retail sites that start from a link in an AI chat or AI browser rose 127% year on year, close to the 130% Adobe forecast for the whole season. Adobe expects the jump to peak at about 159% on Thanksgiving, 95% on Black Friday and 88% on Cyber Monday. Adobe also says AI-referred visitors have flipped from being worth less than half as much as other visitors to being worth nearly twice as much. Salesforce separately expects AI agents, including retailers' own shopper agents, to influence about a fifth of Black Friday online sales. These figures count clicks, not purchases made inside AI tools. Still, AI assistants are now a sales channel that retailers and marketers have to optimise for, not an experiment.

Sources: Digital Commerce 360, TechRadar Pro, Ecommerce Paradise, Shashi.co
