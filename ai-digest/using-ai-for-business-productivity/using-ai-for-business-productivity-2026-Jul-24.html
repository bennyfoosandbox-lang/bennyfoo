1. Brown & Brown deploys Claude to 23,000 employees, reports 2x–8x developer productivity gains

Insurance brokerage Brown & Brown announced the next phase of an enterprise-wide "AI-first" transformation, enlisting Anthropic, McKinsey and Accenture to scale Claude and Claude Code across its 23,000 teammates. Early Claude Code pilots delivered striking numbers: developer productivity gains of roughly 2x to 8x, with work that once took days now finished in hours; an estimated 80–90% cut in analysis and troubleshooting time on certain workflows; and 80% of participating teammates rating the tool's value a perfect 5 out of 5. The company is also standing up a "value management office" to track adoption, business impact, ROI and controls as AI scales — a governance-first posture increasingly common among large enterprises moving past the pilot phase. It's one of the more concrete, numbers-backed case studies yet of agentic coding tools translating into measurable productivity at enterprise scale.

Sources: StockTitan, citybiz, QuiverQuant

2. 92% of CFOs feel pressure to prove AI ROI — but governance is falling further behind

A new Avalara survey of 1,505 CFOs and senior finance executives found that 92% feel personal pressure to prove AI investments are delivering returns, even as the governance needed to support that AI lags badly behind adoption. Half of respondents said their AI agents have produced only limited measurable ROI so far, 30% haven't updated internal controls in the past year, and just 28% require documented audit logs showing how an AI agent reached a decision. Accountability is similarly thin: 23% said responsibility for an AI mishap would fall to no one or be unclear, and 76% admit they lack the in-house expertise to fully understand how their AI actually operates. The findings sharpen a theme running through recent coverage — AI ROI pressure and governance maturity are diverging just as agentic tools get real budget and real authority inside finance functions.

Sources: ESG Dive / CFO Dive, ETCFO, CIOL

3. Adecco Group: AI is redesigning work faster than it's eliminating jobs — but most firms haven't caught up

The Adecco Group, working with advisory firm Altermind, published a global whitepaper — "On the Threshold: AI, the Future of Work and the Rise of Hybrid Labour Markets" — arguing that AI is changing job tasks and skill requirements faster than it's eliminating roles, and has contributed to 1.9 million new AI-related jobs since 2022. Yet the study finds only 18% of US firms and 20% of European firms have actually integrated AI into core workflows at scale, a wide gap between ambition and execution that leaves most productivity gains still unrealized. Adecco proposes "hybrid workforce orchestration" — deliberately redesigning work so people, AI agents, automation and physical AI each handle the tasks they're best suited for — as the operating model it expects to define the next decade of work, while noting 57% of workers want more AI training than they're currently getting. It's a useful counterweight to this week's layoff headlines: the constraint on AI productivity looks less like the technology itself and more like how few organizations have actually redesigned work around it.

Sources: PR Newswire, Staffing Industry Analysts, Adecco Group

4. Databricks and Microsoft extend their enterprise AI alliance into the 2030s

Databricks and Microsoft extended their strategic partnership into the next decade, deepening a nearly ten-year-old alliance aimed at grounding enterprise AI agents in companies' own business data rather than generic models. Under the expanded deal, Databricks will run its own core operations on Azure Databricks, adopt Microsoft's next-generation Azure Cobalt chips (up to 50% faster), and further integrate its Genie and Unity AI Gateway tools with Microsoft 365, Teams, Copilot, Power BI and Purview. Both companies frame the move as tackling what they call the biggest barrier to enterprise AI adoption: connecting models to trusted organizational knowledge while keeping governance and cost under control — the same friction point showing up across other governance-gap stories this week. Existing joint customers cited include Banco Bradesco, Electrolux, SMBC and Unilever, underscoring how much production enterprise AI workload already runs on this pairing.

Sources: Mexico Business News, ChannelLife Australia

5. A back-office AI case study with real before/after numbers: Yiren Digital cuts manual work ~45%, lifts tickets-per-staffer 47%

Chinese fintech Yiren Digital disclosed concrete efficiency metrics from its "All-in-AI" strategy: AI agents now handle 81% of eligible Day-1 asset-recovery tickets, up from 50% in 2024; manual intervention in that workflow fell from 45.0% to 24.9%; and tickets handled per staff member rose about 47%, from 358 to 525 a month. The gains come from MagiCube 2.0, a multi-agent platform reused across six enterprise functions including customer service, risk and compliance, alongside a voice agent processing roughly 1,500 hours of speech daily and a marketing platform executing over 1,700 tasks a day. It's a smaller, single-company case study rather than an industry survey, but it's a rare instance of a business publishing hard before/after productivity numbers for agentic AI in a back-office operation, rather than self-reported sentiment.

Sources: StockTitan, PR Newswire, Yahoo Finance UK
