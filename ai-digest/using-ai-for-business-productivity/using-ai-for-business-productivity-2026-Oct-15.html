1. Workday Rising: Autopilot agents, a headless agent platform and a C1.ai identity acquisition

At Workday Rising 2026 the company made Sana the home screen of its platform and introduced Autopilot Agents that work in the background across hiring, payroll, accounting and compliance, flagging issues and acting within rules each organization sets. Its Trusted Agent Platform now allows headless access, so customer-built agents can use Workday capabilities under the same permissions, approvals and audit trails, and Workday services are being surfaced inside Claude, Gemini Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Slack. Decision Intelligence is now available, Work Intelligence (task-level automate/assist/human analysis on a 75,000-skill graph) is in early access, and Workday agreed to buy agentic identity-management firm C1.ai to control what people and agents can reach, with closing expected by the end of January 2027. A same-day Accenture partnership aims to help customers deploy Workday agents faster.

Sources: India Today, The Hindu BusinessLine

2. Glean: AI saves workers about 11 hours a week, yet 36% hide how much it helps

Glean's survey of 6,000 mostly computer-based workers in the U.S., U.K. and Australia puts AI's time savings at roughly 11 hours a week, over a quarter of the workweek, while arguing that missing context is eating into those gains. A Fortune commentary drawing on the data highlights a behavioral twist: 36% of workers who say AI helped them hid how much, fearing that visible speed-ups could shrink their roles. The author points to Norway's October 2 national AI agreement and suggests employer pledges, such as no pay or job losses during a trial and a say in how saved time is used, as ways to get workers to share their shortcuts. For productivity programs, the finding is that measured gains depend on trust as much as tooling.

Sources: Fortune, Glean report

3. Oyster: 95% of companies have staff doing AI work their job descriptions never mention

Oyster's 2026 Global AI Talent Report, based on a survey of 1,000+ HR leaders and 15,000+ job descriptions, finds AI use has outrun job design: 95% of HR professionals say employees use AI daily in roles where it isn't in the description. Of companies that added AI requirements to existing roles, 62% changed neither title nor pay and 46% never told the people affected; 53% prefer training existing staff over hiring. AI skill requirements in roles hired through Oyster tripled from 5% in 2022 to 15%, with nine in ten not naming AI in the title, and 21% of HR pros say an AI tool missed something important in a high-stakes case such as a termination or leave request. The report shows that productivity gains are being absorbed informally, with no pay, title or hiring process catching up.

Sources: Oyster / Business Wire

4. Duvo raises $32M to let employees and AI agents run business processes together

Duvo, founded in 2025 by a team associated with online grocer Rohlik Group, raised a $32 million Series A led by Balderton Capital, bringing total funding to $48 million. Its Enterprise Execution OS sits on top of existing business software so teams and AI agents can run operational workflows jointly, with employee oversight built in. The company has about 60 staff and plans to spend the money on product development, customer support and sales. The round is part of a busy day of agent funding that also included Zenzap's $33 million for agents inside workplace chat and Labelbox's $100 million-plus Series E for enterprise managed agents, signalling investor appetite for agents that show operational returns.

Sources: Pulse 2.0, CityBiz, Ventureburn, Dealroom, Calcalist (Zenzap), PR Newswire (Labelbox)

5. SS&C survey: 87% of enterprises name productivity their top AI goal, but only 43% see consistent governance

SS&C polled 148 financial-services and healthcare leaders at its Deliver conference in mid-September and found employee productivity is the leading AI goal (87%), ahead of client experience (53%), lower operating costs (44%) and reduced risk (43%). Agents are moving into core operations: 48% expect them in more than a quarter of workflows within three years, including 18% who expect more than half. Yet just 43% say governance is applied consistently across the business, two-thirds name governance and legacy-system integration as top scaling priorities, and one in five call measuring AI performance and ROI critical. The sample is small and conference-based, and the release promotes SS&C's own WorkHQ platform, so treat it as directional.

Sources: SS&C / Business Wire
