1. Atlassian's Rovo AI push pays off in a record Q4

Atlassian's fiscal Q4 2026 earnings, reported August 6, showed revenue up 28% year-over-year to $1.77 billion and cloud revenue up 31% to $1.2 billion, beating Wall Street estimates on both lines and sending shares up roughly 35% in the aftermath. The company credited its Rovo AI assistant, now used by more than 80% of the Fortune 500, with helping drive the beat: Rovo-assisted actions rose 50% sequentially, and customers who adopt Rovo grow annual recurring revenue at more than twice the rate of those who don't. Atlassian pointed to its "Teamwork Graph" — a web of more than 200 billion objects and connections spanning Jira, Confluence and its other products — as the real differentiator, saying agents grounded in that context deliver up to 44% more accurate answers while using 48% fewer tokens than ungrounded queries. The quarter also brought record enterprise deal activity, with customers spending at least $3 million a year growing more than 50% and the $5 million-plus cohort growing more than 70%. For a company built on internal collaboration tools, the results are an early sign that grounding AI agents in an organization's own work graph, not just a general-purpose model, is what turns AI features into paid upgrades.

Sources: The Globe and Mail, Investing.com, StockTitan (8-K)

2. Axon's AI Era Plan revenue jumps nearly 700%

Axon Enterprise's Q2 2026 earnings, reported August 5, showed record revenue of $904 million, up 35% year-over-year and its tenth straight quarter of growth above 30%, with the company raising full-year guidance to 32%-34% growth. The standout was Axon's AI Era Plan, a software bundle that wraps generative-AI report writing and other automation into its public-safety platform: revenue from the plan grew nearly 700% year-over-year and helped push total software and services revenue up 36% to $398 million. Annual recurring revenue climbed 39% to $1.6 billion with net revenue retention of 126%, while Axon's Platform Solutions segment — including its Dedrone counter-drone business — grew 123% to $150 million, topping $100 million in Dedrone revenue for the first time. Axon's results add another concrete public-company data point for how bundling AI-native features directly into existing software, rather than selling AI as a bolt-on, can accelerate both adoption and pricing power. It's a pattern echoed elsewhere this summer at companies like Shopify and Uber, where AI-linked software revenue is now growing multiples faster than the core business.

Sources: PR Newswire, BigGo Finance, Yahoo Finance

3. Yellow.ai to go public in a $550M SPAC merger, betting on pure-play enterprise agentic AI

Yellow.ai, which builds AI agents for enterprise customer service and support automation, announced August 3 that it will go public via a $550 million merger with Bluerock Acquisition Corp., planning to trade on Nasdaq under the ticker "YAI" once the deal closes. The company says it serves more than 650 enterprise clients and generated more than $34 million in unaudited revenue in its last fiscal year, built on a multi-LLM architecture continuously trained on more than 16 billion conversations annually. Founded in 2016 and backed by more than $100 million from investors including Lightspeed, Salesforce Ventures and Sapphire Ventures, Yellow.ai is framing the listing as creating the first U.S.-publicly-listed pure-play enterprise agentic AI platform, a distinction meant to set it apart from broader horizontal AI vendors. The move lands amid a wave of AI-infrastructure consolidation and public-market entries this summer, and gives enterprise buyers a rare, publicly-reported financial window into how a mid-sized agent vendor's revenue and unit economics actually look. Whether the market treats it as a bellwether for enterprise-agent economics, or a cautionary tale, will likely shape how other agent startups think about going public versus staying private longer.

Sources: PR Newswire, Yahoo Finance, AiThority

4. Plug and Play survey: enterprise AI has crossed into production, not into proof

A new Plug and Play survey of large enterprises, reported August 6, found that 74% of the world's largest companies now run at least one AI system in production, but half of those companies still can't consistently prove that it delivers business value. Respondents cited weak data foundations as the top barrier to scaling AI (71%), followed by governance friction (53%) and legacy-system integration (26%), and only 37% of enterprises running AI in production do so within a single business function, meaning most deployments still aren't coordinated across the organization. The survey argues the "plug and play" era of AI adoption is over: enterprises have cleared the technical bar of getting a model into a live workflow, but most still lack the pre-established baselines needed to attribute any resulting revenue or cost change to AI specifically. That distinction matters for productivity claims industry-wide, since it means widely-cited "AI is running in production" statistics can overstate how much verified business value is actually being captured. The findings echo, and sharpen with a fresh number, a theme that has run through this digest all summer: deployment is outpacing measurement almost everywhere enterprises look.

Sources: Forbes, Yahoo Finance UK, Efficiently Connected

5. Salesforce puts Agentforce in front of 9.2 million soldiers, veterans and military families

The U.S. Army's Human Resources Command announced August 5 that it has deployed Salesforce's Agentforce, through partner Missionforce National Security, to provide round-the-clock AI-powered support to roughly 9.2 million soldiers, veterans, civilian staff and military families. Operating inside Salesforce's Impact Level 5 (IL5)-authorized government cloud, the agents answer routine questions, summarize case histories and surface relevant policy and career information from approved Army sources, while routing anything involving benefits decisions or other sensitive matters to human HRC specialists who retain final authority. Army HRC is the first organization within the Department of War to deploy the newly IL5-authorized version of Agentforce for workloads touching controlled unclassified information, making this one of the largest and most sensitive AI-agent rollouts any Salesforce customer has announced to date. For enterprises watching how far agentic AI can be trusted with real caseloads, the deployment is a notable data point: a security-cleared, audited agent platform handling millions of routine support interactions while explicitly keeping humans in the loop for higher-stakes decisions. It also underscores how much of this year's enterprise-agent growth is coming from public-sector and mission-critical use cases, not just customer-facing commercial chatbots.

Sources: Salesforce Newsroom, Business Wire, StockTitan
