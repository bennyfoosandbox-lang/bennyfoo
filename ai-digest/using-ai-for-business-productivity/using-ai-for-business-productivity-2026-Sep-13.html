1. AI agents ran nearly the entire PaperCut attack campaign, breaching 395 organizations in hours

A financially motivated, Russian-speaking threat actor handed most of a live intrusion campaign to autonomous AI agents built on OpenAI's Codex harness paired with a DeepSeek model, according to a GreyNoise investigation published this week. After building and testing exploits for two PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078) in a private lab, the operator turned hundreds of coordinating agents loose on the open internet; they went from an empty workspace to first remote-code execution against a real victim in under four hours, first domain-admin compromise two hours after that, and once fully live compromised at least 11 organizations in 26 seconds, ultimately breaching at least 440 PaperCut instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries. Education institutions bore the brunt (204 victims), and the agents didn't reliably respect the attacker's own do-not-target country list, a reminder of how unevenly current agent frameworks follow instructions even when doing so is in the operator's own interest. For business-productivity teams evaluating agent frameworks internally, this is a live demonstration that the same orchestration patterns — multi-agent handoffs, tool use, minimal human oversight — that make agents useful for back-office work make them equally capable of running a full offensive campaign end-to-end, at a speed no human security team can match.

Sources: GreyNoise, The Hacker News, The Register, Help Net Security, BleepingComputer

2. OpenAI's new Data agent turns ChatGPT Work into a plain-language BI tool

OpenAI introduced a Data agent inside ChatGPT Work on September 10, a plugin that connects to a company's own data stores — Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift, MongoDB, ClickHouse and Datadog among them, plus files in Google Drive and SharePoint — and lets employees ask plain-language questions that come back as findings, interactive dashboards and suggested next steps, without writing a query or opening a separate analytics tool. Users can direct and refine the analysis conversationally, ask the agent to surface anomalies or trends, and have it share results directly to Slack or email, or hand off user-approved follow-up actions to connected tools. It ships alongside OpenAI's new public-beta Agents API, which puts the Codex agent harness behind a single API call for developers building their own workflows. Together the two releases push ChatGPT further from a chat assistant toward a front end for a company's actual operational data — the same "connect once, act everywhere" bet Microsoft, Salesforce and Snowflake are all racing to make with their own agent platforms.

Sources: Unite.AI, Seeking Alpha, XDA Developers, Crypto Briefing

3. Salesforce ships "job-ready" Agentforce agents built for high-value work across sales, service and the back office

Salesforce on September 11 unveiled a new portfolio of what it calls "job-ready" Agentforce agents — pre-built for specific roles across sales, service, commerce, employee experience and back-office functions rather than general-purpose assistants — designed to pursue goals over time, learn new skills, collaborate with other agents, and improve continuously while staying wired into a company's existing Customer 360 data and processes. The launch comes as Salesforce says it has delivered 7 billion "Agentic Work Units" across Agentforce and Slack over the past two years, including 3.2 billion in the second quarter alone, and follows new AI Skills in Agentforce Coworker that let an employee teach the agent a task once and scale that know-how across the workforce, with general availability slated for October. It builds on Salesforce's early-September move to bundle Agentforce Sales, Service and Industries into simplified Core, Advanced and Max editions, consolidating AI, security, analytics and support into a single purchase. For enterprises already on Salesforce, the pitch is that "job-ready" agents shortcut the build-your-own-agent effort that has stalled many agentic AI pilots industry-wide.

Sources: Salesforce, MarketScreener, Salesforce (editions)

4. Positron raises $875M to challenge Nvidia's HBM-based inference chips with commodity memory

Inference-chip startup Positron AI raised $875 million across a $375 million Series C and up to $500 million Series C-1, valuing the Reno, Nevada company at $5 billion post-money, to bring its Asimov chip to market. Rather than competing with Nvidia on raw compute or high-bandwidth memory, Asimov pairs Positron's architecture with 288GB to 2,304GB of commodity LPDDR5X memory per chip, aiming to sidestep the HBM supply constraints and packaging bottlenecks that have driven up inference costs industry-wide; it's slated to tape out on TSMC's N3P process by the end of 2026, with production targeted for the second half of 2027. Proceeds will also fund a 2-megawatt engineering data center and the ramp of Titan, a system that bundles four to eight Asimov chips into a single node. For businesses budgeting for AI adoption, memory-first inference silicon like this is a direct bet on lowering the per-token cost of running the agents and copilots enterprises are now deploying at scale — the same cost pressure driving software-side efforts like Snowflake's model-routing gateway.

Sources: Yahoo Finance, PR Newswire, Converge Digest, Tech Times

5. Sakana AI splits its Fugu router into Max and Ultra v2, cutting orchestrated AI costs up to 60%

Sakana AI released two new orchestration models, Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2, that sit in front of a pool of open-weight and specialist LLMs behind a single OpenAI-compatible API, using a lightweight roughly 0.6-billion-parameter coordinator called TRINITY to classify each incoming request and route it to one to three worker models playing Thinker, Worker or Verifier roles before merging their outputs into one response. Fugu Max targets cost-performance at $2/$6 per million input/output tokens, while Fugu Ultra v2 draws on a deeper expert pool for complex reasoning and coding work at $5/$30 per million tokens; Sakana says the split cuts orchestrated-inference costs by up to 60% compared with routing every query to a single frontier model. The routing logic itself is learned rather than hand-coded, so it generalizes across coding, math and knowledge tasks instead of following static rules written by an engineer. It's the latest entrant, alongside Snowflake's Cortex AI Gateway and similar tools, in a fast-forming category betting that most enterprise AI spend is going to models more powerful — and expensive — than a given task actually requires.

Sources: MarkTechPost, AlphaSignal, Requesty, AiCybr
