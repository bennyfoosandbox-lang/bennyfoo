1. Uber puts OpenAI's ChatGPT Work in front of all 30,000 employees

Uber and OpenAI announced on 22 October that ChatGPT Work, the agent inside ChatGPT Enterprise, will go to Uber's entire global workforce of more than 30,000 people after a pilot of 1,000. It handles multi-step tasks such as research, meeting briefs, presentations and data analysis, and connects to Gmail, Google Drive, Calendar and Slack. Uber is backing it with training, approved integrations and an internal AI skills library, and says about 80% of early pilot users were non-engineers; engineers stay on Codex. Neither company disclosed cost or quantified productivity gains, and coverage notes the rollout comes seven weeks after Uber announced roughly 3,300 job cuts, though nothing links the two.

Sources: Tech Times UK, Adgully, Storyboard18, Forkast, Tekedia

2. Volkswagen rolls Microsoft Copilot out to 330,000 employees, with 26,000 active agents

Volkswagen Group says Microsoft Copilot is now deployed to more than 330,000 employees, with over 300,000 using Copilot Chat each month and more than 26,000 agents active in the preceding month. Use spans engineering, production, procurement and sales, and the two companies plan to expand Volkswagen's Intelligent Data Foundation on Microsoft Fabric so people and agents can find trusted data. It is one of the largest single-company Copilot rollouts reported, but the announcement gives no figures for time saved, output or cost, so the numbers measure reach rather than verified business value.

Sources: Konsulteer

3. Cognizant launches AI Nexus to meter, govern and prove the return on AI spend

Cognizant introduced AI Nexus, a modular multi-model layer with five parts: Engage and Orchestrate for where work happens, and Reveal, Coach and Route for governing it. Reveal maps every token, tool and dollar to the people and outcomes behind it, while Route sends each request to the model with the right balance of quality, cost and control. The pitch rests on Cognizant's own survey of 250 FinOps leaders, where 72% exceeded their AI budget in the past year and 97% had an agent session escalate beyond expected cost or activity. It reflects a shift in enterprise AI from choosing a model to proving measurable value; the capabilities described are vendor claims.

Sources: SMEStreet

4. Zoom bets on a Unified Context Layer to turn conversations into completed work

At Zoomtopia, Zoom unveiled a Unified Context Layer that combines meetings, calls, chats, contact-centre data, documents and connected apps so employees and AI agents can act on shared context. It is exposed through APIs and MCP, with integrations for Microsoft 365 Copilot, Claude, OpenAI Codex and Meta Muse, and Gemini planned. Agent Teams are available now; Centralised AI Management is due in December 2026 and custom AI guardrails in Q1 2027. Zoom also added 15 AI developer services, and a customer example projects $2.7 million in savings over five years, an internal estimate rather than an audited result.

Sources: Express Computer

5. Infosys: AI is 10.5% of revenue, yet growth guidance is cut as clients demand AI-driven productivity

In its Q2 FY27 results on 23 October, Infosys said AI accounted for 10.5% of revenue, up from 8.2% in the prior quarter, with AI revenue up 29% sequentially. Yet it lowered FY27 constant-currency growth guidance to 1-2% from 1.5-3%, citing softer demand, competition, delivery-mix changes and clients expecting productivity gains from existing contracts. The company still added a net 4,480 employees and posted $5.2 billion in large-deal value. It is a clear example of the productivity paradox from the supplier side: AI creates new work while compressing the price of traditional services.

Sources: DQ India
