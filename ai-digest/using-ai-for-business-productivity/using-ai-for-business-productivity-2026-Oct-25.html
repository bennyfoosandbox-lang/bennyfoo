1. Walmart gives AI agents their own profiles, spending limits and an MCP gateway

Walmart announced Agent Profiles and an Agent Gateway on 21 October, giving outside AI assistants a governed way to shop its catalogue on a customer's behalf. Shoppers link each agent to their account, approve every purchase by default or allow spending up to a preset amount (examples cited are $50, $100 and $200), and agents never see card numbers or change payment methods. The Gateway is MCP-based and starts as a beta for companies and developers, with profiles due for customers within about a month. For business productivity it is a concrete template for delegating transactions to agents with per-agent identity, caps and revocation, though reporting notes the limit is per purchase rather than a weekly or monthly budget.

Sources: Digital Commerce 360, The Next Web, PPC Land, The Shelby Report

2. Fortune: as AI productivity gains arrive, bosses ask what employees get back

A 19 October Fortune report (from earlier this week) finds leaders at PepsiCo, Indeed, Dropbox and Bristol Myers Squibb rethinking who should benefit from AI-driven time savings. Indeed says AI helps its engineers write 50% more code and saves employees about six hours a week, which it plans to redirect to more creative work. Bristol Myers Squibb's chief people officer is weighing how much time freed from tedious tasks should return to staff as well-being, while PepsiCo's transformation lead argues against demanding a financial case for every AI use. The piece also cites Quantum Workplace data that frequent AI users report higher burnout, a reminder that reported savings do not automatically become value for workers or firms.

Sources: Fortune, DNyuz (Fortune repost)

3. Grant Thornton: CFO profit optimism hits a record, and 84% say AI is meeting expectations

Grant Thornton's Q3 2026 CFO Survey (from late September, so older than the 24-hour window) polled 229 US finance leaders between 7 and 21 August. A record 80% expect net profit to rise over the next 12 months, and 35% expect growth above 10%, even though only 46% are optimistic about the US economy. Some 84% say their AI investments are meeting or exceeding expectations, with productivity gains driving much of the return, and a Grant Thornton leader credits AI productivity for part of the confidence. The firm's own caveat is that the next hurdle is turning AI capability into business outcomes, with only about half of CFOs confident they will hit their technology goals.

Sources: Grant Thornton press release, Grant Thornton survey report, CPA Practice Advisor, Accounting Today

4. Microsoft puts a prompt box on the Copilot button in Word, Excel and PowerPoint

Microsoft Message Center item MC1470885 (published 11 September, so older than the 24-hour window) says users will be able to type or dictate requests straight from the Copilot button in Word, Excel and PowerPoint, without opening the Copilot pane. The input can reference Microsoft 365 content, and Word and PowerPoint show a contextual input when content is selected. It rolls out worldwide from September to December 2026 on Windows, macOS and web for Microsoft 365 Copilot-licensed users, and needs no admin action beyond updated training and documentation. It is a small change, but removing the extra click to reach the assistant is the kind of friction reduction that tends to drive everyday usage.

Sources: Microsoft Message Center MC1470885, M365 Admin (Handsontek), Microsoft Support: Copilot Dynamic Action Button

5. Small businesses are adopting AI fast, but 70% say they need more training

Thryv's 2026 AI and Small Business Adoption Survey (published in late June, so the oldest item here and a vendor-sponsored study) reports US small-business AI adoption at 66%, up from 55% a year earlier. Of the 561 owners surveyed, 70% say AI raised revenue and 55% say it cut costs, and 86% are at least somewhat comfortable with it. Yet seven in ten say they need more training to use it productively, and about a third turn to tools like ChatGPT to learn. It is included because fresher small-business data was thin this run; it adds a practical point that skills, not access, are the bottleneck.

Sources: Business Wire (Thryv), CPA Practice Advisor, Chain Store Age
