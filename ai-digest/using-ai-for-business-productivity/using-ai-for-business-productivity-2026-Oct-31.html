1. Anthropic's IPO filing puts numbers on enterprise stickiness

Anthropic's public S-1, filed around 26 October, reports that as of 30 September it had more than 11,900 customers spending at least $100,000 a year, over 1,900 above $1 million and about 250 above $10 million. The filing also shows net revenue retention of roughly 900%, driven by customers expanding usage across their organisations, and says more than half of the $100,000-plus customers now use both the Claude platform and a first-party app such as Claude Code or Cowork. For business-productivity buyers it is the clearest disclosed evidence yet that AI spend inside firms compounds once it spreads. Anthropic notes the metrics are unaudited, and one PitchBook analyst expects the retention figure to fall as the base grows. (From earlier this week; the S-1 itself was first covered here through leaks, but these customer-tier figures are new.)

Sources: TechCrunch, SaaStr, Morningstar

2. Replit moves most of its AI spend to OpenAI on a 30%-plus discount

The Information reported that Replit shifted most of its model spend from Anthropic to OpenAI under a deal said to be worth more than $100 million a year, after OpenAI offered over 30% off and Anthropic reportedly would not extend an earlier discount without a larger commitment. Replit's CEO says OpenAI is now the preferred partner for Replit Agent while the company stays model-agnostic, and a source told TechCrunch the Anthropic relationship continues. The episode shows how price and defaults, not just model quality, are now steering enterprise AI spend. Contract size and the scale of the migration are unconfirmed. (Reported 17–25 October.)

Sources: TechCrunch, RuntimeWire, TechRepublic

3. ServiceNow's Flow puts an AI service desk inside Slack and Teams

ServiceNow launched Flow, a standalone conversational service desk that employees reach through Slack, Microsoft Teams, email or a web app. The company says teams can be live in about a day by connecting single sign-on, a chat tool and knowledge sources; Flow resolves routine requests and hands complex or sensitive ones to a person. New customers can buy it without any existing ServiceNow platform, while change management and CMDB-driven work stay on the full ITSM product. ServiceNow expects a 40% cut in ticket volume, which is a vendor forecast rather than a measured result. (Launched 1 October; not previously covered here.)

Sources: SiliconANGLE, CIO, ServiceNow Newsroom

4. Island turns its enterprise browser into a control point for AI agents

Island, which raised $400 million at a $6.4 billion valuation in September, has released an Enterprise Browser for Agents: a headless version of its browser that exposes tools to AI agents through an MCP server running inside it. The pitch is that the same data-protection, access policy and audit rules that govern employees apply to agents from any vendor, with permissions, optional human approval and interception of prompt-injection attempts. It joins a crowded field of agent-governance launches, and the security claims come from Island's own materials with no independent testing yet. (Product release around 29 October; funding from 24 September.)

Sources: Island, The Next Web, RuntimeWire

5. Cresta's Conductor lets developers build and test customer-facing agents in plain language

Cresta has launched Conductor, a developer-focused engine for building and tuning customer-facing AI agents. Developers describe what they want in natural language, Conductor drafts a blueprint from existing documentation and conversation data for approval, then tests the agent against simulated customers and suggests fixes before launch. After launch it reviews transcripts to find root causes and produce a prioritised fix list. The sources reviewed do not confirm the exact launch date, so treat the timing as unverified.

Sources: TechIntelPro, Pulse
