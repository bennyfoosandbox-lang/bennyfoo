1. Hitachi claims up to 240x productivity gains from its new Agentic AI Integration Platform

Hitachi announced on July 24 that it will fully apply AI agents across the entire system-development lifecycle — from requirements definition through design, coding, testing and operations — via its new "Agentic AI Integration Platform." Internal pilots showed productivity gains of roughly 200x from design through testing, and up to 240x in requirements-definition work specifically, driven by task-specific agents plus a persistent "enterprise context" knowledge base that captures customer intent and operational know-how across a project's life. The platform is model-agnostic, letting teams draw on Anthropic, Google Cloud and OpenAI models alongside Hitachi's own generative-AI framework and GlobalLogic's VelocityAI. Hitachi is targeting the platform first at large, mission-critical system-integration projects in finance, public services, energy and railways — sectors with the deepest legacy-system backlogs and the most to gain from compressing months of requirements work into days.

Sources: BigGo Finance, IBTimes JP, Europe Says

2. The AI ROI plateau holds for a second straight year — even as production quality climbs

Domino Data Lab's fifth annual Enterprise AI Report, released July 21 and based on a survey of 639 senior enterprise AI leaders, found that 57% of enterprises still say their AI ROI fails to outpace investment — identical to last year's figure. That's despite a jump in production quality: 93% now report improved production capability, up from 88% in 2025. The report frames this as a "last-mile gap" between models that work well in production and the business users who are supposed to extract value from them, suggesting the bottleneck has shifted from technical readiness to organizational adoption. Two years running of a flat ROI number, paired with rising technical competence, is one of the clearest single data points yet that the productivity payoff depends on workflow redesign, not further model improvement.

Sources: PR Newswire, Carrier Management, Telecom Review Americas

3. Kyndryl: AI now runs in 57% of enterprise core processes, but workforce readiness is sliding backward

Kyndryl's 2026 People Readiness Report finds AI now embedded in or broadly deployed across 57% of enterprise core processes, up sharply from 35% a year ago, with 77% of firms having scaled generative AI across multiple functions. Yet only 23% of business leaders believe their workforce is fully prepared for AI — a six-point drop from 2025 — and just 11% of organizations have hit both of their primary AI objectives. 79% agree the pace of AI development is outrunning their ability to adapt governance and operating models, and 52% say finding staff with the right AI competencies has gotten harder over the past year. A "Pacesetters" cohort — the 9% of firms that redesigned roles, ran real change management and invested in readiness — are 1.5x more likely to see AI-linked revenue growth, underscoring that the gap is a people problem more than a technology one.

Sources: MarketScale, Network World, PR Newswire

4. Amazon cuts jobs in its AGI unit even as it commits $200B to AI infrastructure

Amazon confirmed layoffs this week within its Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) organization, cutting post-training and model-customization roles even as the company pours roughly $200 billion into AI infrastructure. The company hasn't disclosed headcount but confirmed the cuts sit within AGI's research side, part of a broader pattern that has now eliminated more than 30,000 jobs since October 2025. Notably, the specific functions being cut — post-training and model customization — overlap heavily with what Amazon's new Nova Forge product is designed to automate and sell to customers, suggesting the layoffs and the product launch reflect the same strategic pivot: away from in-house frontier research and toward productizing AI customization for enterprise customers, including a $1 billion program embedding AWS engineers with clients building agentic systems. It's a concrete example of AI reshaping headcount even inside the companies building the technology.

Sources: Tech Times, GeekWire, CNBC

5. The hidden cost of enterprise AI: 43% of firms report $2M+ in AI security-incident costs

A new WitnessAI report, "The Hidden Cost of Enterprise AI," finds that 43% of enterprise decision-makers experienced $2 million or more in costs tied to AI-related security incidents over the past year, with 21% saying their single worst incident alone cost $1 million or more. 86% of respondents had investigated at least one AI-related security or operational incident in the past 12 months. Despite that, adoption keeps outrunning governance: 70% are already using or piloting autonomous AI agents, but fewer than one in five (18%) say all their agents are formally inventoried and approved by security. 91% are concerned AI agents raise their financial risk exposure, yet 64% still believe the productivity value outweighs the risk — a bet that's paying off on paper while leaving most organizations unable to say exactly what their agents are doing.

Sources: IT Brief, PR Newswire, VMblog
