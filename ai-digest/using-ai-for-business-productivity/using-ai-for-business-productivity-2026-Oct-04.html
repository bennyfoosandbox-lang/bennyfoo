1. Perplexity pitches itself as the 'meta-router' for enterprise AI

Perplexity's Computer for Enterprise, unveiled at its Ask 2026 conference and analysed in a fresh Forkast piece, bets that buyers would rather manage a pool of models than pick one winner. It breaks an objective into subtasks and routes each to the best of roughly 20 models, runs sessions in isolated Firecracker microVM sandboxes, and offers 400+ connectors plus custom MCP connectors, with usage-based pricing from an organization-wide credit pool. Perplexity cites an internal 16,000-query study claiming $1.6M in labor savings, but those figures are vendor-reported and unaudited. The takeaway for business productivity buyers: as model prices converge, orchestration and anti-lock-in are becoming the product. (Note: the platform launch predates today; the analysis is new.)

Sources: Forkast, VentureBeat, Gend

2. ZoomInfo bundles agent orchestration into every contract after buying DoubleO.ai

ZoomInfo bought multi-agent orchestration startup DoubleO.ai on September 30 and launched Agent Teams inside GTM Studio the next day, with no new SKU or separate contract. The launch ships 50+ ready-made agents for revenue plays such as champion tracking and closed-won lookalikes, run through approval-gated Playbooks with audit trails and a credit estimate before each play. Terms of the deal were not disclosed, and reliability figures are vendor-reported only. It matters because it shows orchestration shifting from a premium product to a default platform feature, while analysts note the consumption pricing behind it is still unpublished.

Sources: Forkast, Beri

3. Microsoft's Roslansky to exit at year-end as Copilot becomes a workplace platform

Satya Nadella told employees on October 1 that Ryan Roslansky, who ran Microsoft 365 and Copilot product teams alongside his LinkedIn role, will leave at the end of 2026, citing the need to be in Redmond full time. Reporting lines are being redistributed: Sumit Chauhan and Lan Ye move under Charles Lamanna, design lead Jon Friedman under Copilot chief Jacob Andreou, and Dan Shapero stays LinkedIn CEO. The shuffle lands as Microsoft repositions Copilot around Home, Code and Autopilot, a persistent cloud agent with its own identity and memory. For business buyers it is a signal of leadership churn at the center of the largest workplace-AI suite, though Microsoft says direction is unchanged.

Sources: Konsulteer, DevX (Copilot Autopilot explained)

4. AI agents move into text messages, including for workplace tasks

TechCrunch's October 3 roundup catalogs a growing set of assistants you reach by iMessage, SMS, WhatsApp or Telegram instead of a dedicated app, including Poke, Folk, Iris and family-focused Fambot, which raised a $3.5M pre-seed. The pitch is zero-install delegation: text a task, and the agent works through the steps while keeping context from earlier conversations. Some target workplace use by plugging into team messaging for routine lookups and scheduling. It is an early, consumer-led channel, but it points to a lower-friction way for small businesses and individual contributors to adopt agents without a rollout.

Sources: TechCrunch, TechBuzz, Mezha
