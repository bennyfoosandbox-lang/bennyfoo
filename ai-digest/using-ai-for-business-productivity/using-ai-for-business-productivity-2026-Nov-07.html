1. Meta buys Conductor, the app that runs teams of coding agents in parallel

Meta has acquired Conductor, a startup whose Mac app lets developers run several AI coding agents at once, each in its own isolated copy of a codebase, then review and merge the results. Conductor does not ship its own model; it orchestrates tools such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor, and says more than 100,000 people use it after a $22 million Series A in March. The team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs, terms were not disclosed, and the hosted cloud service shuts down on 20 November. For business productivity, it shows the competitive edge moving from the model to the layer that manages many agents working side by side, and it leaves existing users with a short migration window.

Sources: Axios, SiliconANGLE, AI Weekly, RuntimeWire

2. OpenAI to acquire Pierre Computer and fold Code Storage into Codex

OpenAI announced on 4 November that it will acquire Pierre Computer, the company behind Code Storage and a set of open-source developer libraries. Pierre's Trees and Diffs components already power file navigation and code review inside the Codex app, and OpenAI plans to bring Code Storage into Codex itself. Coverage frames this as a push to own more of the software-delivery workflow and compete with GitHub, though one outlet reads the plan as narrower than a GitHub replacement. Financial terms were not disclosed and the deal still depends on customary closing conditions, following OpenAI's earlier purchase of Astral.

Sources: OpenAI, DevOps.com, The Stack, AI Weekly

3. Five9 agrees to buy Level AI for $325 million in cash

Five9 signed a definitive agreement on 5 November to acquire Level AI for about $325 million in cash, paid from its balance sheet, according to its SEC filings. Level AI's conversation-intelligence apps have been sold through the Five9 CX Marketplace since 2022, and Five9 says the purchase adds AI built on Level AI's own models, which it describes as cheaper and lower-latency than frontier models for customer conversations. The company expects to close by the end of its 2026 fiscal year. The deal is a sign that contact-centre vendors are buying specialised AI capability rather than renting it, as agent-driven service becomes a core productivity use case.

Sources: Five9 Form 8-K (SEC), Five9 Form 10-Q (SEC)

4. CrowdStrike ships an “AI Kill Switch” to stop rogue internal agents

At Fal.Con Europe on 3 November, CrowdStrike launched an AI Kill Switch inside Falcon Guardian that lets administrators halt a misbehaving internal agent, stop it relaunching on host groups and cut its access to enterprise networks. Activating it directs connected AI gateways to block access to the models that drive the agent, and the company says endpoint-level termination is coming next. A human operator still has to trigger or lift the cutoff. As companies give agents real permissions to get work done, a dependable off switch is becoming a precondition for deploying them, and rivals such as Straiker already sell similar controls.

Sources: CrowdStrike blog, CNBC, CSO Online, TechRadar

5. Claude Enterprise to merge chat and Cowork by default on 16 November

Anthropic has made its unified Claude experience, which combines chat and the Cowork agent in one app, available to Claude Enterprise plans, and reports say it switches on by default for existing organisations on 16 November unless an owner opts out. The merged experience carries context between chat and Cowork through shared memory, so the same assistant can move from answering a question to carrying out a multi-step task. For IT teams, the practical point is that administrators have a short window to review access controls, spend limits and data settings before the default flips. This item is from the last couple of weeks rather than the last day, included because the effective date is imminent.

Sources: Claude blog, AI Chat Daily, Mixed
