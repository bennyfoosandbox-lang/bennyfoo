1. Anthropic launches Claude Duet, a proactive agent that works your inbox before you ask

Anthropic introduced Claude Duet, a standalone research-preview agent aimed at professionals and small-business owners who have too much to track. Built on Opus 5.5, it sends a morning brief from connected apps, rechecks them every few hours, and runs tasks in a cloud virtual machine with its own browser. It asks for approval before sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting files, and it never sees passwords. Access is by waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers, and background checks count against existing plan limits, so the real cost of always-on delegation will show up in usage quotas.

Sources: MacRumors, 9to5Google

2. OpenAI's frontier models and Codex come to Google Cloud, billable against existing cloud commitments

OpenAI and Google Cloud said they will put OpenAI's frontier models, including GPT-6 Astra, and Codex agentic workflows into Google Cloud's Model Garden over the coming weeks, starting with private previews for select enterprise customers. Buyers can purchase through the Google Cloud Marketplace and draw down existing spend commitments, and the models will also reach Google's Antigravity environment and Gemini Agent. OpenAI now reaches customers of all three major US clouds, though Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner. For businesses, model choice is increasingly a procurement decision made inside the cloud contract they already have.

Sources: OpenAI, Google Cloud Blog, Reuters, Axios

3. Survey: AI makes managers' jobs harder, with over half struggling to judge employees' work

A Nielsen survey of 1,700 directors and senior managers in seven countries, commissioned by Monday.com, found more than half find it harder to evaluate work after adopting AI because output now arrives polished and its origin is unclear. Some 78% have changed how they assess staff, 46% use AI to inform decisions and 39% use it to draft performance reviews, which raises bias concerns. Tool sprawl is growing too: 57% report more tools in circulation and only 29% call their AI infrastructure fully optimized. 58% expect to oversee more AI agents than people within a year, and 46% fear losing control of them, which makes the productivity story as much a governance problem as a tooling one.

Sources: TechCrunch, Monday.com AI report

4. Oracle extends AI Agent Studio to industry applications with no-code agent building and MCP support

Oracle announced AI Agent Studio for Industry Applications, a no-code and low-code environment for building, testing, orchestrating and monitoring agents for sectors such as construction, retail, finance, hospitality, government and utilities. It adds prebuilt industry agents, natural-language and visual authoring, MCP connections to Oracle tools, a choice of models on OCI, and observability that tracks AI-unit consumption. Oracle says it is planned to be available within the next 12 months in the US, so this is a roadmap signal rather than something customers can deploy today. It shows the enterprise suite vendors converging on the same pattern: agents inside the systems of record, wrapped in permissions and audit trails.

Sources: Oracle, Investing.com, Crypto Briefing

5. Pigment raises $250M at a $2.3B valuation as agents write most new planning metrics

Paris-based planning platform Pigment raised a $250 million Series E at a $2.3 billion valuation to build out Graphite, its compute engine, and the AI agents that run on it. The company says Graphite lets thousands of agents and people work on one governed model at once, and that 85% of new metrics on its platform are now created by an AI agent. The round is a data point that investors are backing agents in finance, sales and workforce planning, where deterministic, traceable results matter more than a chat interface. The 85% figure is company-reported, so treat it as a vendor claim.

Sources: PR Newswire, Quartz, Silicon Republic, PYMNTS
