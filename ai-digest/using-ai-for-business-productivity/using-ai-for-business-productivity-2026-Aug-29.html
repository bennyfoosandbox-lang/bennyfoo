1. ServiceNow's AI business crosses $1 billion — and it's not chatbots driving it

ServiceNow's Q2 2026 earnings showed AI annual contract value (ACV) crossing $1 billion for the first time, up more than 40% quarter-over-quarter, as total revenue climbed 24% to $3.99 billion. The more telling number is underneath: agentic AI production deployments — live autonomous workflows in customer environments, not chatbot pilots — grew ninefold over nine months, while deals bundling five or more AI products rose 5.5x year-over-year. Half of ServiceNow's net-new business is now non-seat-based, meaning customers are paying for AI outcomes rather than per-user licenses, a shift its AI Control Tower governance product (500+ customers within six months of launch) is built to police. For a company that builds workflow software for the world's largest enterprises, this is one of the clearest signals yet that agentic AI has moved from pilot budgets into signed, recurring contracts.

Sources: Tech Times, 24/7 Wall St., Fortune (earnings call transcript), Seeking Alpha

2. Workday's AI ARR triples to nearly $600 million as agents go from novelty to default

Workday's fiscal Q2 2027 results, reported August 27, showed AI product ARR approaching $600 million, up more than 200% year-over-year, with AI now driving over 25% of all new annual contract value. More than 5,500 customers are actively using one or more of Workday's "organic" agents, up 35% quarter-over-quarter, and its Deployment Agent alone logged a roughly 500% jump in query volume. The company also took its Financial Audit Agent generally available and unveiled Agent Passport, which tests and continuously monitors every AI agent — Workday-built or third-party — before and after it goes into production, addressing the governance gap that has slowed rollout elsewhere. New customers including KPMG, Guess and BWX Technologies, plus expanded deployments at Merck, Novartis and Caterpillar, suggest AI capability is now a real factor in HR and finance platform purchasing decisions, not just a bolt-on.

Sources: Workday / PR Newswire, BigGo Finance, Yahoo Finance (GuruFocus)

3. A new survey says AI agent use among engineers jumped 70% in a year — and most call it a productivity leap

Temporal's 2026 State of Development Report, based on a spring survey of 550+ software engineers and engineering leaders in the US and UK, found that 80.8% now use AI agents daily or more, up from 47.3% a year earlier — a 70.8% relative increase. Just as notable: 91.1% say agents have "improved" or "revolutionized" their productivity, and 85.5% report at least some trust in agent outputs. But the same survey is a caution against reading the adoption curve as maturity: 41.1% of respondents hit agent-related issues daily, and 9% say continuously, which the report's authors frame as confidence in agents outpacing the reliability infrastructure built to run them. For business leaders evaluating where to invest next, the gap between adoption speed and operational maturity is the real story, not the headline growth number.

Sources: Morningstar / Business Wire, Developer Tech, IdeaToMVP

4. Google hands its agent-to-agent protocol to a neutral foundation, joining Anthropic's MCP

On August 20, Google formally donated its Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol — which governs how AI agents communicate with each other — to the Linux Foundation-directed Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), placing it alongside Anthropic's Model Context Protocol (MCP), which standardizes how agents connect to tools and data. The AAIF has grown from 49 to more than 250 members in under a year, with platinum backing from AWS, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft and OpenAI. For businesses deploying multi-vendor agent stacks, this matters practically: MCP and A2A under one neutral governance body reduces the risk of a fragmented, vendor-locked protocol war, and should make it easier for security patches and interoperability fixes to propagate consistently across agent deployments built on different platforms.

Sources: Forkast News, Techzine, Pebblous

5. AI-attributed layoffs hit 205,000 workers in 2026 — already matching last year's full total

A running tracker of documented workforce reductions found AI or automation was cited as a primary or contributing driver in job losses affecting roughly 205,000 U.S. workers through August 2026 — matching or exceeding the prior full-year total in under eight months. More than half of all major layoff announcements this year now cite AI explicitly, concentrated in customer service, data operations, entry-level software roles and finance back offices — precisely the functions where AI productivity tools have shown the most measurable near-term gains. The share of layoff announcements citing AI has more than doubled since January, with May and June setting monthly records, and separate survey data finds 51% of business leaders expect their own company to cut roles specifically because AI is consolidating or eliminating work before year-end. For business leaders, it's a reminder that productivity gains and workforce reduction are increasingly the same story told from two different vantage points.

Sources: Outsource Accelerator, IBTimes UK
