1. US productivity jumps 3.6% in Q3 as unit labor costs fall — and the AI-credit debate heats up

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on 5 November that nonfarm business productivity rose at a 3.6% annualized rate in the third quarter, well above the roughly 2.7–2.8% consensus. Output grew about 3.7% while hours worked barely moved (+0.1%), so unit labor costs fell 0.7% — the pattern you would expect if firms are getting more done without adding headcount. Commentators are split on attribution: some point to the AI and automation investment boom, others to a capital-spending cycle more broadly, and economists warn that quarterly productivity data are volatile and often revised. Fed leadership has said any AI payoff should emerge gradually, which makes this print a key data point for the 'is AI showing up in the numbers' argument.

Sources: Axios, Advisor Perspectives, Haver Analytics, FourWeekMBA

2. Perplexity launches “Teammates”: persistent agents for ongoing business projects

Perplexity introduced Teammates, agents built for long-running work rather than one-off prompts, running on top of Perplexity Computer. Each teammate keeps a persistent thread and context, can be added to Slack channels so colleagues assign it tasks, and can hand work to other teammates for multi-step projects across marketing, sales, support, operations or finance. Web access is limited to the $200-a-month Max tier at launch, and coverage notes that heavy or parallel use can burn through usage credits quickly. It extends the 'always-on digital coworker' race with Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, and enterprises should check admin controls, data handling and cost before wider rollout.

Sources: TechRepublic, Perplexity, NewsBytes, Analytics Insight

3. Mistral rebuilds Studio into an enterprise agent platform with managed agents, apps and evals

On 5 November Mistral expanded Mistral Studio with managed agents (sandboxes, subagents, skills and hooks), employee-facing apps, workflow triggers, OpenTelemetry-based observability and dataset-driven evaluations, alongside its Mistral Large 4 model. The framing is a clean division of labor: agents investigate and act, workflows run fixed steps and approvals, and apps give staff an interface. Workflows are generally available while apps and managed agents are in public preview on Mistral Cloud, with private-cloud and on-premises deployment promised for the end of November. For businesses wary of US platforms, this packages production tooling — traces to eval datasets, online scoring of live runs — in a sovereignty-friendly stack.

Sources: Let's Data Science, Technobezz, Expert Beacon, AI-TLDR

4. Goldman: workers displaced by AI take about a month longer to land new jobs

A Goldman Sachs analysis of more than 15,000 laid-off workers (2016–2025) found that those in roles most likely displaced by AI in 2025 took roughly a month longer to be rehired than other displaced workers — about three weeks longer for under-35s and five weeks for those 35 and over. Among displaced workers from AI-exposed jobs who found work in 2025, around three-quarters landed in occupations with similar or greater AI exposure, up from just over half in 2023. The note is an early read on the first wave of AI-related layoffs, and there is still no definitive count of AI-caused job losses. For leaders banking productivity gains through headcount, it adds a human-cost and reskilling dimension to the efficiency story.

Sources: Axios, Fortune, FourWeekMBA, AI Weekly

5. Illumio survey: 70% of firms let AI act where humans once signed off, but only 49% can audit every action

Illumio's 'Licensed to Act' research (published 21 October, so slightly older than the 24-hour window but new to this digest) surveyed 1,100 IT and security decision-makers across 10 markets. About 70% say their organizations now let AI make decisions or take actions that required human approval a year earlier, yet only 49% can see and audit every action an AI agent takes, and three in four reported at least one AI-related security issue or warning sign in the past year. While 82% agree agents need different controls from traditional software, only 25% rank explicitly defining permitted AI actions among their top two controls. It is a sharp reminder that productivity gains from autonomous agents are outrunning governance; this is a vendor-sponsored survey, so treat exact figures as indicative.

Sources: Let's Data Science, IT Brief Australia, Help Net Security, CDO Magazine
