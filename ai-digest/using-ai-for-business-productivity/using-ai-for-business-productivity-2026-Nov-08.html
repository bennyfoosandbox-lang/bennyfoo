1. AI is minting “no-employee” businesses — and may be hollowing out the middle

CNBC reports that business applications showing no sign of future hiring rose 26.8% in AI-exposed industries between Q1 2024 and Q1 2026 (about 222,000 to 281,000 a quarter), while applications likely to become employer firms fell 6.4%, per a George Mason Mercatus Center analysis of Census data. Operators describe replacing photographers, designers, bookkeepers and ad managers with Claude and ChatGPT: one runs 12 e-commerce brands with no staff, another reached roughly $250,000 in booked business in seven months while spending under $60 a month on AI tools. Stanford economist Erik Brynjolfsson argues the real shift is AI doing work that once needed a whole team, and predicts a “hollowing out” of mid-sized firms as very small teams and giant platforms both gain. The researchers caution the data is preliminary and does not prove AI caused the rise, and Gusto data suggests existing small businesses that adopt AI actually grow headcount.

Sources: CNBC, Mercatus Center (via CNBC)

2. Bank of England: AI drove nearly a fifth of UK growth, but firms report only ~0.5% productivity gain

The Bank of England’s November Monetary Policy Report, published 5 November, says AI-related activity accounts for just under a fifth of UK GDP growth since the start of 2026. That growth comes from building and selling AI, such as data-centre investment, rather than from businesses using it. Firms in the Bank’s survey estimate AI has lifted their productivity by only around 0.5% over three years, and the aggregate employment effect is close to zero, although AI is reducing the odds that firms hire or replace workers. For leaders weighing AI spend, the takeaway is that the central bank sees a demand boost so far and is still waiting to see whether it becomes a supply-side productivity gain; the 0.5% figure is self-reported.

Sources: PPC Land

3. Study: managers trust employees 68% of the time — but only 24% when AI was used

A six-study paper in The Leadership Quarterly, by researchers at Hebrew University and WHU, found supervisors extended trust to employees who worked independently 68% of the time but just 24% of the time when the employee used an LLM such as ChatGPT. Lower trust was linked to weaker perceived performance, lower citizenship ratings and reduced promotability. The penalty was no longer statistically significant when the organisation explicitly encouraged LLM use, yet it persisted when AI was banned or the company had no stated policy, and neither task importance nor manager AI experience reliably reduced it. The authors warn this can push workers toward concealed “shadow AI”, making a clear written AI policy as important to productivity gains as tool access.

Sources: People Matters, Futurism

4. Snowflake launches CoWork and always-on “Bots” at Expedition 2026

At its Expedition 2026 event Snowflake unveiled CoWork, a personal productivity agent for knowledge workers, alongside CoCo for coding, and introduced always-on CoCo Bots and CoWork Bots that run in the background and escalate only when human approval is needed. The company positions its platform as the control and governance layer for enterprise agents, pairing the bots with a business ontology so agents understand company context and with its Cortex AI Gateway for oversight. Shelter Mutual Insurance said it already has eight agents running in production, a sign of the shift from pilots to deployment. It is another vendor bet that persistent, approval-gated agents rather than chat assistants are where office productivity gains will come from.

Sources: Times of India, TechGig, ITBEAT, Justo Global

5. ServiceNow starts another layoff round as AI efficiencies top $1B and severance jumps

ServiceNow has begun a fresh round of layoffs in India, the UK and the US without disclosing numbers, while saying it still aims to end 2026 at the same headcount it started with. Its latest filing shows $87 million of severance in the third quarter versus $24 million a year earlier, and $167 million over nine months versus $53 million. Management has credited AI with more than $1 billion in annualised efficiency gains, and the CFO said sales-operations headcount is down significantly while quota-carrying sellers grew by double digits. Shares are down more than 30% year to date, showing that even the vendor selling AI productivity is paying a real restructuring bill to capture it.

Sources: People Matters, CTech by Calcalist, Tech in Asia
