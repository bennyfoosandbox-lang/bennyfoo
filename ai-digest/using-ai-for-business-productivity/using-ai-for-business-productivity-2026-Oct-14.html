1. Jabra: AI is handing workers time back, but they are not working any less

Jabra's WorkLife 2026 report, released October 13, surveyed more than 6,000 knowledge workers across France, Germany, India, Poland, the UK and the US in May and finds a productivity paradox: workers say AI is saving them time, yet in every category of work measured they report doing more than a year ago. The biggest rise is in strategic planning and analysis, where about half of respondents (51%) say they spend more time than before. AI use also tracks with more collaboration rather than less: 64% of frequent AI users say they work more with colleagues than a year ago, versus 25% of lighter users. The takeaway for managers is that reclaimed hours are being reabsorbed by higher expectations unless the organization deliberately decides what the saved time is for. This is vendor-commissioned research, so treat the headline numbers as directional.

Sources: Jabra / GlobeNewswire, FinancialContent, Le Lézard

2. Cisco lets teams invite Claude and OpenAI agents into Webex meetings and spaces

At WebexOne (October 7), Cisco said employees will be able to invite collaborative AI agents into Webex spaces, meetings and calls, where they can pick up the conversation's context and carry out multi-step work across Webex and third-party apps. The headline integration is Anthropic's Claude Managed Agents, cloud-hosted agents that keep working in the background after the team logs off, alongside OpenAI-powered agents. A central layer for identity, governance and audit is meant to let enterprises decide how much autonomy each agent gets. Most of this is slated for early 2027 (personal agents for Webex Calling arrive in Q1 2027), and no pricing or general-availability date has been given for the Claude integration, so this is a roadmap signal more than a shipping product.

Sources: UC Today, Computerworld, SiliconANGLE, Dutch IT Channel

3. OpenAI's GPT-6 models will power Atlassian's Rovo agents across Jira and Confluence

Atlassian and OpenAI expanded their partnership on October 6 so OpenAI's frontier (GPT-6 family) models sit behind agents across Atlassian's platform and Rovo. The pitch rests on Atlassian's Teamwork Graph, the context layer linking people, projects, documents and decisions, which lets Rovo trace blockers and missed milestones across Jira tickets and Confluence pages. New Atlassian MCP Server connectors are meant to move data between ChatGPT, Codex and Atlassian tools, and OpenAI says more than 3,000 Atlassian developers already use Codex. The bigger ideas, such as assigning Jira work directly to agents and reviewing the results, are still described as exploratory, and no commercial terms were disclosed.

Sources: OpenAI, Channel Insider, Four Week MBA

4. California signs the "No Robo Bosses Act" and an AI-layoff disclosure law

Our earlier note that SB 947 was waiting on Newsom's desk has resolved: he signed it around September 30 as part of a package of AI worker protections. From July 1, 2027, employers cannot rely solely on an automated decision system to discipline or fire someone, and a human reviewer must corroborate the output with other evidence. A companion law (SB 951) requires Cal-WARN notices from January 1, 2027 to disclose when a mass layoff or termination is caused substantially by AI or automation. Other signed bills restrict workplace surveillance and the use of biometric data to infer workers' emotions. For any business deploying AI in HR or workforce planning in California, human-in-the-loop review is now a compliance requirement rather than a best practice.

Sources: TechRadar, KSAT / AP, ABC News / AP, TechInformed

5. Gartner: about 30% of workers laid off for AI will need to be rehired by 2029, at higher cost

Gartner forecasts that by 2029 roughly 30% of employees laid off because AI replaced their roles will have to be rehired, likely at significantly higher cost, citing lost institutional knowledge and weakened talent pipelines. Some outlets round the figure to "a third"; Gartner's own framing is that AI productivity gains drove only about 1% of last year's job losses. VP Analyst Tori Paulman argues agentic AI does not yet do enough of an organization's work to justify large layoffs, and that the real opportunity is workforce amplification rather than automation. The forecast landed in mid-September and is still circulating this week, making it a useful counterweight to headcount-cutting AI business cases.

Sources: CIO Dive, Channel Dive, IT Brief UK, The New Daily
