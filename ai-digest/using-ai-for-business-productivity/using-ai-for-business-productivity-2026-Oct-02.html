1. Templafy: 95% of knowledge workers still edit AI drafts before they are usable

Templafy's "Business-Ready AI Gap 2026" report, released October 1, finds that 90% of US and UK knowledge workers use AI to create business documents at least weekly, yet 95% edit the output before it can go out the door. Staff spend almost four hours a week reviewing, correcting and validating AI-generated documents, and 48% say that review leaves little real time saved. Among people who spend more than an hour revising a document, 34% say editing takes longer than writing it themselves. The takeaway for productivity programmes is that access to a model is only the start: grounding it in approved content, templates and company rules is what makes the output business-ready.

Sources: GlobeNewswire, Medianet, Macau Business

2. Barclays scales Claude and Claude Code across the bank

Barclays and Anthropic announced on October 1 a broad expansion of Claude across the bank, aimed at speeding software delivery, modernising legacy systems and improving operations. The bank expects half of its developers to be using Claude Code by the end of 2026, with a majority in 2027. Existing deployments already give a sense of scale: the Colleague Knowledge Assistant is used by more than 16,000 UK employees and has handled over a million searches, and Claude processes about 120,000 client emails a day in Global Markets. For a tightly regulated Tier-1 lender, it is a concrete adoption target rather than a pilot announcement.

Sources: Bloomberg, Anthropic, PYMNTS, Crypto Briefing

3. GoTo survey: AI saves 2.3 hours a day, but 83% fear being blamed for its mistakes

GoTo's "Pulse of Work in 2026" study of 2,500 knowledge workers and IT leaders across ten countries finds that employees who use AI report saving about 2.3 hours a day. The same people are anxious: 83% fear they could be blamed or fired over an AI-driven error, 31% have hesitated to use AI for that reason, and 84% say their employer could do more to encourage responsible use. Oversight is thin, with 55% admitting they do not always double-check outputs and 43% saying they have used content they suspected was low quality or fabricated. Sixty percent feel pressure to use AI to raise productivity, and 47% say AI use is considered in performance reviews, which points to unclear accountability rather than tool quality as the bottleneck.

Sources: IT Brief UK, GoTo, VMblog, Fintech News

4. Hackett Group and ARIS: process context makes AI outcomes 5x more likely to succeed

New research from The Hackett Group and ARIS, published October 1, argues that agents fail for lack of business understanding rather than lack of capability. Organizations with strong process context are five times more likely to report very successful AI outcomes, and 86% of respondents say agents cannot be deployed reliably without it. Yet only 22% have comprehensive, real-time visibility into end-to-end processes, while 59% describe their visibility as fragmented across systems. The finding supports a practical order of operations for agentic rollouts: map and instrument the process first, then hand it to an agent.

Sources: Business Wire (via FinancialContent), North America Outlook, VMblog

5. BearingPoint: three-quarters see AI impact, but only 13% scale as planned

BearingPoint's study of 1,050 C-suite and senior leaders in 13 countries finds that nearly three-quarters of organizations with AI in production report measurable top-line or bottom-line impact, yet only 13% have scaled their initiatives fully in line with the original business case. The gap between leaders and the rest is stark: 47% of AI Leaders scale completely as planned versus 6% of Implementers. Meanwhile, 62% say AI has already created workforce overcapacity of at least 10%, and 94% expect that by 2030, raising an open question about how organizations redeploy the freed capacity. The report ties the winners to financial accountability, trusted data, governance and early workforce decisions.

Sources: Business Wire (via FinancialContent), Le Lézard, 01net
