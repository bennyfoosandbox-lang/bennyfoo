1. Workday's Global Workforce Report: AI is rewriting jobs faster than it is cutting them

Workday's October 5 workforce report finds leaders leaning toward getting more from existing staff rather than shrinking: 40% expect AI to help them extract more from current employees, while 28% expect it to reduce headcount. The data also shows internal mobility stalling (moves to new in-house roles fell at 57% of employers, with promotions flat) even as nearly four in ten employees lived through a reorganisation in the past year. Job-requisition data from 550+ Workday Recruiting employers shows demand for basic prompting skills peaking in January 2026 and then falling 25%, while demand for hands-on skills such as building AI tools rose 51% between September 2025 and July 2026. The takeaway for productivity programmes: the skills bar is moving from using AI to building with it.

Sources: Workday Newsroom, PR Newswire, Stock Titan

2. UAE workers save 3.6 hours a week with AI, then spend 3.4 hours fixing its output

A Workday study of UAE employees, "Beyond Productivity: Measuring the Real Value of AI in the UAE", puts numbers on the rework problem: self-reported savings average 3.6 hours a week, but respondents also spend about 3.4 hours a week clarifying, correcting or rewriting AI-generated work, nearly cancelling the gain. Usage is intense (90% use AI at least weekly, 18% daily) and 33% say they use the efficiency to absorb greater workloads without adding headcount, versus 24% who say savings go mainly to cost reduction. Workday has not published sample size or respondent-level data, and both figures are self-reported averages, so they cannot be netted into a verified result. It is still a useful warning that measured time saved and net productivity are different things.

Sources: Khaleej Times, Workday Investor Relations, TBreak

3. Google rolls out reusable "Skills" across Workspace and Gemini, setting up the end of Gems

Google began rolling Skills out to Google Workspace on October 5, with the Gemini app following October 13 and both expected to finish by mid-November. A skill is a saved, reusable set of instructions (a brand voice, a lesson plan format) that can be invoked with a slash command, stacked with other skills in one chat, or applied automatically when Gemini judges it relevant. Teams can author skills in Google Docs, collect comments, and enable them through Workspace Studio, which turns individual prompt craft into shared, governed team know-how. Google says Skills will replace Gems, with Gems beginning a gradual exit in November, so admins with Gem-based workflows should plan a migration. Rollout is phased, so availability varies by tenant.

Sources: Google Workspace Updates, Google Workspace Blog, Android Central, The Keyword (Google)

4. Microsoft 365 Copilot Business moves to default usage-based billing for new purchases

Under Message Center post MC1476200 (published September 21), new Microsoft 365 Copilot Business purchases will automatically get the Azure resources and a default spending policy needed for usage-based billing, which meters Copilot Credits for advanced experiences such as Copilot Cowork, Copilot Studio agents and Work IQ APIs. Partner write-ups put the start at October 19 for direct purchases and November 2 for the CSP channel, with a default cap reported at $10 per user per month; sources disagree on exact dates and defaults, and one analyst report says enterprise tenants stay default-off, so admins should confirm in the Microsoft 365 admin center. Existing subscriptions and policies are unchanged. For budget owners, the practical point is that agentic Copilot use is shifting from a flat seat fee to metered consumption that needs a spending policy before it scales.

Sources: Stoneridge Software, EPC Group, PupuWeb (MC1476200), Microsoft Licensing: Copilot Credits
