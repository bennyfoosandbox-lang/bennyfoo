1. OpenAI launches Dots, always-on agents that work across 4,000 apps around the clock

OpenAI unveiled Dots at DevDay on September 29: persistent agents powered by GPT-6 Astra that learn what matters to a user, keep working toward goals without daily oversight, and connect to more than 4,000 apps through its plugin ecosystem. Each Dot gets its own cloud computer and browser that the user can open to inspect its work, and can be messaged in ChatGPT, Slack or Teams with context carried across channels. It rolls out to ChatGPT Pro and Business Premium users, with a beta for Enterprise, Edu and Healthcare workspaces, and each user gets one Dot at launch. For business productivity it moves the pitch from prompt-and-respond assistants to delegated, continuous work, which raises the same oversight questions that agent-governance vendors are now raising money to solve.

Sources: 9to5Google, Unite.AI, Quartz, Yahoo Tech, TBreak

2. Anthropic ships Claude Sonnet 5.5: same price, 30% faster, up to 30% cheaper per task

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5 on September 28, less than a week after Opus 5.5. It keeps Sonnet 5's pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, but generates output more than 30% faster and can cut the total cost of a task by as much as 30% because it uses fewer tokens and tool calls. In several tests it comes close to Opus 5.5, giving enterprises a cheaper option for agentic work, and it is the first Sonnet to launch with the cyber safeguards previously reserved for more capable models. It is available on Anthropic's platform, AWS, Google Cloud and Azure with zero-data-retention support, and landed the same day in GitHub Copilot.

Sources: VentureBeat, TechCrunch, Thurrott, The Next Web, GitHub Changelog

3. Anthropic opens Claude Marketplace with 2,000+ connectors, plugins and partner agents

Anthropic's Claude Marketplace, live since September 23 and widely covered this week, puts connectors and plugins, Claude-powered agents and service partners in one catalog that organizations can buy from inside the platform. Launch partners include Atlassian, Google, Microsoft, Notion and Salesforce for connectors, CrowdStrike, Cursor, Harvey, Legora, Lovable and Snowflake for agents, and Accenture, BCG and Deloitte for services. Developers build connectors with the Model Context Protocol and Agent Skills and submit them for listing. The move positions Anthropic as a platform rather than just a model vendor, and puts it in direct competition with the SaaS incumbents whose products it plugs into. Note: this item is from earlier in the week, included because it was new to this digest.

Sources: BleepingComputer, PYMNTS, gHacks, AI Weekly, EnterpriseDNA

4. Meta launches Muse for Small Business, an agent that runs sales, cash flow and ads across your apps

A day after announcing its enterprise platform, Meta launched Muse for Small Business on September 29. An owner gives the agent a goal, such as finding new customers, and it works across connected tools including Asana, Zoom, Box, Canva, Slack, Intuit QuickBooks, Shopify, Stripe, Notion and Dropbox, plus Meta ad accounts and business Facebook and Instagram pages. Meta pitches it as a digital operations layer that analyzes sales and campaigns, manages cash flow and inventory, drafts customer messages and produces ads. Pricing matches the existing Muse app, which is free with usage limits and subscription-based beyond that, so it undercuts most paid business agents on entry cost.

Sources: CNBC, TechCrunch, Unite.AI, Quartz, Neowin

5. Reco raises $55M to see and secure every AI agent inside the enterprise

Reco, a New York security company founded in 2020, raised $55 million led by AT&T Ventures, bringing its total to $140 million, to extend its SaaS security platform to AI agents. Its product maps which identities agents use, what permissions and data they can reach, and which workflows they can trigger, across OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft Copilot, Salesforce, ServiceNow and Workday. Its CEO expects annual recurring revenue, currently in the double-digit millions, to triple this year. TechCrunch notes it is one of a crowded field: about $435 million has gone into 12 agent security and governance rounds since April, a sign that companies deploying agents are budgeting for oversight alongside the agents themselves.

Sources: TechCrunch, SecurityWeek, BankInfoSecurity, FinTech Global
