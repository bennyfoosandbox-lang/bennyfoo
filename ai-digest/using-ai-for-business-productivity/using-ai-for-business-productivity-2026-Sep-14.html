1. Anthropic tells shareholders it expects a second straight profitable quarter as it preps a $2 trillion IPO

Anthropic has told a small group of investors it expects to post an adjusted operating profit again in Q3 2026 — on the heels of a first profitable quarter that saw H1 revenue hit roughly $16.2 billion — as it lines up an IPO that could value the company as high as $2 trillion, more than double its ~$965 billion private valuation from May. Run-rate revenue reportedly reached $65 billion by late July, up from $47 billion in mid-May, driven largely by enterprise API demand for Claude. For a company whose economics were long assumed to be underwater on compute costs, sustained profitability materially changes the pitch enterprise buyers hear about the durability of frontier-model vendors they're building workflows around. A confidential draft S-1 is already in with the SEC, with a public listing targeted for October.

Sources: Detroit News, Yellow, TradingKey, GraniteShares

2. Trackers now put 54% of 2026 layoffs on AI or automation, with over 170,000 workers affected

Layoffs.fyi's running tally shows 54% of layoff events tracked in 2026 cite AI, automation, or machine learning as a factor, up from under 8% in 2025 — a shift that has pushed past 170,000 AI-attributed job losses and spread well beyond tech into finance, logistics, consulting, media, retail and manufacturing. The first ten days of September alone saw more than 6,300 tech job cuts globally, led by Uber's ~3,300-role (10% of workforce) reduction aimed at stripping out management layers and redirecting capital toward autonomous vehicles and core platforms, alongside cuts at PayPal, Apple, Zomato and Oracle. Total verified job cuts worldwide in 2026 have now passed 624,000. For businesses evaluating AI adoption, this is the clearest signal yet that "AI productivity" is increasingly being read by boards as a headcount lever, not just a tooling upgrade — even as some employers (IBM, Walmart) are publicly pushing back on the narrative that AI necessarily shrinks the workforce.

Sources: IBTimes UK, Business Standard, Gulf News

3. Legal AI firm Harvey raises $550M at a $15.5B valuation, nearly doubling in nine months

Harvey, the legal AI platform, closed a $550 million round co-led by Diffusion and Lightspeed Venture Partners at a $15.5 billion valuation — up from $11 billion just six months ago and its third major raise in under a year. The company says it now serves more than 3,000 organizations, with annual recurring revenue above $400 million, roughly 80% of Am Law 100 firms on the platform, and five Fortune 10 companies as customers. Notably, Harvey is using the new capital to build its own proprietary models rather than continuing to rent capacity exclusively from OpenAI and Anthropic — a move that mirrors a broader trend of vertical-AI companies wanting direct control over the economics and behavior of the models their entire business depends on. For business-productivity watchers, Harvey is one of the clearest proof points that a narrow, deeply-integrated AI product for professional services can command frontier-lab-scale valuations.

Sources: TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Harvey, Tech Startups

4. Mistral raises the largest-ever European tech round — $3.5B at a $24B valuation — to sell "sovereign" enterprise AI

Mistral closed a Samsung Electronics-led €3 billion (~$3.5 billion) Series D at a post-money valuation above $24 billion, nearly doubling its worth in the year since its $2 billion Series C valued it at $13.7 billion. The round — the largest equity raise ever by a European tech company — was co-led by Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing backer PSG Equity, with new money from Advent, BlackRock-managed funds and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg alongside returning investors a16z, ASML, General Catalyst, Lightspeed, NVIDIA and Salesforce Ventures. Mistral is positioning the raise around "sovereign, open-weight AI" — models enterprises and governments can run on infrastructure and data they fully control, an increasingly explicit counter-pitch to US hyperscaler-hosted models for regulated industries and public-sector buyers in Europe. It lands a week after Mistral's own partnership with Cloudera to embed its models directly into governed enterprise data platforms.

Sources: Crunchbase News, TechCrunch, Mistral, FinSMEs

5. DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash, an MIT-licensed model roughly 90x cheaper than rivals, and starts routing its Pro tier onto it today

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash, the smallest member of its new architecture family, with a 552-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts backbone, native vision support, a 1-million-token context window, and open weights under a permissive MIT license that enterprises can self-host and modify commercially. Off-peak pricing runs as low as $0.003 per million cached input tokens, and the company says it beats larger rivals like Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on benchmarks while costing roughly 90x less to run — a combination aimed squarely at cost-sensitive enterprise deployments doing high-volume document or codebase analysis. Starting today, September 14, DeepSeek is routing its existing V4 Pro tier onto V4.1-Flash by default until a dedicated V4.1 Pro model ships, meaning any business already on DeepSeek's paid tier gets the cheaper, faster model automatically. For business-productivity buyers, it's another data point that frontier-adjacent capability is getting radically cheaper to license and self-host, not just to rent via API.

Sources: VentureBeat, Dataconomy, The Business Standard, Geeky Gadgets
