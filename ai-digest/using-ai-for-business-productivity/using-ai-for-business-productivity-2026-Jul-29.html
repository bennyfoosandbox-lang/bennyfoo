1. Visa cuts 2,600 jobs, 7% of its workforce, as CEO ties the reshuffle to AI

Visa is eliminating roughly 2,600 positions — about 7% of its global workforce of some 34,100 — with cuts falling hardest on technology and product teams. In a staff memo sent Tuesday, CEO Ryan McInerney said AI is "shaping the way work gets done at Visa" as the company drives efficiency to reinvest in "highest potential opportunities" like affluent customers, cross-border payments, business remittances, and geographic expansion. A person with direct knowledge of the plan told reporters AI was a significant factor in the decision but not the sole driver. It's the latest in a string of AI-attributed cuts at large employers this month, and one of the most direct admissions yet from a major financial-services company that AI-driven efficiency is now shaping headcount decisions rather than just individual task automation.

Sources: CNBC, Bloomberg, Fast Company, SF Chronicle

2. Dun & Bradstreet: three-quarters of businesses now see AI returns — but only 6% have data ready to scale them

Dun & Bradstreet's latest quarterly AI Momentum Survey, covering 10,000 businesses across 32 countries, finds enterprise AI value continuing to climb: 48% of respondents now report "pockets of ROI" and another 28% report "broad" or "strong" ROI across multiple projects, while the share of organizations actively "scaling AI" (rather than just piloting it) has risen to 34%. The catch is data infrastructure — only 6% of businesses say their enterprise data is "fully ready" to support AI at scale, meaning complete, current, and reliable enough to drive decisions consistently across teams and systems. D&B frames this as an inflection point: the ROI question that dominated 2025 is giving way to a data-readiness question, since uneven or incomplete data is now the binding constraint on turning early AI wins into enterprise-wide returns.

Sources: PR Newswire, StockTitan

3. Cognizant launches an EMEA AI Unit built to beat the industry's 88% agentic-pilot failure rate

Cognizant stood up a dedicated EMEA AI Unit combining advisory, engineering, and delivery capabilities to help enterprises move agentic AI from pilot to production — explicitly targeting the IDC-documented statistic that 88% of enterprise AI agent pilots fail to reach scale. The unit runs a three-tier "Frontier Deployed Engineering" model spanning AI strategy through delivery, is platform/model/cloud-agnostic, and is backed by a plan to build out 5,000 "Frontier Certified" engineers across the region; Cognizant points to two live pharmaceutical deployments as early proof points. The move — announced the same week Cognizant was named Anthropic's Global Premier Partner — signals large systems integrators are racing to package "de-risked" agentic AI delivery as a business line of its own, betting that most enterprises need help clearing the pilot-to-scale gap more than they need another model.

Sources: PR Newswire, Tech Times, Cognizant Newsroom, AiThority

4. NTT Research and Harvard: there's an optimal number of AI agents, and enterprises are blowing past it

Researchers from NTT's Physics of Artificial Intelligence Lab and Harvard University's Center for Brain Science published findings showing that adding more AI agents to a workplace task doesn't reliably increase productivity — and past a certain point, actively hurts it. Elizabeth Pavlova (Harvard CBS) and Hidenori Tanaka (NTT PAI Lab / Harvard CBS) found that coordination overhead between agents — redundant work, conflicting actions, and communication costs — becomes the limiting factor well before an organization runs out of tasks to automate, meaning there's a real ceiling on useful multi-agent deployment rather than a simple "more agents, more output" curve. The research lands directly on a live enterprise decision: as companies like Snowflake and Microsoft ship tools for orchestrating fleets of agents (see below), this suggests the harder problem isn't building more agents but knowing when to stop adding them.

Sources: BusinessWire, The Register, FinancialContent

5. Snowflake launches Cortex AI Gateway to stop enterprise AI agents from running up runaway bills

Snowflake unveiled Cortex AI Gateway, a governance layer that lets enterprises centrally control, monitor, and cost-manage both first-party agents (Snowflake CoWork, CoCo) and third-party agents built on external platforms such as Claude Code and Cursor. The product gives IT and finance teams a unified, end-to-end record of agent activity and token consumption, attributes spend to the specific teams, agents, or workloads driving it, and lets organizations enforce hard spending limits before costs run away — directly answering the token-rationing problem several enterprises (Uber, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce) have hit this month. Snowflake also announced a first wave of security integrations with identity vendors 1Password, Aembit, Linx Security, SailPoint, and Saviynt — an unusual coalition of usually-competing vendors aligning around a shared trust model for autonomous agents, underscoring that agent governance and cost control, not raw model capability, are becoming this quarter's dominant enterprise AI product category.

Sources: Snowflake, VentureBeat, AIwire, ChannelE2E
