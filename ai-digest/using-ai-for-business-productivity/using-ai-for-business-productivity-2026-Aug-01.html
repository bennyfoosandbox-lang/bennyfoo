1. The EU AI Act's enforcement teeth go live tomorrow — and they reach U.S. businesses too

August 2, 2026 is when the European Commission's enforcement and penalty powers over general-purpose AI providers formally become applicable — the Commission and its AI Office can now request documentation, run technical evaluations of models, demand risk-mitigation measures, restrict or withdraw a model from the EU market, and issue fines of up to 3% of global annual turnover or €15 million, whichever is higher. The underlying transparency and GPAI obligations themselves took effect a year ago (August 2, 2025); what changes tomorrow is that regulators can actually act on violations, with four separate legal routes to a penalty. A parallel delay, approved by the European Parliament in June, pushes compliance for standalone high-risk systems (recruitment tools, credit scoring, and similar Annex III use cases) out to December 2027 — so the immediate bite on August 2 is aimed squarely at the general-purpose models powering most business AI tools. Because the Act's reach is extraterritorial (any organization whose AI outputs affect EU residents is in scope, GDPR-style), this is a live compliance deadline for U.S. and global companies running AI-driven productivity tools, not just EU-based ones.

Sources: TECHi, Beam AI, ComplianceHub.Wiki

2. Fed researchers: AI's productivity payoff is still invisible in the data — and that may be normal

A new St. Louis Fed analysis of roughly 490,000 corporate earnings-call transcripts from 5,198 publicly traded U.S. firms between 2000 and 2025, published July 31, finds executives talking about AI-driven productivity constantly — while the aggregate productivity data still isn't moving, confirming three years of official statistics showing no measurable economy-wide bump. The researchers frame this as consistent with a "century-old historical pattern" seen with earlier general-purpose technologies like electrification, where measurable gains took a decade or more to show up after broad adoption. Their proposed mechanism: when AI makes some output radically cheaper to produce, that same output becomes less valuable, and the price decline cancels out the volume gains in the productivity math. It's a research-grade complement to the Barclays and Atlanta Fed findings already tracked in this digest's archive — another independent dataset landing on the same "J-curve, not a light switch" conclusion.

Sources: Fortune, St. Louis Fed

3. ADP rides AI to a record quarter — HCM agents now reach nearly all 1.1 million clients

ADP's fiscal Q4 2026 results, reported July 29, beat Wall Street estimates on both revenue (+7% to $5.48B for the quarter, $21.9B for the year) and adjusted EPS (+17%), with management crediting AI tools as a real driver rather than a talking point. HCM agents inside ADP Assist are now available to nearly all of ADP's 1.1 million-plus clients; over the fiscal year, 3.1 million unique active users held 12 million conversations with the assistant, and since January it has surfaced 45,000 compliance insights letting clients catch tax and regulatory issues before they became problems. The modern Lyric HCM platform saw a 94% jump in live clients, and "The Zone," ADP's AI-infused service platform, reached 48% of its service workforce by year-end — ahead of the company's own target. It's a rare data point in this digest's ongoing ROI debate where the AI investment shows up directly in the earnings beat, not just in survey sentiment.

Sources: BigGo Finance, Sahm Capital, GuruFocus

4. Oracle wires Google's Gemini models directly into Fusion and NetSuite

Oracle and Google Cloud announced an expanded partnership on July 30 that brings Gemini models into Oracle AI Agent Studio for Fusion Applications and embeds them as an option inside Oracle Fusion Applications and NetSuite — putting a rival cloud's frontier model directly inside the ERP and CRM systems millions of business users already run daily. The move lets enterprise customers build, connect and run agentic workflows using Gemini without leaving their existing Oracle applications, extending Oracle's prior OCI-level Gemini access down into the applications layer where day-to-day productivity work actually happens. Markets read it as a signal of real demand: Oracle shares jumped as much as 8% intraday on the news, settling around a 6.5% gain. It continues a pattern this digest has tracked all summer — Microsoft adding Claude to Copilot Chat, Cognizant deepening its Anthropic tie-up — of productivity-software vendors going multi-model rather than betting the platform on a single AI lab.

Sources: Oracle, PR Newswire, TNW

5. 8x8 pushes AI Studio past the contact center into every business function

8x8 announced on July 30 that it's extending its AI Studio platform beyond customer service to the whole organization, adding multi-model support, voice-powered agent building, and one-click connectors to 11 third-party business tools so any team can deploy an agent without new vendors or custom development. A companion feature, 8x8 Pulse, turns calls, meetings, emails and support interactions into what the company calls "governed company memory" — context that's searchable and actionable by teams beyond the one that had the original conversation. The release also ships 8x8 Resolve, putting incident and emergency alerting on the same channels frontline workers already use day to day. It's a concrete instance of a broader shift this summer — from IT-built agent pilots toward business-user-built tools — that Alteryx's Agent Studio and Google's WorkflowAI have both been pushing toward as well.

Sources: Business Wire, Telecom Reseller, MarTech Cube
