1. OpenAI gives its agents a wallet, just as United Airlines bans agents from buying tickets

On Friday 13 November OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its “dots” agents complete purchases for users. Payments use card tokens from Visa, Mastercard and American Express, so the agent never sees the real card number, and every purchase still needs the user’s approval with Face ID or a fingerprint. For now Wallet is limited to US ChatGPT Pro subscribers with eligible US cards, OpenAI takes no cut of transactions, and the merchant, not OpenAI, handles refunds and disputes. Two days earlier United Airlines had revised its Contract of Carriage (Rule 6.J.6) to bar AI agents from making, changing or servicing tickets or buying anything, even when a person approves, and reserved the right to cancel such bookings and remove MileagePlus miles. Agentic purchasing is becoming practical, but sellers are deciding whether to accept it. Companies planning agent-run procurement or travel booking should check supplier terms before automating checkout.

Sources: CNBC, TechCrunch, The Next Web, Quartz, Decrypt, Skift (United), AirlineGeeks (United), PPC Land (United)

2. Surveys: most big firms run AI agents in production, but few can tie them to P&L or assign a manager

A Genpact survey released 12 November polled more than 2,000 executives at companies with over $1 billion in revenue across 17 industries. It found 79% now have AI agents in production, but only 20% use them broadly and consistently across functions, and just 15% have fully tied AI investment to P&L results. Among firms with agents in production, 92% kept their human workforce the same size or grew it, which goes against the idea that agents replace staff one for one. A smaller Vida survey of 215 US businesses, released the same day, found 60% give AI agents formal job titles and 16% count them as part of the workforce, but only 41% assign a human to manage or oversee them. Both surveys are vendor-sponsored, but they agree on the main point: deploying agents is now routine, while measuring them and managing them like workers is not.

Sources: Stockhouse (Genpact release), StockTitan (Genpact), Stockhouse (Vida release), FinancialContent (Vida)

3. AT&T moves from AI copilots to rebuilding whole business processes around agents

A SiliconANGLE report published 14 November describes AT&T using autonomous agents to redesign entire business processes, not just to speed up individual employees. The company now processes about 45 billion AI tokens a day, roughly 5.6 times the volume of a year earlier. Its agents work in customer care, fraud prevention, field operations and enterprise research, and run on its Ask AT&T platform and H2O.ai’s Super Agent. AT&T’s data and AI chief Andy Markus has said the focus is now on “entire workflows, entire processes where decisions have to be made throughout,” with a central review that scores each use case for business value, customer impact and risk. Cost control is part of the plan: AT&T reports that routing coding traffic to open-weight models cut AI coding costs by 56% with only about a 2% drop in quality. For other large firms, AT&T shows that an agent program at this scale needs governance and model-cost decisions as much as new tools.

Sources: SiliconANGLE, BriefGlance, PYMNTS, Fierce Network, CIO

4. Anthropic's IPO is reportedly several times oversubscribed ahead of pricing on 18 November

Anthropic’s amended S-1 offers 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, to be listed on Nasdaq as “ANT.” At the top of the range that would be about $64 billion raised, making it one of the largest IPOs ever, at an implied valuation of up to roughly $1.4 trillion. Alongside the IPO, seven companies including Nvidia, Google and Microsoft have agreed to buy $26 billion of Class N stock in a private placement. Bloomberg reported on 11 November that orders were several times the shares on offer, and some reports say bankers are weighing a price above the range. Pricing is expected on 18 November, with trading from 19 November. This comes during a week of AI-stock volatility, so the outcome will show how much public investors will pay for an enterprise AI vendor. For enterprise buyers, a listed Anthropic would mean quarterly disclosures and more pressure to show revenue growth, both of which could change its pricing and product priorities.

Sources: Stock Analysis (Bloomberg), Renaissance Capital, RTTNews, Let's Data Science, Fast Company, SF Standard

5. Security Bank staff have built 3,100 AI agents of their own, and the bank is training more

Philstar reported on 15 November that staff at Security Bank, a Philippine lender, have built about 3,100 “low-level” AI agents for business and operational tasks. Daily engagement is 98% among the 1,210 employees who hold Microsoft Copilot Premium licenses. According to the report, the bank’s digital leadership wants AI to take on more routine work so staff can spend more time on customer service and decisions that need judgment, and it is expanding training to more employees. The bank says it keeps a human in the loop on its AI solutions. Security Bank was one of the first companies in the Philippines to adopt Microsoft 365 Copilot in 2023, when it started with 300 users. Its rollout shows employees, not a central AI team, building agents at scale, but the article does not say whether the bank measures what those agents produce. This item rests on a single report.

Sources: Philstar, Security Bank (2023 Copilot adoption)
