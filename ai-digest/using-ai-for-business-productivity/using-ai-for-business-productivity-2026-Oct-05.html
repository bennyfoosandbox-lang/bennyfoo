1. McKinsey: AI could push 11 million US workers into new occupations by 2035

The McKinsey Global Institute published a workforce study on 29–30 September estimating that roughly 11 million US workers, about 7% of the labor force, may need to switch occupations by 2035 as AI reshapes work, within a wide 6–16 million band. The report models 36 million positions displaced against about 40 million created by AI-driven growth, so total openings in 2035 end up above today's level. The pain is concentrated: more than 75% of likely movers sit in office and administrative support, retail and sales, and transportation and logistics, and lower-wage workers without degrees face the most risk. The implied pace of about 770,000 occupational moves a year is roughly 3.6 times the historical rate, which is why the authors frame the next decade's challenge as mobility rather than scarcity. Coverage dated slightly before today's window; it is included because it is new to this digest.

2. IBM offers its Bob coding agent as a self-hosted deployment for regulated enterprises

On 1 October IBM announced a self-hosted deployment option for IBM Bob, its agentic software-development platform, so organisations can run it on-premises, in private or sovereign clouds, or fully air-gapped. The pitch targets companies whose source code, regulated data or critical systems keep them from using externally hosted AI development services, pairing data-residency and governance control with the productivity of an AI delivery partner. IBM says Bob grew from about 100 internal users in summer 2025 to more than 80,000 IBM employees, and that it routes each task across models by accuracy, performance and cost, drawing on Anthropic Claude, Mistral open-source models and IBM Granite. For business productivity buyers it is another sign that the compliance objection to AI coding agents is being answered with deployment choice rather than waivers.

Sources: Thailand Business News, Technode Global, IT Digest

3. OutSystems: 96% of enterprises already run AI agents, but 94% fear sprawl

An OutSystems survey of about 1,900 global IT leaders finds that 96% of enterprises are using AI agents in some capacity and 97% are exploring system-wide agentic strategies, a clear move from pilots to production. The catch is control: 94% say AI sprawl is raising complexity, technical debt and security risk, and only 12% have a centralized platform to manage it. Some 38% mix custom-built and pre-built agents, which makes the stack hard to standardize. The takeaway for productivity programs is that governance, not model quality, is now the bottleneck on scaling agent gains. The exact publication date of the release was not confirmed from the excerpts available to this run, so treat it as recent rather than same-day.

Sources: OutSystems, Techleap, Enterprise DNA

4. DigitalOcean puts Managed Agents into public preview with per-session microVMs and a tool gateway

DigitalOcean opened public preview of Managed Agents on 21 September, bundling a harness runtime, governed tool access and serverless inference so teams can run agent workloads without building the infrastructure themselves. Each session runs in its own Firecracker microVM, agents resume from pause in about 305 ms (the company claims 46% faster than leading alternatives), and the harness can run tools such as Claude Code, Codex or LangGraph unmodified. An Action Gateway fronts more than 16,000 tools from over 500 providers through one managed MCP endpoint, keeping credentials out of the model context. It matters for business productivity because it lowers the cost for mid-sized teams to move custom agents from demos to governed production. This item is about two weeks old and is included because it is new to the knowledge base.

Sources: Finviz / press release, AlternativeTo, DigitalOcean docs

5. HENNGE spins out 'HENNGE AI' to sell an AI gateway for governing agents and model costs

Japanese SaaS vendor HENNGE said on 1 October that it set up a new company, HENNGE AI, to commercialise the AI Gateway it built for its own use. The gateway is a proxy in front of large language models that lets organisations switch between multiple models, see usage and cost, and apply policy-based access control at user or group level. It is positioned as a secure foundation for running AI agents safely, squarely at the cost-governance and shadow-AI problems that surveys keep flagging. Reportedly the new company is itself run largely by AI agents, with only two human directors, though that detail rests on a single report. Treat the product details as early; the coverage found by this run is limited to one Japanese trade article.
