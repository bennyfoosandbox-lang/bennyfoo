1. Palantir's Q2 2026 earnings blow past expectations as commercial AI platform revenue surges

Palantir reported Q2 2026 revenue of $1.94 billion, up 93% year-over-year, with adjusted EPS of $0.41 beating consensus estimates. The standout number was U.S. commercial revenue, which surged 149% YoY to $764 million, as enterprises moved its AI platform from pilots into production workflows. The company closed 220 deals worth at least $1 million during the quarter — 73 of them north of $10 million — and raised full-year revenue guidance to 82% growth ($8.15–8.16 billion), sending shares up roughly 12% in after-hours trading. It's one of the clearest earnings-season signals yet that enterprise AI platform spend is converting into durable, scaling contracts rather than one-off experiments.

Sources: CNBC, Businesswire, Pulse2

2. Microsoft's Project Perception — agentic AI security operations — enters public preview

Microsoft's agentic cybersecurity platform, Project Perception, moved into public preview on August 3, built around a new in-house model, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, designed to do "most of the work of larger models at half the cost." The system runs red agents to map attack paths, blue agents to investigate and triage findings, and green agents to take corrective action, all coordinated by an orchestrator, with identity and governance handled through Microsoft's Agent 365 and human approval still required for consequential decisions. It's Microsoft's clearest bet yet that agentic AI's near-term enterprise value lies less in generating content and more in running security and IT operations continuously and semi-autonomously. For businesses, it's a template — bounded autonomy with mandatory human checkpoints — likely to spread to other back-office agent categories as governance pressure builds.

Sources: GeekWire, Futurum Group, Official Microsoft Blog

3. Cloudflare's Agents Week lays out infrastructure for the "agentic cloud"

Cloudflare kicked off its second annual Agents Week on August 2, unveiling infrastructure aimed at businesses building and operating AI agents at scale: Browser Rendering was rebranded and upgraded to "Browser Run" with 4x higher concurrency for agent workloads, a new lossless "Unweight" compression system claims up to 22% model footprint reduction, and Outbound Workers give sandboxed agents a zero-trust egress proxy. The company frames the push as building "Cloud 2.0" — infrastructure designed for agents as a primary workload rather than humans, arguing today's cloud and web were built for people, not autonomous software. Announcements continue through August 7, spanning compute, connectivity, security, identity and economics for the agent stack. For businesses evaluating where to run production AI agents, it signals the major cloud and edge platforms are now competing specifically on agent-native infrastructure, not just model access.

Sources: The Cloudflare Blog, Cloudflare Press Releases, StartupHub.ai

4. Ai4 2026 opens in Las Vegas with Hinton, Ng and Fei-Fei Li on one stage

Ai4 2026, billed as North America's largest applied-AI conference, opened August 4 at The Venetian in Las Vegas with more than 12,000 attendees and nearly 400 exhibitors — including AWS, SAP, IBM, Google Cloud and PayPal — up sharply from roughly 225 exhibitors in 2025. The marquee session pairs Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li and Andrew Ng, three of AI's most influential researchers who hold sharply differing views on existential risk, in a joint keynote organizers are framing as a face-off. Programming through August 6 is explicitly enterprise-focused, with tracks on deployment and scaling, governance frameworks and workforce transformation running alongside the technical sessions. The jump in exhibitor count and attendance is itself a data point on how fast the enterprise AI vendor ecosystem is expanding, with companies showing up to sell governance, infrastructure and agent tooling rather than just model access.

Sources: Tech Times, Yahoo Finance

5. Freehand raises $75M to put AI agents in charge of Fortune 500 supply-chain spend

Freehand, a startup automating enterprise procurement and payments with AI agents, raised a $75 million round co-led by Battery Ventures and NewRoad Capital Partners (from earlier in the week of July 28), bringing its total funding to $100 million. Its agents already manage supply-chain spend for Meta, Unilever, Johnson & Johnson, Pfizer, Dunkin' and Cardinal Health — negotiating rates, enforcing contracts, processing payments and reconciling data across enterprise systems. Customers report recovering 5–10% of supply-chain spend, completing procurement workflows 5–7x faster, and cutting procure-to-pay cycle times by more than 70%. It's a concrete counterpoint to this year's steady drumbeat of "AI ROI is elusive" surveys: in back-office spend management specifically, investors and Fortune 500 customers alike are backing measurable, multiple-fold efficiency gains with real money.

Sources: Tech Funding News, Forbes, PYMNTS
