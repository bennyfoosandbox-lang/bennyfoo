1. Workday claims Gartner 'Market Shaper' status in AI agents for HR, with 95% automated frontline hiring

Workday said on 8 October that Gartner named it a Market Shaper in its first Emerging Market Quadrant for AI Agents in Human Resources (established vendors), a report published in September. The more useful part for business buyers is Workday's own performance claims, which Gartner does not endorse: a 95% automation rate for frontline hiring, a 70% cut in time-to-hire, a 15% rise in internal hires, and payroll questions resolved in under a minute. Gartner's own summary is narrower, noting that the market now spans the whole employee lifecycle and telling CHROs to match vendors to their functional scope and architecture. Treat the numbers as vendor-reported, but they show HR is becoming a measurable proving ground for agents.

Sources: Workday Newsroom, Finviz (press release), Gartner abstract

2. Realtor.com puts an approval-gated AI assistant in front of 192,000 real estate agents

Realtor.com launched RealAssist AI inside its Realtor.com+ collaborative search platform on 6 October, after several months of testing with select users. The assistant onboards clients and co-buyers, keeps buyer preferences and notes, builds tailored home searches from MLS data fields, drafts client messages and produces a daily summary of each buyer's chats, searches and price-change activity with suggested next steps. Agents review and approve every action, a human-in-the-loop design that is becoming the default for customer-facing agents. Reports differ on reach, with one trade outlet citing 21 live markets and another a 10% adoption rate, so the early uptake picture is still murky.

Sources: RISMedia, Real Estate News, Inman

3. Microsoft opens Work IQ preview so Copilot can reason over Dynamics 365 and Power Platform data

Microsoft began a public preview of Work IQ for Dynamics 365 and Power Platform data on 30 September, rolling out through October, with Dynamics 365 ERP coverage due in late October. Work IQ builds a semantic model from existing app configuration such as views, forms, relationships and a business glossary, then lets Copilot and agents combine those system-of-record answers with email, Teams meetings and documents. The catch is that answer quality depends on how well an organisation has maintained that configuration. It follows the 23 September general availability of the ERP plugin and MCP server for Copilot Cowork, which gave agents access to ERP records in the first place.

Sources: ERP Today, Cloud Wars

4. Culture Amp: 71% of workers feel more productive with AI, yet workloads barely move

Culture Amp's AI at Work benchmark, drawn from about 112,000 employees across 123 organisations, found 71% say AI helps them feel more productive, rising to 93% among the heaviest users. But 72% of power users call their workload reasonable versus 69% of non-users, a three-point gap, so perceived efficiency is not translating into lighter loads. Power users are more motivated (72% versus 57% of non-users), and there is a 27-point gap between staff who feel encouraged to explore AI and those who say leaders have explained where the company is heading. The report dates from mid-September but was widely covered in early October.

Sources: Culture Amp, IT Brief Australia, IT Brief UK

5. St. Louis Fed: generative AI is being adopted as fast as the PC, but the productivity payoff is uncertain

A 6 October St. Louis Fed post by Charles Gascon, Violeta Gutkowski and Rehann Silvanus, drawing on its quarterly Eighth District business survey, says generative AI is spreading as quickly as personal computers did in the 1980s, with potential but uncertain productivity effects. Earlier Fed research gives context: workers who used generative AI in the prior week saved about 5.4% of their hours, which works out to roughly 1.4% of total hours across the whole workforce once non-users are counted. I could only retrieve the opening of the new post, so its specific survey figures are not reported here.

Sources: San Francisco Fed (St. Louis Fed research), VentureBeat (earlier Fed adoption study)
