1. California's top professors retreat to pen and paper as campus AI deals deepen

Professors at USC, UCLA, Berkeley and Stanford told the New York Post that AI has forced them to rebuild how they assess students. USC's Morgan Polikoff has moved to sit-down paper tests and stopped grading homework for correctness, saying the technology was “shoved down all our throats”, while UCLA's Thomas Maierhofer runs device-free in-person exams and grades take-home work on completion only. The tension is that the institutions are buying in: USC reportedly pays about $3.1 million a year for ChatGPT Edu covering 80,000 users, and Stanford runs a pilot giving every student free access to ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude through August 2027. Stanford law professor Julian Nyarko adds a counterpoint, citing a blind test in which professors preferred Google's AI answers over colleagues' about 75% of the time.

Sources: New York Post / California Post

2. 82% of Korean teachers say they cannot tell AI smart glasses from ordinary glasses ahead of exams

A Korean Teachers and Education Workers Union survey of roughly 2,700 teachers found that 82.3% think the Ministry of Education's current supervision procedures make it difficult to identify smart glasses in the CSAT and mock exams, against 3.5% who think it is possible. The biggest worry, at 64.4%, is disputes if ordinary glasses are mistaken for smart ones, and 48% fear the burden of treating students as suspects. Some 64.8% say the most urgent fix is dedicated personnel and a system to judge suspicious devices. It follows the ministry's nationwide inspection of schools for AI glasses and shows the enforcement burden landing on invigilators.

Sources: SBS World News

3. HolonIQ: AI now features in 12% of global university partnerships, up from 2.1% five years ago

A HolonIQ Q3 2026 education AI report, summarised by 36Kr, says AI and data now account for about 12 of every 100 higher-education cooperation initiatives, compared with roughly 2 five years ago. Tech companies are driving much of it: Anthropic's reported $100 million Claude Frontier Academy aims to train 10,000 deployment engineers by the end of 2027, while Microsoft and AWS are putting billions into AI talent teams. Examples include Western Governors University working with Anthropic on learning systems and competency certification, and INSEAD and the University of Sydney rolling out ChatGPT Edu to all students and staff. The shift suggests universities are becoming channels for vendor talent pipelines, not just buyers of tools.

Sources: 36Kr (HolonIQ report)

4. Maryland school board candidates range from K-5 AI bans to 'AI-free zones' after the state's October deadline

Capital News Service surveyed candidates across Maryland, where the Artificial Intelligence Ready Schools Act required all 24 school systems to adopt AI integration plans by early October. More than 135 candidates are running, and positions vary widely. Cecil County's Brian Hampton backs a ban in grades K-5 and may extend it through eighth grade, Montgomery County's Cassandra Sung proposes grade-level competency gates and AI-free zones, and Wicomico County's David Plotts argues that prohibition is unrealistic and students should learn verification, bias awareness and privacy. The result is that the winners will decide how the state's new plans are actually enforced.

Sources: Maryland Daily Record (Capital News Service)

5. Bahrain's Ministry of Education nears launch of B~SERA, a curriculum-bound AI learning platform

Bahrain's education minister said on 25 October that the B~SERA platform is very close to its final launch, though no public date is confirmed. The first phase offers AI-assisted tools for students to revise lessons, build study plans and take interactive tests with feedback, all limited to approved school curricula rather than open-ended chat. Teachers get lesson-planning tools, and parents get access to grades and behaviour notes through separate accounts. A points-based rewards store for completed assignments is also planned. Keeping the AI inside the national curriculum is a notable design choice compared with open chatbot rollouts elsewhere.

Sources: News of Bahrain
