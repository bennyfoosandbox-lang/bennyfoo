1. British Columbia and a school district sue OpenAI, seeking money for a new school

On September 21, British Columbia's Attorney General and School District No. 59 (Peace River South) filed a civil lawsuit against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in U.S. District Court in San Francisco — the first time a government and a school district, rather than individual families, have sued the company over the Tumbler Ridge Secondary School shooting. The complaint alleges OpenAI's own reviewers flagged 18-year-old Jesse Van Rootselaar's ChatGPT conversations about gun violence as a credible risk months before the attack and recommended referring him to authorities, but the company never warned police. The shooter killed five students, an education assistant, his mother and stepbrother before dying by suicide. B.C. and the district want OpenAI to cover the cost of the emergency response, mental-health support and a replacement school and wellness center, plus a court order changing how the company handles users who threaten violence — sharpening the question of what duty an AI company owes when the user on the other end of the chat is a student.

Sources: Tom's Hardware, Yahoo News, Lawyer Monthly

2. A new Fed study puts a number on how much AI is hurting Texas college grads' paychecks

Dallas Federal Reserve economists Samuel Dodini and Tucker Smith published research on September 22 finding that since ChatGPT's 2022 launch, Texas graduates from majors more exposed to generative AI — computer science and computer engineering among them — are 1.7 percentage points less likely to find a job within a year of graduating than peers from less-exposed majors like nursing, education and psychology, and those who do find work earn about 5% less. The same study finds AI-exposed employers cut job postings 5-6% by mid-2024 and 8-9% by early 2026. It's a more granular, labor-market-side companion to the Census/SSRN "college wage premium" research covered in earlier digests here, using Texas job-posting and employment data rather than national wage surveys, and it lands on exactly what college is supposed to deliver: the technical majors long sold as safe bets are now showing the clearest AI drag on hiring.

Sources: Dallas Fed, Fort Worth Inc., NBC 5 Dallas-Fort Worth

3. 85% of graduating seniors already use AI daily — most say their program never taught them how

Handshake's Class of 2026 Workforce Outlook, drawn from survey data on more than a thousand graduating seniors and covered by CNBC on September 25, finds 85% of this year's seniors report using AI tools — up 31 percentage points in two years, with more than a third using them daily — yet only 28% say they received formal instruction on AI within their program. More than half believe they're already more proficient with AI than their own professors, and 58% say they'll need stronger AI skills to succeed at work than their coursework gave them. The disconnect shows up in mood as much as skills: of the 61% of seniors who describe themselves as pessimistic about their careers, nearly half blame AI at least in part, even as full-time job postings mentioning AI keywords have nearly doubled year over year.

Sources: Handshake, CNBC

4. A UK university turns £300,000 in government funding into 180 AI micro-internships

The University of Leicester's School of Business secured £300,000 from Innovate UK this month to launch the East Midlands Pathways to AI Programme (EMPAIP), a 45-month effort connecting postgraduate students directly with regional employers on AI adoption. The program pairs 180 AI-focused micro-internships with structured six-week student placements and an "AI for Business Accelerator" for local business leaders, framing the university's job as closing a regional AI skills gap through employer partnerships rather than through classroom policy alone. It's a quieter counterpoint to the bans and lawsuits dominating this beat: a university treating AI literacy as a placement-and-partnership problem to solve with industry, not a threat to police in the seminar room.

Sources: University of Leicester, EdTech Innovation Hub
