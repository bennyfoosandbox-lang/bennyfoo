1. UAE education minister: human interaction stays vital as AI spreads

Speaking at the Education First Forum in Abu Dhabi on 6 October, the UAE's Minister of Education, Sarah Al Amiri, argued that rapid AI adoption does not remove the need for human contact in learning and that teachers still have a crucial role. It matters because the UAE has made AI a mandatory subject in public schools and has been training teachers at scale, so a government at the front of AI-in-schools adoption is publicly drawing a line around the teacher's role. The reporting is a single outlet's account of the remarks and gives no new funding or policy figures.

Sources: The National

2. Karnataka pushes government-school AI training down to Class 6

Karnataka Chief Minister D K Shivakumar has told officials to explore AI training in government schools from Class 6, extending a programme that already runs for Class 9 students, with support from the state IT department and private partners. Officials were asked to study Google's school initiatives in Telangana after the company signalled interest, and to identify land for what the state describes as India's first government AI university in Bengaluru. Coverage notes that the curriculum, teacher qualifications, assessment model and start year for Class 6 have not been specified, so this is a direction of travel rather than a finished rollout.

Sources: Deccan Chronicle, Global Education News, InsideAI News

3. Pakistan's HEC trains university administrators in applied AI

Pakistan's Higher Education Commission, through its National Academy of Higher Education, has announced a three-day training programme on Applied Artificial Intelligence for University Administration, running 7 to 9 October. It follows the academy's faculty-focused AI literacy courses, which have reached roughly 400 university teachers across two cohorts, and shifts attention to the people who run campuses rather than those who teach in them. The announcement is reported by one outlet and details of the curriculum and attendance were not available.

Sources: ProPakistani, APP: HEC concludes AI training for two cohorts

4. EDUCAUSE '26: AI is exposing gaps in higher-ed data governance

Coverage from the EDUCAUSE 2026 conference argues that AI is exposing weak data governance on campuses. A joint EDUCAUSE, NACUBO, CUPA-HR and AIR study, as summarised by Kiteworks, found 94% of higher-education workers use AI tools at work but only 54% know whether their institution has a policy covering that use, and 56% use tools their institution did not provide. EDUCAUSE researcher Sean Burns said the findings point to gaps in AI-related data preparation. The statistics come from a vendor summary of the study, so they are worth checking against the original report.

Sources: GovTech: EDUCAUSE '26, Kiteworks, The EDUCAUSE Ledger

5. Morgan State and Google build an AI research campus (from 10 Sep)

This item is from earlier in September but is new to the knowledge base. Morgan State University and Google Public Sector announced on 10 September that the HBCU will get high-performance GPU compute through Google's Program for Accelerated Research, plus a Google-focused Center of Excellence for cloud, AI adoption and workforce training. The work will run on Morgan State's in-house Obsidian AI platform, which acts as the governance and security layer, and the university says the aim is Carnegie R1 research classification. The sources do not disclose the value of the compute or when the Center of Excellence will open.

Sources: Google Cloud press corner, EdTech Innovation Hub, MarketScale
