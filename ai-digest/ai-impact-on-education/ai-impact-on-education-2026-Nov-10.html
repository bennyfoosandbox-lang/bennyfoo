1. Anthropic signs enforceable safety and privacy terms for teachers using Claude, joining Microsoft in the AFT standard

Anthropic has signed a memorandum with the AFT-backed National Academy for AI Instruction that lets school districts write the academy's safety and data-privacy contract language into their Claude for Teachers agreements. According to the union, the terms apply automatically to Anthropic's existing district contracts. Among them: student data may not be sold, teachers keep responsibility for students' educational decisions, and schools and parents must be given clear information about the product. This is a material update: in September, when the AFT, UFT and Microsoft announced their binding standard, the union said talks with OpenAI and Anthropic were still underway. The deal has limits. It covers only Claude for Teachers, which is not offered to anyone under 18; it does not extend to third-party ed-tech built on Claude models; either side can exit on 60 days' notice; and talks with OpenAI continue.

Sources: Education Week, Daily Caller, FourWeekMBA, AFT National Academy for AI Instruction — partnerships

2. Update: Sen. Markey gives Google until Nov. 30 to let schools turn off AI and keep Search, as Chicago blocks student chatbots

Sen. Ed Markey wrote to Sundar Pichai on Nov. 9 asking whether Google will let K-12 schools disable AI Overviews, Gemini and Guided Learning without losing Google Search. He said the policy puts districts in an "impossible position" and asked Google to answer by Nov. 30, including how many districts using Google products already restrict student chatbot access. Google maintains that AI is now too deeply integrated in Search to offer one without the other. Meanwhile, Chicago Public Schools confirmed that generative AI tools, including Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude, stay blocked on student accounts and that it has no plans to add them. In California, EdSource's reporting has been syndicated across local outlets, and districts there are asking how much leverage one vendor should have over their schools. The KB has tracked this Google storyline since early November; what's new today is a firm congressional deadline and the largest district so far publicly holding its line.

Sources: Quartz, University Herald, Boston.com, CBS Chicago, Palo Alto Online

3. JED Foundation: students who turn to AI when struggling report worse mental health

A JED Foundation report, now getting national coverage, surveyed more than 5,500 middle and high school students at 21 U.S. schools between October 2025 and April 2026. More than half said they use AI when they feel sad, stressed, lonely or overwhelmed. Overall AI use was not associated with worse outcomes. But students who use AI specifically while struggling emotionally reported poorer mental health and well-being and more history of suicidal thoughts and attempts. JED's research lead says the link is an association, not proof of cause, since already-struggling students may simply reach for AI more. For schools, the finding sharpens the line between AI as a study tool and AI as a companion. That matters as districts weigh chatbot rollouts and as a separate lawsuit ties a 13-year-old's crisis to Gemini on a school Chromebook.

Sources: Education Week, JED Foundation report, JED Foundation release, KATV / TNND

4. Bangladesh will put AI into the curriculum for Classes VI–XII from 2027

Education Minister ANM Ehsanul Haque Milon told parliament that AI content will be added to the curriculum for Classes VI to XII from the 2027 academic year. Class VI will cover AI basics, AI-powered tools, VR/AR and robotics. Classes IX–X move on to ethical and unethical uses of AI, machine learning and deep learning, and Classes XI–XII focus on applying AI in daily life, study and work. Milon said pupils must learn to check and evaluate AI-generated output, not just operate the tools. Reports describe this as an announced plan rather than an approved final curriculum, with no published timeline for textbooks or teacher training. It comes a week after the KB logged that Bangladesh's universities still have no national generative-AI guideline.

Sources: Dhaka Tribune, Bangladesh Pratidin (BSS)

5. Update: South Portland, Maine rejects its pre-K–8 AI moratorium 5–1 and promises a fuller plan in January

Yesterday's digest covered South Portland's vote on a student AI moratorium. The Board of Education has now voted 5–1 against the resolution, which would have barred generative AI in grades pre-K through 8 for the rest of the school year while a policy was written. Members who voted no argued that AI is already everywhere and students should learn to use it as a tool rather than be walled off from it. Public comment was mixed. The district still has no formal AI guidelines; officials say a broader plan covering AI integration and district cybersecurity will launch in January. The result is a counterpoint to the wave of pauses in NYC, Los Angeles, Portland (Ore.) and several Maryland districts: a small district looked at the moratorium model and chose to regulate rather than freeze.

Sources: WGME, Yahoo News, Portland Press Herald
