1. Harvard's science division launches five AI-themed faculty searches while a hiring freeze holds

Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences science departments are opening at least five AI-related faculty searches spanning eight-plus departments, after Dean of Science David Johnston pitched themed searches as the only way to win new positions during the university's hiring freeze (he secured 5.5 search authorizations). Posted searches include two in statistics and AI and one in life sciences and AI, with a quantum-science-and-AI search also planned, and Johnston's March email required "tangible ties" to undergraduate teaching. The top-down approach is contested: astronomer Avi Loeb urged waiting a few years before committing long-term hires to a fast-moving technology, and biologist Daniel Lieberman said faculty, not deans, should pick search fields. It is a concrete sign of AI reshaping who universities hire, not just how students learn.

Sources: The Harvard Crimson

2. Instructure data: no dedicated AI tool ranks in the top 100 of U.S. higher-ed learning tech

Instructure's first higher-education EdTech Top 40, built from Canvas LTI launch data on 19.5 million U.S. users (Sept 2025–April 2026), finds that despite the AI hype no dedicated AI tool breaks the top 100. Google Gemini was the most widely adopted AI tool but reached only about 88,000 users across 211 institutions. Institutions averaged 28 LTI integrations, and content, collaboration and video dominate the stack; separate Instructure research says only 11% of higher-ed instructors have had comprehensive AI training. The caveat is that LTI data misses consumer AI tools students use outside the LMS, so the gap is between formal institutional adoption and informal use, and the report comes from a vendor with its own stake in the LMS.

Sources: Instructure via PR Newswire

3. Update: Gemini study help reaches all ages in Classroom, with a Sierra Leone trial showing a 0.26 SD math gain

Google's August expansion of Gemini study help in Classroom to students of all ages, previously covered, now has new detail: teachers can assign source-locked Gemini Notebooks that students can read but not edit, and see whether students opened them. Google and Fab AI also reported a randomized trial in 12 Sierra Leone schools (1,423 students) where teacher-led, Guided-Learning-only use produced a baseline-adjusted math gain of 0.258 standard deviations, with a wide 95% confidence interval of 0.027 to 0.488 and larger gains for stronger starters. Education Week reporting cited in the piece shows some schools were caught flat-footed by the default-on rollout, and Common Sense Media found the school Gemini did not tailor responses to students' ages. The trial covered students 13 and older, so effects on younger children remain unproven.

Sources: WinBuzzer, Education Week

4. Punjab, Pakistan pairs a 160,000-teacher AI training drive with AI education for special-needs children

Pakistan's Punjab province launched an AI Curriculum and Training Initiative to train 160,000 teachers, backed by the School Education Department, UNICEF and the Office of AI, ahead of plans to teach AI as a subject from Class 1 to Class 12 with new labs. On 29 September the province added AI education for special-needs children, with specialized training for special-education teachers, according to Special Education Secretary Shoaib Iqbal. The broader curriculum overhaul also cuts textbook volume by 25% and adds QR-linked video lessons. Teacher training at this scale is the real test: the plan only works if the 160,000 educators are ready before the classroom rollout reaches them.

Sources: ProPakistani – special children, ProPakistani – 160,000 teachers

5. Dubai's KHDA to publish a standardised AI policy for private schools in 2027

Dubai's Knowledge and Human Development Authority is drafting a standardised policy on the ethical use of AI in private schools, due in 2027, KHDA Director General Aisha Miran told ARN News. The authority will work with external partners, stakeholders and experts to set guiding principles for introducing AI in classrooms, and Miran framed the goal as raising children who understand and adapt to new technologies. It follows the UAE Cabinet's approval of an AI curriculum for public and private schools, so the policy is meant to supply the guardrails the curriculum mandate lacks. Details are thin and the 2027 timeline means schools will run AI programs for a year without a unified rulebook.

Sources: Dubai Eye 103.8
