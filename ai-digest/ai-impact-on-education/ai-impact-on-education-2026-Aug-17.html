1. Ohio's first-in-the-nation AI policy mandate is now live as schools reopen

As of July 1, 2026, Ohio became the first state to legally require every K-12 traditional public school district, community school, and STEM school to adopt a formal AI-use policy — and that mandate is being tested for real as students return to class in mid-August. Districts can adopt the Ohio Department of Education and Workforce's model policy, released in January, or write their own as long as it aligns with the state framework covering acceptable use, academic integrity, AI literacy, and limits on entering student personally identifiable information into third-party tools. Local coverage from the Columbus and Dayton areas shows compliance is uneven in practice: some districts, like Fairborn City Schools, say they cleared the deadline easily because informal AI guidance was already in place, while others are still finalizing language as the school year starts. Because Ohio converted what had been optional state guidance into a statutory duty, education-policy watchers are treating it as a bellwether other states may follow.

Sources: Spectrum News 1, Tech & Learning, Dayton 24/7 Now

2. California's AI-bill purge sends a "no AI professors" law to Newsom's desk

Update to our August 11 coverage: on August 13, California's Assembly and Senate appropriations committees held the annual suspense-file hearings on roughly 30 pending AI bills, killing five and clearing the rest for floor votes. Two education-relevant bills made it through the full legislature and now sit on Governor Newsom's desk — SB 928, from Sen. Sabrina Cervantes and sponsored by the California Faculty Association, which would require the instructor of record for every California State University course to be a human being (AI tools may assist, but can't hold the role), and AB 1651, addressing AI's use in the California State Bar exam. SB 928 passed the full Assembly unanimously on August 13, making it one of the first state laws anywhere to explicitly bar AI from serving as the accountable instructor in higher education. Newsom's signature or veto is expected well before CSU's 23 campuses open for fall semester.

Sources: Tech Times, Office of Sen. Sabrina Cervantes, Transparency Coalition

3. The UAE makes AI a required subject in every government school, kindergarten through grade 12

As the UAE's new school year opens, the Ministry of Education has made AI instruction mandatory across all public and MoE-curriculum private schools, spanning seven core subject areas from kindergarten through grade 12 — one of the most sweeping K-12 AI mandates announced anywhere this year. The rollout builds on the UAE's National AI Strategy 2031 and follows survey data showing 75% of UAE teachers already use AI tools daily in their teaching, a rate that ties Singapore for the highest among countries surveyed in the OECD's TALIS study. Unlike opt-in pilots elsewhere, this is a system-wide curriculum requirement rather than a supplemental program, putting the UAE alongside China and India among the first countries to convert AI literacy from elective to compulsory instruction. The mandate arrives alongside unified national school holidays and updated exam formats for the new academic year.

Sources: Gulf News, Third Rock Techkno

4. India starts training 10 million teachers for its mandatory AI curriculum, a year ahead of the first bell

India's plan to make an AI and Computational Thinking curriculum mandatory for grades 3-8 starting in the 2026-27 academic year (grades 9-10 follow in 2027-28) now faces its hardest logistical test: training the more than 10 million teachers who will have to deliver it. The government's primary vehicle is NISHTHA (National Initiative for School Heads and Teachers Holistic Advancement), which will roll out grade-specific modules and video-based learning resources rather than a single national training event. The curriculum itself is framed around "AI for Public Good," emphasizing ethical use and critical thinking over technical coding skills, making India one of only a handful of countries mandating AI instruction from primary-school age. Education researchers covering the rollout say teacher readiness, not content or infrastructure, is the single biggest risk to the timeline holding.

Sources: The Secretariat, AI Ready School, Convergence Now

5. Florida's newest high school opens its doors with a state-funded AI career track built in from day one

South Marion High School welcomed its first class on August 10 — Marion County, Florida's first new high school in 21 years — with a $260,000 state Workforce CAP grant funding an artificial-intelligence career-and-technical-education track alongside programs in avionics, digital cinema production, entrepreneurship, and agribusiness. It's the district's 36th CTE curriculum category, housed in a $180 million, 115-acre campus built for 2,700 students that's meant to relieve overcrowding in southwest Marion County. The AI track is a concrete example of a broader pattern: rather than treating AI purely as a classroom tool or a policy problem, some districts are now building it directly into vocational pathways aimed at giving students AI-adjacent job skills before graduation. The opening wasn't without hiccups — school officials are simultaneously managing a costly construction issue discovered beneath the campus's new athletic facilities.

Sources: Ocala Gazette, WCJB, WinsSolutions, Ocala-News.com
