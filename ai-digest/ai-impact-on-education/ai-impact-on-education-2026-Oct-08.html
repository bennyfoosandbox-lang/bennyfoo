1. Frederick County, Maryland bans student AI use from pre-K through 12th grade

Update on a story we have covered as a policy-in-progress: the Frederick County school board voted 6–1 on Wednesday to bar students from using AI at every grade level, with exceptions for AI literacy and IEPs. Members had been weighing a ban for younger grades only, but teachers and the student board member pushed to extend it through high school. The lone dissenter argued that blocking older students would leave them less prepared for college. The vote lands ahead of an Oct. 22 deadline tied to Maryland's 2026 state AI-in-schools requirements, making it one of the strictest student AI restrictions in the Washington region.

Sources: The Baltimore Sun, Fox 5 DC

2. Alpha School eyes an Evanston campus as local critics question its 2.6x claim

The Evanston RoundTable reports that Alpha School, the for-profit AI-driven private school network, is considering the former Roycemore School building for a fall 2027 opening, and that its arrival has prompted pushback from education researchers, teachers and policy analysts. The school says its students grow 2.6 times faster than peers on nationally normed MAP tests, but those figures are internal analyses that have not been independently verified, and writer Kelsey Piper has published a report casting doubt on them. The piece also notes the network's new Chicago campus, which opened on 8 September. As Alpha spreads into new suburbs, local debate over evidence and tuition is following it. This item rests on a single outlet.

Sources: Evanston RoundTable

3. South Africa's Cap.π (Cappi) launches an AI study platform days before Matric exams

Hypertext reports that Cap.π, pronounced Cappi, has launched as an AI education platform aligned to South Africa's national CAPS curriculum, with a separate mode for the Independent Examinations Board syllabus. It is built to serve students, and also teachers and parents or guardians through three portals. Co-founder Tim Holliday of Maze Education is quoted on its aims, and Acer South Africa is bundling Cappi student vouchers with a batch of 4,000 PCs sold through Walmart-owned stores. The launch landed just days before the 2026 Matric exams began, a clear bid for exam-season demand. This rests on one outlet and appears to be drawn from a press release, so the claims are the company's own.

Sources: Hypertext

4. Lagos secondary schools get a two-track AI course for teachers and students

Hydrogen Payment Services has wrapped up the first AI4Learning Initiative with the Lagos State Ministry of Basic and Secondary Education, an event held on 30 September in Victoria Island for state-run secondary schools. The programme ran separate learning tracks for teachers and for students, with the stated aim of encouraging responsible use of AI in learning. It adds to a run of Nigerian efforts to train educators on AI, a theme this digest has tracked from lecturers to state ministries, and shows private fintech firms filling a skills-training gap. This item rests on a single outlet and gives few outcome figures.

Sources: Nairametrics

5. Globe pushes Khan Academy's AI learning tools to more Filipino teachers and families

Globe Telecom is promoting its Khan Academy Philippines partnership during National Teachers' Month, framing it as an easier route to digital learning for families. Khan Academy Philippines is the nonprofit's first international licensee, with content localised to the Department of Education curriculum, and teachers can assign lessons and mastery goals and spot learning gaps. Khanmigo, the AI assistant, is free for teachers and can help with lesson planning, rubrics and exit tickets. The partnership dates to August 2025, so this is a relaunch of existing offerings rather than a new product, and it arrives as Khanmigo's real-world uptake is under scrutiny elsewhere.

Sources: Manila Times (Globe release), Globe newsroom
