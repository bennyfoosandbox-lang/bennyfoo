1. OpenAI turns ChatGPT Edu and ChatGPT for Teachers into agentic classroom assistants

On August 4, OpenAI extended the "workspace agent" capabilities it built for ChatGPT Work and Codex into its education products, adding three new plugins to ChatGPT Edu (for colleges) and the free ChatGPT for Teachers plan. A K-12 Educator plugin lets teachers feed in materials they already have to generate differentiated lesson resources, interactive visuals, and "actionable insights," while a Learning Commons integration lets ChatGPT reference local academic standards so teachers keep control over grading and pedagogical calls. OpenAI paired the launch with a "ChatGPT for Academic Researchers" program giving eligible researchers 12 months of free Pro-level access for scientific work in a secured workspace. Coverage was split: OpenAI's own announcement framed it as freeing up educator time, while The Register's writeup was pointedly skeptical, asking why teachers and professors would want to hand more of their own workload to the same technology many are still trying to police in student submissions.

Sources: OpenAI, The Register, EdTech Innovation Hub

2. Google throws open Gemini in Classroom to K-12 students of every age, starting August 10

Google Workspace Updates confirmed that Gemini in Classroom — previously gated behind individual admin approval and only opened to higher-education students as recently as November 2025 — will expand to K-12 and higher-ed students "of all ages" starting August 10 on the web and August 17 on mobile, wherever a school's admin has enabled the underlying AI services. A new "Learn with Gemini" prompt surfaces on assignments listed under "Due Soon," letting students pull a guided study session, practice quiz, or flashcard set straight from their own class materials without leaving Google Classroom. The rollout follows Google's broader Gemini for Education push introduced at ISTE and BETT 2026, and lands the same week OpenAI and Anthropic are each widening their own free K-12 and higher-ed offerings — sharpening a three-way race among the major AI labs for default status in U.S. classrooms as the 2026-27 school year opens.

Sources: Techlusive, Google Classroom Help
