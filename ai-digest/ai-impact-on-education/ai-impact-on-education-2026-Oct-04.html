1. University of Alaska moves toward a system-wide AI policy as AI dominates misconduct cases

The University of Alaska Board of Regents will weigh system-wide AI guiding principles in November, after an August report from the UAA Dean of Students Office found AI now accounts for most conduct cases: 119 of 150 students cited for academic misconduct used AI, with AI-linked plagiarism up 73% and misconduct overall up 56%. Until now each of the system's campuses has left AI rules to individual faculty syllabi, a structure the UAA Faculty Senate specifically requested to protect academic freedom. The draft stresses humans staying "in the loop" and "appropriate AI in every discipline," but students at the preliminary meeting said it is too soft and lacks privacy detail, and one proposed a mandatory AI-literacy prerequisite. The story shows how a policy vacuum on a large campus turns into a conduct-office caseload.

Sources: Anchorage Daily News

2. IIM Bangalore has MBA students build chatbots that answer their own exam

Rather than ban AI from exams, Prof Subhabrata Majumdar at IIM Bangalore had 78 MBA students take a GenAI midterm by building and prompting their own chatbots, which then answered the exam questions. He built a password-protected no-code RAG platform on Vercel in two weeks using AI-assisted coding, restricted to course materials with no internet access. Grading rewarded prompt design, parameter tuning and how well students operationalised the course content; top bots used specific, detailed instructions and cleaner plain-text inputs. Course evaluations came in at 4.5 to 5 out of 5, and Majumdar plans to open-source the platform and extend the format to other IIMB courses.

Sources: The Financial Express

3. St. Paul schools block AI for K-8 while the board plans a governance policy by 2028

St. Paul Public Schools bars students in pre-K through eighth grade from using AI and blocks Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude on district devices, while grades 9-12 may use Gemini only under teacher direction through school accounts. The board has set a goal of adopting a formal AI Governance Policy by the end of the 2027-28 school year, so day-to-day practice is currently led by teachers rather than rules. At one magnet school, teachers ask for multiple drafts and one teacher runs a live exercise showing ChatGPT failing an open-ended problem the class had already solved. The article notes 37 states now have official AI guidance, Minnesota among them.

Sources: Pioneer Press (TwinCities.com)

4. Nigerian lecturers use AI to cut course prep from days to minutes, and ask for national rules

Distinction AI, an edtech platform from FlexiSAF's Distinction Labs, helps Nigerian educators generate lecture slides, videos, assignments and exams; one Abuja lecturer says slides that took three days now take minutes, and an AI assistant marks coursework. The company reports 10,000+ instructors, 20,000 courses and 160,000+ registered learners, and says it added a human QA review after AI-generated practice questions were flagged as inaccurate. A Nile University researcher called for NUC and Ministry of Education policy on AI use and accountability, especially around student identities and grades. The piece also cites a 2025 World Bank study in Edo State where AI tutoring with instructor support produced gains equal to 1.5 to two years of schooling. Company figures are self-reported.

Sources: The Guardian Nigeria

5. Australia's Education Department warns a parliamentary inquiry that AI could dull pupils and expose university research

Australia's federal Education Department told a parliamentary inquiry into artificial intelligence that chatbots risk harming children's critical thinking and could expose sensitive university research to theft, according to The Australian. The full article is paywalled, so this summary rests on the published headline and standfirst only; details such as the evidence behind the warning were not verifiable. It is nonetheless a notable signal that a national education department is framing AI as a risk to both school-age learning and the research base.

Sources: The Australian (paywalled)
