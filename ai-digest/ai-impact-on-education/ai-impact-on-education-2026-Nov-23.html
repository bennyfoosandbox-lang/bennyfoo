1. India’s Chief Justice: no student should be punished on an AI detector’s word alone

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant used the opening address of the Commonwealth Legal Education Association conference in Hyderabad on 21 November to warn law schools against treating AI-detection software as proof of cheating. He said no student should be penalised on the basis of a detection tool alone. He pointed to evidence that detectors which work well on essays by US schoolchildren often misidentify writing by non-native English speakers, which is a large share of students in India and across the Commonwealth. He also rejected a blanket ban on AI in legal education as “neither practicable nor wise”, since graduates will join a profession that already uses these tools. Instead he urged schools to rethink traditional exams, set clear AI rules before assessments begin, and asked CLEA to draft model guidelines on disclosing AI help in legal research. Coming from the head of India’s judiciary, the remarks give students accused on detector scores alone a strong reference point and add pressure on universities to put due process into their AI-misconduct rules.

Sources: LiveLaw, LawBeat, The Federal, The Logical Indian, Indian Masterminds

2. California’s public colleges start building their own AI data centers and chatbots instead of relying only on Big Tech

A CalMatters investigation published on 23 November finds California’s three public higher-education systems quietly building in-house AI alongside their headline deals with commercial vendors. The California Community Colleges system is asking the state for $21 million next year for a more independent AI network, which its proposal says would cost “a fraction” of commercial services. The chancellor’s office wants to link the AI hardware individual campuses are buying into one shared computing pool. About 20 community-college districts, plus some UC and Cal State campuses, already draw on that shared network. UC San Diego’s TritonGPT, built with its supercomputing center, is the most advanced campus-run chatbot, and UCSD has shared parts of it with Berkeley (BearGPT) and Merced (SCOUT). Officials cite cost, data security and the ability to track the power and water use of open-weight models they run themselves. The push comes as Cal State’s roughly $56 million OpenAI contract runs to 2029 and UC has just signed its own systemwide ChatGPT deal. For universities, it shows a hedge forming against dependence on a single vendor.

Sources: CalMatters, KPBS, LAist, Palo Alto Online, Lookout Santa Cruz

3. Karnataka plans AI face-recognition attendance for government college students, with alerts sent to parents

Karnataka’s Higher Education department has proposed an AI-based facial-recognition attendance system for undergraduate and postgraduate students at government colleges, according to a Deccan Herald report from this weekend. Teachers would record attendance through an AI mobile app, and the result would be pushed straight to each parent’s registered phone number. That extends parental monitoring into higher education, where students are mostly adults. The system has been piloted at a few colleges, and NewsBytes reports that officials, including Commissioner H N Gopalakrishna, want it statewide from next semester. It builds on the state’s “Nirantara” face-recognition attendance in government pre-university colleges. A parallel plan for schools has drawn privacy objections, and the state’s school department has said there is no move to roll it out across all government schools. The proposal is an example of AI in education being used for surveillance rather than teaching, and it raises the same questions about consent and storing biometric data now facing campuses elsewhere.

Sources: Deccan Herald, NewsBytes, The Literature Today (Nirantara in PU colleges), Careers360 (school plan contested)

4. Google commits $5 million to IIT Kharagpur for AI research, teaching and campus AI infrastructure

Google announced a $5 million (about ₹46 crore) commitment to IIT Kharagpur on 21 November, during the institute’s 75th-anniversary celebrations. The money is split three ways: applied AI research, AI education and training, and campus-wide AI infrastructure and tooling. The research focus includes Indic-language AI, materials science, agriculture and educational technology. The education part pays for new AI training and curriculum for students, faculty and school teachers. The institute’s director said the support was envisaged by alumnus and Google CEO Sundar Pichai, and another alumnus pledged a separate $5 million seed endowment for a data initiative. The grant follows a pattern in which big AI companies fund elite universities to shape AI curricula and talent pipelines, here in India, one of the largest higher-education markets. It also arrives as Google faces criticism from US school districts over making AI features a condition of using its K-12 Search.

Sources: Business Standard, Business Standard (follow-up), BusinessToday, Entrepreneur India

5. UIC faculty vote on strike authorization with control over AI as a central sticking point

Faculty at the University of Illinois Chicago held a strike-authorization vote between 18 and 20 November, and who controls AI in teaching is one of the issues splitting the two sides. UIC United Faculty says management’s proposals would let administrators make “top-down and sometimes secret” decisions about working conditions, including the use of AI and educational technology. The university’s bargaining update counters that it will not let a single bargaining unit decide how all other UIC colleagues use AI. Fourteen of 23 issues are settled, with mostly economic articles left, and a bargaining session was set for 22 November. If members authorize a strike, the union must give 10 days’ notice to the Illinois Educational Labor Relations Board before walking out. No result had been published when this digest was compiled. UIC joins Cal State, the University of Illinois Springfield and Rutgers, where AI protections are a core bargaining demand, a sign that faculty contracts are becoming a main place where campus AI rules get decided. (From earlier this week.)

Sources: UIC United Faculty, UIC Today (university update), UIC Faculty Bargaining, Capitol City Now (UIS AI concerns)
