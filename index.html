The software lifecycle, rebuilt around AI.

Not a tool rollout. An operating change. AI works in every stage and every role, from the first requirement to the release.

  1. Requirements

    Product owners

    Backlogs and user stories shaped with AI, so the intent is clear before anyone builds.

  2. Specification

    Functional leads

    Functional specs drafted with AI and reviewed by people, in days rather than weeks.

  3. Build

    Engineers

    Design, code and review, AI-assisted from the first line to the pull request.

  4. Test

    Testers

    Test design and automation driven by AI, so coverage grows with every change.

  5. Release

    The whole team

    Working software in users' hands sooner, with fewer defects making it that far.

How I lead.

I work for the team. The team ships. Everything below follows from that.

Servant leadership, taken literally.

The team does not work for me. I work for them. My calendar is spent on the things that let engineers, product owners and testers do their best work without asking twice.

Lead from underneath.

I clear the path.

Blockers, approvals, unclear requirements, cross-team politics. I take them off the team's plate so delivery keeps moving.

Obstacles are my job, not theirs.

People first. Delivery follows.

Teams that feel safe and trusted ship better software. I invest in the person before the plan, and the plan gets better for it.

Trust is the fastest process.

Best people in the best place.

I match people to the problems where they are strongest, give them the mandate, and get out of the way.

Then step back.

Let's make it real.

I compare notes with people working on AI transformation: what is working, what is not, and experiments worth teaming up on. If you are building something interesting, I would like to hear about it.
